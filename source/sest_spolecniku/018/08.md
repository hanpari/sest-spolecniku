_As always, it came down to money._

By then, Svetlana was already in the Game, fleeing a gathering of the temple council. As an ordained venerable sister, she had every right to attend, but she happily hid behind the excuse of her youth and, to Brother Ambrose’s immense relief, slipped away at the first opportunity, trailed by the envious glare of her supreme superior, the diminutive High Priest.

From the little she had heard, it was enough to understand that the Temple of Sweet Lady Lada was a wealthy ship barely staying afloat. In a fit of grandiosity, back when the temple stood at the height of its power, someone had decided it would be a splendid idea to purchase and maintain a floating island above the city.

_A textbook case of living beyond your means,_ she thought as she watched the exhausted accountant weeping over a catastrophic balance sheet. The floating island devoured most of the temple's income.

_No wonder everyone is waiting for our downfall. Quite literally,_ she reflected as she sat inside a steampunk carriage descending toward the ground along with a retinue of loyal followers whose expressions ranged from sour to outright miserable.

“Why did you have to volunteer, Venerable Sister?” grumbled Brother Jerome. “Why?”

“Serving the poor honors the name of our Sweet Lady,” Mouse reminded him, ever loyal, though clearly no more enthusiastic about the mission than anyone else.

“That too,” said Svetlana. “But mostly I wanted to escape that dreadful bore-fest. Agatha was just about to propose yet another prayer break. If you ask me, that goose is addicted to the mind-numbing light.”

“Venerable Sister!”

Mouse discreetly nodded toward the temple guards escorting them. Most were large fellows skilled in swinging clubs and occasionally doubling as orderlies.

“You shouldn’t speak like that in front of them,” she whispered. “They might take your words the wrong way.”

“They’re no less addicted than she is,” Svetlana replied indifferently. “Frequent bliss-fits make up for their miserable wages. We need to cut expenses everywhere. And relax, Mousie.” She pointed at her faintly glowing medical staff. “I’ve isolated us from those simpletons. They can’t hear a word.”

“Thank goodness.”

Neither Jerome nor Mouse were fanatics like Sister Agatha, but even they sometimes disapproved of their irreverent superior.

_In their own way, they’re remarkably adaptable,_ she thought. _Unlike me, a flower doomed to wilt in every season._

“When I volunteered,” she continued, “to serve the _poor and needy_ down in the undercity, the people who can’t afford to fly all the way up to the temple, I didn’t expect praise. But still...” She gave them a stern look. “The lack of enthusiasm is almost criminal. Not even that nitwit Agatha volunteered. And you’d think that if she practiced her art on the _poor and unnecessary,_ she’d cause significantly less damage than usual.”

“If only you’d asked first,” Brother Jerome remarked bitterly. “We could have warned you, Venerable Sister.”

“I was bored,” Svetlana snapped. “This looked like the perfect opportunity to vanish into thin air.”

“Let’s hope we don’t vanish permanently,” Jerome said darkly, “like Brother Crispin.”

“Brother Crispin? Who’s that?”

“More like who he _was._ He was the venerable brother whose apartment you inherited.”

“And us two, along with an entire hospital ward,” added Mouse.

_So the mysterious owner of my psychoactive poetry collection finally has a name?_

“Is he dead?” she asked.

“No one knows,” Mouse answered hesitantly.

“Wait!”

Suddenly Daisy pushed her way forward.

“I thought Venerable Brother Crispin was dead,” she said irritably. “I distinctly remember Brother Ambrose holding a memorial service for him.”

_What's gotten into you? Afraid you'll lose our cozy little apartment?_ Svetlana wondered. Daisy generally preferred listening to acting.

“His Holiness gave me the keys and everything he left behind,” Daisy complained.

_So you're planning to claim Brother Crispin's poetry collection for yourself? Poor thing. Afraid you'll lose your psychoactive treasure if the dear departed rises from the grave?_

Daisy genuinely loved reading. Among all her other vices she was an incurable bookworm, while Svetlana, at the mature age of twenty, considered all literature equally pointless.

“More than a year ago,” Jerome said in an especially grim tone, “Venerable Brother Crispin disappeared. He was abducted from our mission. The very mission we're flying to now,” he emphasized. “Since then, no one sufficiently... ah... brave has volunteered to return.”

“Is it haunted?”

“No one knows.”

“Is it abandoned?”

“Certainly not,” Mouse objected. “The temple still runs a small shop there selling medical supplies and medicines.”

“Are you sure?”

Beyond the round bulging window, which resembled a submarine porthole, the ground rushed closer.

In **Twilight City**, all land-level and underground districts were reserved for the poorest of the poor, those who could not afford the monthly fees required to live on the floating islands.

_Even the beggars' quarters are picturesque here,_ Svetlana thought.

The houses looked perpetually moments away from collapse. Built from stone or timber, they leaned at every conceivable angle. One had the impression the entire district had been constructed on a serving tray that someone later shook violently. Among them rose a proud slender tower crowned by a familiar statue of Lady Lada, an unmistakable symbol of wealth.

“So even down here doctors like to think they're something special?” Svetlana remarked disdainfully. “Let’s just hope the rabble doesn’t hang us from lampposts.”

“No one would dare,” Mouse protested.

“Care to wager on that?” asked Svetlana.

From what she saw, the locals were hardly thrilled by the arrival of the carriage. Several street urchins had already stooped to the ground to gather what were almost certainly stones.

Yet despite her gloomy expectations, the moment she stepped out and the crowd recognized the unmistakable attire of a venerable sister, no stones flew. Instead, there were several cries of delight and a few cautious smiles.

_Your stage,_ she thought, stepping aside for Daisy. _I’ve never cared much for basking in worship._

“Glory to Sweet Lady Lada!” Daisy shouted, flooding the entire street with white radiance.

She then strode safely through an alley full of vacant faces and pounded on the massive gates of the mission.

_Just like at home, where the rich feel safest hiding inside concrete bunkers underground,_ Svetlana smirked.

“Who dares?” came a voice from within after a lengthy delay.

Growing impatient, Daisy hammered ever harder.

“Who dares disturb the rest of the temple's servants?”

“What rest?” Daisy frowned and kicked the gate. “Service to our Sweet Lady Lada knows no rest.”

_Speak for yourself!_ thought Svetlana comfortably ensconced within the recesses of their mind. _Speak for yourself, you conscientious goose._

“To hell with you, woman,” answered a coarse unpleasant voice from beyond the gate. “I've told you a hundred times. We don't waste medicine on paupers.”

_He'd make an excellent physician,_ thought Svetlana. _Greed is the first prerequisite for success in medicine._

“Woman?” Daisy frowned. “I am the First Daughter of Baron Mosswell Hill, peasant.”

“And a venerable sister of the Temple of Sweet Lady Lada,” Mouse added quickly.

“And I’m His Holiness the High Priest,” the rough voice laughed, joined by several others. “You’ve tried plenty of tricks, woman, but this one’s especially pathetic. If I were some puffed-up fool from the temple above, I’d have you whipped already. Lucky for you, I’m a forgiving sort. But show your face here one more time and I'll have you shot like a stray dog. Now crawl back into whatever hole you came from. We don't serve your kind here. Come on, boys, the cards won't play themselves.”

“That’s unfortunate,” Jerome said, pointing upward toward the floating island with obvious relief. “They warned us the local staff could be somewhat eccentric. But now they've crossed every line imaginable. We can't even get inside. We'll have to go back.”

“Go back? Have you lost your mind? Punishment must follow wrongdoing immediately.”

White with fury, Daisy had absolutely no intention of retreating.

“Hold my staff,” she said curtly.

“What are you doing, Venerable Sister?” Mouse asked nervously as she watched her superior retreat ten paces and lower her head like a stubborn ram preparing to charge.

_Yes, what exactly are you doing?_ Svetlana joined in. _I hate to remind you, but there’s another tenant living in that skull. Don't crack it open._

Ignoring every warning, Daisy hiked her robes above her knees, ignited her Inner Flame, and sprinted toward the wall.

Just before reaching the gate, she leapt like a deer, soaring impossibly high until she reached the very top of the wall. Catching the edge, she swung herself onto the red-tiled roof and, without a second's hesitation or the slightest concern for her horrified passenger, threw herself down into the courtyard below.

_You've gone completely insane!_ shrieked Svetlana, who had never appreciated stunts of that nature.

With her Inner Flame blazing, Daisy landed on her feet with a heavy thud.

It all happened quickly, but even Svetlana, no expert on falling from great heights, noticed that Daisy had somehow slowed their descent.

_Ah yes, another Superman trick,_ she thought sourly. _Use it too often and I'll have a nervous breakdown._

They landed directly behind a group of men.

It was a bizarre collection of rabble. Only one wore robes vaguely resembling those of a temple novice. The rest dressed like villains from a low-budget adventure film. Their appearance matched. Dried dirt clung to their boots, trousers, and hands. Their hair was tangled and gray with grime.

“You wicked dogs,” declared Daisy. “I'll drown you in your own filth.”

And so she did.

Before any of them could react, she seized one by the hair, yanked so hard his head nearly came off, and dragged him toward an old wooden trough used to feed pigs. She plunged his face into the foul stagnant water and did not stop until he ceased struggling.

“That bitch!” screamed his stunned companions, reaching for the knives tucked into their belts.

“Wait! That's a venerable sister!”

The shout came from the man wearing the faded novice's robe.

“I don't care if she's Lady Lada herself. I'll gut her like a fish,” shouted another, charging at Daisy.

_No thank you,_ thought Svetlana as she stared at the jagged blade. _That's how my dear father died. Perhaps I've been unfair to him after all._

A memory flashed through her mind: the genuine terror on Eugene Troyak's face when he had tried to warn them. His own brief career in the Game had also ended beneath a knife.

_If Daisy dies, do I die with her?_ she wondered. _What a unique opportunity to experience clinical death. Split open from belly to throat._
