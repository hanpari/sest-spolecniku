# May I treat you, sir

Sweaty and aching, Xavier got dressed with Medard’s kind assistance. His arms and legs were covered in bruises that were already starting to darken.

“Thanks, buddy,” he groaned. “I’m lucky that woman didn’t break my bones. I owe you some money, I think.”

He created a virtual banknote and handed it to Medard, who stared at it in shock.

“One hundred thousand dollars! Are you insane?”

Even though Medard had lost his bet in the fake draw, he hadn’t expected such generous compensation.

“You definitely deserve it,” Xavier noted. “If it weren’t for you, those three thugs would’ve waited for me.”

“So what?” Medard gave a bitter smile. “A guy who can take down a silver‑ranked bruiser in fifteen minutes would make short work of them. You made me look like an idiot.”

“Maybe. But I could’ve ended up with a knife in my back. Besides, if I hadn’t met you, I wouldn’t have had the chance to earn extra cash.”

No matter how he looked at it, having almost half a million dollars was worth a moment of discomfort.

“Which reminds me — how did you do?”

Instead of answering, Medard raised a thumb. “Officially a Player. My score surprised everyone. I don’t want to brag, but according to the doctor I have excellent affi‑something.”

“Affinity,” Xavier prompted absent‑mindedly. If only one could trust Blackheart! For all he knew, the shoulder‑patting might have been just a psychological trick to motivate candidates to push themselves to the limit in the Game.

“In any case,” Xavier continued, “what’s your plan now?”

“Plan? Go home and sign up for the Game tonight. What else?”

“If I were you, I’d stop by the gym and get checked. You might be in for a surprise.”

“At my age? I stopped testing myself long ago. Every result was worse and worse.”

Society offered its citizens measurements of physical and mental fitness. However, most bronzes and plastics avoided them like the devil avoids the cross. As they aged, their results followed a downward curve, so they believed it was better not to give their data away.

They didn’t realize that corporate AIs automatically disqualified candidates with missing or outdated tests. Not that Medard had great chances — but by refusing to update his results, he was unknowingly sawing off the branch he sat on.

Still, Xavier’s recommendation now carried weight for Medard.

“Why should I go there?” he asked curiously, pointing at his growing belly. “You know, I’ve been a bit out of shape lately.”

But before Xavier could answer, he noticed an unfamiliar girl approaching him with a cardboard box in her hands.

“May I treat you, sir?”

She was a tall beauty with long blond hair and blue eyes, dressed in a bright white shirt and pants. Without waiting for permission, she quickly knelt in the grass beside him, unconcerned that the clean fabric would get stained. She examined him swiftly, made him twist his wrist, lift his legs, and paused for a moment at the old, unremoved scars snaking under his knees and calves. She offered no comment.

Out of place in her role, the blonde had slender fingers with pink nail polish, luxury women’s watches on her wrist, and smelled of a subtle perfume. Nothing suggested she was the kind of young woman who routinely played Samaritan and treated injured strangers.

“I have a regenerative injection that should help you,” she explained. “It looks like you need it.”

“That’s not necessary.”

Startled, Xavier immediately pulled back. The last thing he was willing to do was accept an injection from someone he didn’t know, no matter how tempting the offer sounded.

The girl seemed surprised by Xavier’s reaction and, with disarming naivety, showed him a transparent ampoule labeled RI‑01 and a syringe sealed in plastic.

“I keep it for my brother,” she explained. “It’s cheap, but it works. If you don’t take it, I doubt you’ll walk away from here on your own.”

“That’s not your concern, miss,” Xavier snapped coldly.

“Don’t be like that,” Medard said, looking embarrassed. “It’s very kind of you, miss, but…”

“You don’t have to worry,” the girl assured them, not understanding what the problem was. “I study at the local medical faculty. I know what I’m doing. I’m here with my brother. You can ask him.”

She pointed at a young man standing behind her, watching Xavier with a guarded expression. He did resemble her, but his features lacked feminine softness, giving him a stern, almost hostile look. Worse, he carried a backpack with a wooden handle sticking out — something he could use as a weapon at any moment. Even though his skin lacked the redness typical of supertonic users, Xavier would bet that both siblings came from a family far from ordinary.

To make matters worse, another girl stood at the young man’s side — this time dark‑haired — and she sent chills down Xavier’s spine. Maybe the fight with Xantipa Miller had shaken him, or the painful session with Dr. Blackheart had rattled his nerves, but he felt he knew her.

At first glance, he could tell the dark‑haired girl didn’t come from the same social circles as the siblings. All her electronics belonged to the cheapest category, meant for plastics; her skin looked natural, without the otherworldly perfection of expensive cosmetics and supplements that distinguished silvers and golds from lower citizens.

Yet she still possessed an exotic charm; perhaps that was why the siblings allowed her to accompany them. It wasn’t unusual for beauties from lower classes to fight for the chance to follow better‑situated peers and gain access to resources they otherwise wouldn’t have.

“My sister studies medicine,” the young man said neutrally. “We are Svetlana and Grigori Troyak. You can trust us.”

Xavier had never heard of the Troyak family. It would have been strange if he had — the city had over ten million inhabitants. But Grigori Troyak introduced himself with the confidence of someone everyone should know.

“Really?” Xavier gasped. “*The* Troyaks? What an honor, sir!”

When the young man realized he had been mocked, he blushed slightly. His dark‑haired companion smiled faintly, as if she felt no obligation to be embarrassed on behalf of the pack leader.

“I thought,” the young man explained, showing his citizen‑candidate ID, “that our intentions were honest.”

“And your name is?”

Xavier couldn’t tear his eyes away from the dark‑haired girl. He couldn’t know her — certainly not, she had to be fifteen years younger — yet he felt he had shared something intimate with someone like her. When he asked her name, a tremor of the past stirred in him, the shy hesitation of a young man trying to coax a phone number from a pretty classmate.

“Gabrielle Marrow,” the dark‑haired girl said calmly. “Citizen‑candidate. Chemistry student. You have nice bruises, sir.”

“Valehart Xavier,” he said formally, extending his hand. She accepted it, and the mild surprise on her face came not from fear of infection but from the unexpectedly friendly gesture. While he refused Svetlana Troyak’s injection, he introduced himself to Gabrielle. To cover his *faux pas*, Xavier offered his hand to the others as well. None of them showed much enthusiasm about meeting a silver — not even Gabrielle.

*She unsettles me!* Xavier thought. *Any other girl would sell her parents for a chance to meet me.*

In his world of intrigue, the presence of such a person triggered caution, not romantic fantasies. He knew well that people who wanted to get to him could access his psychological profile. Choosing a young woman he would subconsciously react to wasn’t difficult. He had always had a weakness for this southern type with golden‑tinted skin. As a European, he suffered from the prejudice that being tan meant being healthy and attractive.

“That will do!”

Lost in thought, he didn’t notice Svetlana Troyak injecting his arm.

“I didn’t give permission,” he growled and grabbed her wrist, making her cry out in pain.

“Let her go immediately!”

Her brother reacted quickly — too quickly. He pulled something from his backpack that looked like a broom handle. Upon closer inspection, Xavier recognized a wooden practice sword used in eastern fencing. *A brother defending his sister? What an old cliché!*

As for him, he had no doubts. **First‑Savings** had decided to collect the debt he owed them. The one named Svetlana was supposed to provoke trouble and *treat* him under a convenient pretext. God knows what she had injected into his veins. Maybe a sedative. After all, how else do you drug someone on the street except under the guise of good intentions? The dark‑haired Gabrielle served as a distraction — the type he would trust because she lacked the aura of silvers, let alone golds. A simple girl from an honest, unambitious family.

*Will she watch them beat me? She always watched. She was like that!* He had met someone similar before, but couldn’t recall when or where.

Gabrielle stirred memories that made no sense. Even if **First‑Savings** wanted to take him down, they wouldn’t act so subtly as to target his subconscious. Their managers resembled Xantipa Miller — always planning directly, without unnecessary frills. When they shouted they would destroy him, they wouldn’t think of digging into his long‑buried past.

“I apologize, Grigori,” he said, releasing Svetlana from his grip. He made sure to address the young man by name and watched his reaction, ready to pull the blonde in front of him as a shield. Grigori Troyak blushed again, then hid the practice sword behind his back.

“I apologize, Mr. Valehart. My sister should have waited for your permission. But she’s like that by nature. She likes helping the weak and injured.”

He said the last words with clear disapproval, as if afraid he might look soft in front of others. *Of course,* Xavier thought. *Compassion is weakness.*

“I’m sorry, Miss Svetlana,” he said gently. “In my defense, I must note that I wouldn’t be surprised if a certain corporation tried to drug me on the street. You know how it goes.”

“I have no idea,” Svetlana replied calmly, remarkably unfazed by the brief drama. “How many fingers do you see, Mr. Valehart? I noticed you took a hit to the head. You might have a concussion.”

“I doubt it. I took a weak blow within my tolerance.”

“You can tell?” Grigori asked, surprised and with a hint of admiration. “You must have experience, right?”

“You’re both talking nonsense,” Svetlana scolded them. “You should never underestimate a head injury, Mr. Valehart. By the way, when the fight started, you mentioned insomnia. One of my professors specializes in sleep disorders. I can give you his contact.”

“No need.”

Xavier half liked and half hated how she fussed over him.

“He cured many patients without drugging them and handing them over to corporations,” Svetlana added sharply. “In fact, I’d be very happy to recommend a psychiatrist as well. I’m completely serious — you don’t need to smirk. Paranoia is common among silver and gold citizens.”
