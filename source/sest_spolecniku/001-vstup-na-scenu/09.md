# I have these scars because of her

Although it didn’t bother him that the girl thought he was insane, Xavier struggled to absorb how his world was collapsing. Meeting a *second* person who possessed what people dismissively called a *good heart* was so conspicuous it had to be a conspiracy. He watched Medard out of the corner of his eye — the second suspect — to see whether he exchanged secret signals with the siblings, but the bearded man wandered back and forth in a desperate attempt to be useful.

Gabriela Marrow, the dark‑haired beauty, was bored and examined the electronics display, where an OR headband caught her attention — a model reserved for higher citizen categories. Even if she had the money, she couldn’t afford it. Xavier suppressed a completely irrational urge to buy the headband for her.

George Troyak, who stood politely nearby, asked several questions about the dueling system — and it seemed he couldn’t wait to undergo his first fight.

“Do you think Mrs. Miller made a mistake by choosing a fistfight? What would give her a better chance?”

“First…” Xavier focused on George so he wouldn’t have to focus on Gabriela. *Just an ordinary girl. She can’t possibly know my past.*

“First, it was *me*,” he said firmly, “who offered the draw — and Mrs. Miller did me the favor of accepting it. Hardly a mistake.”

“I don’t believe it. You were starting to gain the upper hand.”

“Second, even if they taught you otherwise, victory at any cost is nonsense.”

“Sure, but you gained nothing here, so why…?”

Naturally, Xavier had no intention of revealing the backstage reasons for his decision.

“Third,” he continued, without answering, “I keep my weaknesses to myself. So don’t expect me, friend, to tell you how to beat me.”

“I see. You must think I’m an idiot.”

Xavier actually liked George, so he frowned. He couldn’t wait to return to the safety of his apartment, shut himself off from the world, and focus on his goal. He looked at Gabriela — and finally remembered. The director of the youth institution, a stunning beauty, the secret object of every young man’s dreams! Fourteen‑year‑old revolutionaries, foolish and confused, were easily persuaded — and those who weren’t, she had other methods for.

“Gabriela dislikes older men,” Svetlana Troyak murmured when she finished treating him. “It probably won’t be bad for you, since you’re staring at her like that.”

“What was her mother’s maiden name?”

“No idea. And you’d better not ask her. Gabriela is an orphan,” Svetlana said sternly. “Besides, telling a young girl you knew her mother? That’s cliché, isn’t it? Hello, lost love of my life!”

“I certainly hope not,” Xavier smirked and pointed at his legs. “I have these scars because of her.”

“Ah.” Svetlana examined him with professional interest. “Does it often happen that random passersby are characters from your past? Do you think they threaten you? Hurt you? Talk about you behind your back?”

“Essentially yes,” Xavier admitted. “Nonstop. For example, Medard here,” he pointed at the bearded man patiently standing nearby. “I had the impression he was making a deal with the bookmaker who took bets on my fight.”

“And that’s true,” Medard explained awkwardly. “He wanted you to call him. He promised me money if it worked out. But of course I won’t pressure you.”

“Do you have his card?”

“Yes, here.”

“Clifford Oplatka? Never heard of him. I’ll call him and force him to pay you… Any more questions, future doctor?” Xavier asked mockingly. But the girl had the hide of a rhinoceros — sarcasm didn’t touch her.

However, Medard, far more sensitive to subtle cues, shifted uneasily and, to steer the conversation elsewhere, pointed at the cardboard box Svetlana had set aside.

“Something alive, huh?” he asked kindly. “A guinea pig or a hamster?”

“Here?” Svetlana, until now confident, hesitated. “None of those.”

“I like animals too. May I look?”

“I believe you’ll regret opening that box.”

With awakened interest, Gabriela Marrow turned and fixed her black eyes on Medard. Xavier felt his heart pounding. *I believe you’ll regret it… That diction, that indifferent calm with a hint of mischief. Could Gabriela be her daughter?*

“What’s behind those doors, Madam Director?”

“I believe you’ll regret opening them, my little Xavier.”

He shook his head. He didn’t see any explicit family resemblance between the women — only a certain kinship in gestures and speech.

“Nonsense.”

Despite the warning, Medard — with Svetlana Troyak’s explicit permission (he would never force his way into someone’s property without it) — opened the box lid. The moment he looked inside, he jumped back with a scream, frantically rummaged in his pocket, and pressed a handkerchief to his mouth.

On the dirty bottom sat a white dove, more dead than alive, with drops of blood on its injured wing. Someone had placed a few handfuls of torn grass inside and an improvised water dish made from a cut plastic bottle. The bird stared blankly ahead, submerged in its pain.

Had an angry cobra’s head emerged from the box, it couldn’t have frightened Medard more. No one laughed. The fear of birds among unvaccinated citizens was well known. Some shot them on sight, desperately trying to eradicate anything with wings. Meanwhile, wealthier districts let peacocks roam the streets to deter plastics from entering.

“Mr. Medard,” Svetlana said gently. “VIUR isn’t transmitted by birds. You shouldn’t believe alarmist rumors.”

“I know!”

Even though pale Medard didn’t dare come closer, he forced himself to lower the handkerchief.

“I worked for corporations,” he added in a desperate attempt not to look like a superstitious bumpkin. Xavier sighed. He would have loved to shake Medard and tell him to stop waving around his inglorious bronze period.

“If you’re scared,” Xavier said, “keep your distance. No need to feel guilty. And you, future doctor, don’t lecture him.”

“I wanted Mr. Medard to feel safer,” Svetlana shrugged.

“Sure,” Xavier replied. “Without vaccination he’ll die within a few years. How safe does he feel?”

For the umpteenth time, Medard rushed in to prevent an argument. “I’m fine. I feel great. A bit of irrational fear never killed anyone. Besides, I was lucky,” he added with a hint of pride, “I became a Player. The doctor assured me that getting a bronze badge is only a matter of time.”

“I’m sure he also recommended,” Xavier said sourly, “that you brag about it on every street corner.”

Medard fell silent, embarrassed.

“What’s wrong?” George asked. “You know, we were also on our way to the GC. If we get into the Game, should we keep quiet?”

Xavier pointed at the line of applicants and the even larger crowd of disgruntled citizens interrogating everyone who came out.

“This Game,” Xavier said, “raised huge hopes and caused even bigger disappointment. Every plastic feels like they’re being denied access even to virtual reality. Don’t be surprised they’re angry.”

“I see.”

“Not only that,” Gabriela added quietly, “a lot of silvers and golds are trying to get in. On the other hand,” she added mischievously, “not everyone agrees. Didn’t your father forbid you to come here?”

“My father can go to hell,” George snapped. “Aren’t you here with us?”

“I owe your father nothing,” Gabriela said calmly.

“And I do?”

“Some would disagree. But every child needs to rebel against authority. Isn’t that right, Mr. Valehart? You could advise us. I don’t believe your friend got inside without your help.”

Medard turned to Gabriela, wounded by how she belittled his abilities.

“I…”

*Well, well!* Xavier understood why the dark‑haired girl had spent so much time staring into the shop window. She wasn’t interested in the smart headband — she had been interested in him from the start. She watched him like a reflection in the glass, seemingly indifferent but always with her finger on the pulse of the situation. He remembered the director of the institution, sitting before a battery of monitors, pointing at them with a slender finger and telling him kindly: “Here I am all‑knowing and all‑powerful, my little Xavier. I shouldn’t advise you, but some ancient civilizations believed it wasn’t a sin to defy authority. The sin was getting caught.”

“Even if you’re right, advice is valuable,” he noted. “Someone has to pay for it, **my little** Gabriela.”

*Nothing. My little fell flat. I must have been wrong.*

“What about a debt of gratitude?” the girl suggested. “Svetlana treated you.”

“Without me asking.”

“What happened, happened.”

“True,” Xavier admitted. “But how does that concern you? Let me guess. You want to manipulate me into advising Svetlana *gratis pro deo*.”

Neither sibling defended Gabriela. Only Medard charged into a pointless attack, but Xavier stopped him. “Don’t worry, friend. We understand each other. How about letting yourself be bribed?”

“Me? I couldn’t. I got into the Game thanks to you. And I promised I wouldn’t tell anyone anything.”

Xavier shook his head. For some people it would be a pose, but Medard was genuinely honest and naive to the bone. *Shoot him and stuff him for a museum! It would be better if he stayed quiet. In a few sentences he revealed everything the girl needed to know!*

“Plastic once, plastic forever,” Xavier sighed. “I’ll tell you how it will go. Once I leave, you’ll stay behind and Gabriela will make you an offer. When it’s time to pay, she’ll ask the Troyaks for a loan she simply won’t return. Am I right, Svetlana?”

“You think the worst of people,” Svetlana protested, while her brother only shrugged. “If such a situation arose, the money would come out of my pocket. Svetlana doesn’t believe in our society’s value system.”

“I noticed. We all carry our burdens,” Xavier said, pointing at Medard like a rare exhibit. “A pure soul, a species on the brink of extinction. Svetlana, a moment please. We’ll talk in private… But first a small legal disclaimer,” he added with a smirk, “I am in no way planning to violate the confidentiality I signed at the GC. I simply want to arrange a private meeting with a beautiful young lady. Maybe invite her to dinner. Brother as protector won’t object, I assume.”

“Svetlana is an adult,” George said with a crooked smile and clenched fists. He didn’t like what Xavier was implying. “It’s none of my business whom she meets or how.”

*Another one playing the villain,* Xavier thought. He noticed how Svetlana flinched when her brother didn’t defend her more. The siblings were far from Dr. BlackHeart and his verbal poker of hints and insinuations. Dark‑haired Gabriela turned back to the shop window, twirling strands of hair around her tanned fingers.

*That girl is a natural talent! She knows silence is enough.*

But not everyone understood their role so well.

“I don’t want your advice,” Svetlana snapped. “I treated you because you needed help. Nothing more, nothing less.”
