# He Looks at Me as if I Were a Divine Revelation

“For a drink?”

Despite his washed‑out appearance, the vice‑chairman of the student council wasn’t afraid to strike while the iron was hot. *But really,* Xavier thought, *what could he lose? Either he offends a silver‑nose stranger or the gamble pays off.*

Had a beautiful female student stood where Jacob Lickboothe stood now, her offer would have been more obvious. Yet with the rise of virtual reality and the growing aversion to physical contact, such bets had become long shots. People dreamed of perfect bodies they knew from the digital world — imagined or cultivated through expensive cosmetics, plastic surgery, and nutritional supplements. And many silver-noses were asexual.

Under normal circumstances, Xavier wouldn’t have bothered with Jacob; he disliked young, ambitious types who believed in society’s philosophy. But now, needing to find Svetlana and her companions, he couldn’t afford to miss the opportunity.

“All right,” he said at last. “I’ve got a moment. I think you can help me, Jacob. Across the street I saw *Moonstones*. Let’s sit there.”

Jacob nodded, suddenly a shade paler. *Moonstones* operated a chain of luxury cafés and restaurants accessible only to silver citizens and above. Candidate‑citizens couldn’t enter without an invitation, so for Jacob Lickboothe, stepping inside would be like landing on **cloud nine**. Affording the prices, however…

“No worries,” Xavier said, skipping his usual smirk. “I’m paying. As I said, you can help me.”

*You can help me,* he thought, amused. *Not **I need help**. I’m giving you the chance, my young friend, to be useful.*

“Of course, sir. With pleasure. How exactly?”

“I’m looking for someone. But that can wait — nothing urgent.”

There were several reasons Jacob might want to wriggle out of helping find Svetlana and her group. First, providing contact information about a third party was illegal, and enough scandals had surfaced to scare anyone. Second, people like Jacob believed opportunities were limited, so sabotaging someone else meant helping oneself. Third, if Jacob wanted to stay in touch with Xavier, all he had to say was: *‘I’ll ask around and let you know. May I have your card?’*

The third option suited Jacob best. For Xavier, less so — he didn’t want to hand out his number or wait.

“Do you think it’s open?”

Anyone standing before *Moonstones* for the first time would ask the same. The building had no windows, and its only entrance looked more like the gate to a treasure vault.

“It’s open,” Xavier nodded and placed his identifier on the reader. A message appeared:

> For further communication, please use your smart headband or smart glasses. Or would you prefer to speak with human staff?

“I’m here! Shall I handle it?”

Without being prompted, the little fairy offered her services, facing the avatar of a waiter in pressed trousers, a white shirt, and a polished velvet vest.

*No, call a real employee.*

“Are you insane?” snapped the fairy. “Do you know how long it’ll take for someone to show up? How can you be efficient if you waste five minutes of your life waiting?”

*Shut up!*

Normally, Xavier would have agreed with his tiny tormentor, but today, if he wanted to impress his guest, a human waiter had more weight than a holographic avatar.

“What would you like, sir?”

It didn’t take five minutes before the massive doors opened — this time with an actual person between them.

“A table for two,” Xavier ordered. “Then bring us two *Calls of the Heavenly Heron* and one *Tear of the Fiery Flame*. Pronto!”

“Certainly, sir.”

When the waiter left with a bow, the fairy hopped onto the chrome railing by his left hand, crossed her arms, and shouted: “*Heavenly Heron* and *Fiery Flame*? Both cost a fortune! When do I get my *Profi* package?”

Though Jacob couldn’t hear her, he must have thought the same — his face showed awe and gratitude.

Xavier’s heart bled too, but the act he played wasn’t his own invention. He copied it *verbatim* from a former encounter with one of his golden clients.

Everything was there: the insolent manners, the disregard for the guest, and the overpriced order for effect.

Yet while Xavier had endured his client through gritted teeth, Jacob found such behavior impressive. Perhaps he imagined that one day he would enter *Moonstones* himself, accompanied by a suite of eager subordinates.

“This place is amazing,” he said, settling into the leather seat and gazing at the surrounding telescreens that broadcast the street outside, creating the illusion of open space.

“It’s an overpriced fortress,” Xavier sneered. “Only those who don’t trust themselves need to hide inside.”

“A fortress, sir?”

It was almost frightening to watch the young man devour his every word. Xavier preferred people who trusted no one and nothing.

“You didn’t know? Of course, you don’t come here, so it’s understandable,” he added with the disdain of a true silver‑nose. “*Moonstones* are designed to provide a safe haven for people like me — and perhaps one day, you — in case of street protests.”

“Yes, sir. That’s extremely enlightening.”

*Yes, sir? Enlightening? Nothing more?*

Xavier imagined himself in Jacob’s place, rising from the table and throwing the *Heron* into the silver snob’s face. *Does the boy have no self‑respect? He looks at me as if I were a divine revelation!*

Xavier pointed to the red cut‑glass cup filled with *Fiery Flame*.

“Let’s drink. I don’t have much time, Jacob, so briefly. A student helped me when I got into a duel. Today I passed the university and thought I’d thank her or her brother. I don’t have much time, so I hope you’ll help me handle it quickly.”

“Of course, sir. What are their names?”

While the young man eagerly offered his help, Xavier waved his hand toward the glass to show how little he cared about the matter.

“The *Fiery Flame* will guide you in the right direction,” he explained with conviction he could only fake. “They say whoever tastes *Fiery Flame* once can’t stop until he can afford it regularly.”

“I’d have to be at least silver,” Jacob said dreamily.

“Exactly.”

The secret of *Fiery Flame* lay in its composition — a mix of addictive substances inducing mild euphoria, combined with alcohol.

“Excellent,” Jacob smacked his lips approvingly. But his words meant little; he would have praised horse urine if it were served at *Moonstones*.

“Mr. Valehart,” he continued. “Tell me the name and I’ll arrange everything. You can rely on me.”

“Forget it. Maybe later. Now pour yourself the *Call of the Heron*. I’ve had better teas, but I admit the pot is exceptional.”

With the expression of an art historian admiring a masterpiece, Xavier studied the impractical heron‑shaped teapot made of fine porcelain and covered with golden bands. Nothing else interested him.

When Jacob realized he had somehow lost the chance to serve the silver‑nose, he panicked.

“Believe me,” he blurted, “as vice‑chairman of the student council I have access to the student database. Once you tell me the name, you’ll have the number in five minutes.”

“Incompetent bungler,” muttered the bored fairy, who was restless because the restaurant was empty. “I’d need ten seconds.”

Xavier yawned. “I think I was hasty. The student did treat me, but she was impertinent. Let’s drop it.”

“Mr. Valehart, just give me the name.”

“I think she was called Svetlana Troyak. But forget it. Her brother’s name was Grigori.”

“Those two? Just a moment.”

Indeed, it didn’t take long before Jacob wrote Svetlana’s and Juraj’s numbers on a piece of stiff paper that turned out to be his own business card.

“Smart move,” Xavier commented approvingly, but didn’t bother to offer his own phone number.

“If you ever need anything, let me know, Mr. Valehart.”

“And we’ve got what we wanted,” cheered the fairy. “Finally we can get out of this hole.”

But Xavier didn’t hurry. Not only because he didn’t want Jacob to know how important the contact was to him, but also because he had regained the ability to sleep — so he could enjoy the drug’s effect and the presence of a flatterer hanging on his every word.

“I’m sorry, but I have to go,” Jacob said suddenly.

“What?”

*I am the one who should dismiss you, young man,* thought Xavier with unpleasant surprise.

“I truly, truly apologize, Mr. Valehart, but it’s noon and I’m trying to catch the director of the **Institute for Advanced Construction Technologies**. I want to discuss my thesis with her. She comes here for lunch at this hour.”

“Understand.”

The fairy stared at him mockingly and tapped her oversized watch. With horror, Xavier realized he had not only lost an hour chatting with the young bootlicker — he had enjoyed it.

*How to get disappointed in myself,* he thought.

“That’s why you people never accomplish anything,” the fairy muttered. “We can leave now, you pitiful creature. I’ve already paid. The local avatar sends his regards and hopes to see you again.”

*What did you tell him?*

“That you’re stupid enough to come again!”

*Ouch, that hurts,* Xavier thought humbly. He expected her to demand the *Profi* package, but she merely circled around him in silence, very meaningful silence.

Outside the restaurant, the two men parted.

“I have to run! Goodbye, Mr. Valehart! There is my supervisor.”

Jacob pointed at a young, formally dressed woman emerging from the university gates with a briefcase in hand, and sprinted toward her.

Just as Xavier turned the nearest corner, he heard a scream behind him: “Help! Murder! Help me!”
