# **Only a Fool Meddles in Other People’s Business!**

*Attempted murder? In broad daylight? You’ve got to be joking.*

The society Xavier lived in was a polite attempt at survival of the fittest. People didn’t die on the streets; VISP always left enough time to clean up early cases. Patients breathed their last in hospitals, in specialized wards, where they wouldn’t frighten other unvaccinated Plastics. Violence — apart from ritualized duels — was tolerated only within narrow limits.

Even Xavier hesitated when he witnessed the attack: two men trying to stab a young woman. She defended herself with a black briefcase, bits of leather flying off with every strike. Jacob Lickboothe and the other onlookers kept a respectful distance, shouting like woodland creatures without daring to intervene.

*Syndicate,* Xavier realized when he spotted the black‑and‑yellow wasp masks on the attackers’ faces. A clear warning: *Mind your own business — or pay the price.*

So no one stepped in, clinging to the touching belief that someone braver might appear.

“The official recommendation in cases like this,” the little fairy remarked as she watched Xavier take off his jacket and wrap it around his left arm, “is to stay back and wait for the police… But why do I even bother,” she added with a hopeless shake of her head. “We’re not making it to the end of the year.”

As he broke into a run, Xavier felt the lizard inside him stir — thrilled by the hunt.

*Can the influence of the Game spill into the real world?*

“Why not?” the fairy chimed. *Yesterday we found a virus that would eventually drive you insane. If you think you’re an animal, try biting them.*

One masked man stepped into Xavier’s path.

“Back off, hero,” he growled. “Or we’ll find you.”

Xavier feinted left, and when the man lunged to block him, Xavier changed direction, raised his arm, and slammed him to the ground. The man’s head hit the asphalt with a dull thud.

The other two didn’t even turn around before Xavier was between them, striking at their unprotected necks. One dropped instantly; the second had the luck and reflexes to dodge sideways.

“What’s wrong with you, lunatic?” he shouted, swinging his knife. “Can’t you see who we are?”

The lizard in Xavier wanted to leap forward without regard for consequences — and Xavier didn’t stop him. With every strike came a rush unlike anything he had ever felt. Yesterday, fighting Xantippa Miller, he had been cold‑blooded, obsessed with control. The lizard, however, couldn’t think in anything but primitive terms — pure, animal fury.

The thrill didn’t last long. A street thug was no challenge for Xavier, who, as a silver citizen, had access to technologies that enhanced the human body. And although he hadn’t gone to the extremes Xantippa had — drinking supertonics like water — he had undergone a similar transformation.

What startled him was the speed of his reactions. It seemed the lizard’s dominance bypassed the higher parts of his brain, letting him perceive the world on a primal level.

“You’re not going anywhere!”

When they realized they had failed, the masked attackers tried to flee. Xavier managed to catch only one — the one threatening the woman with a knife.

*Shame,* he thought. The automatic civic system awarded points for every apprehended criminal. Xavier had no hope of becoming a gold citizen, but he certainly didn’t want to slip down to bronze.

“Are you all right, miss?” he asked as he tied up his trophy.

“You should be asking whether *you’ll* be all right,” hissed the bound man. “Only a fool meddles in other people’s business. We’ll find you. You’re finished, you hear?”

“Quiet. I’ve heard that song from other birds,” Xavier said calmly. “Corporations, politicians, soldiers, Syndicate thugs — they’ve all threatened me. As you can see, I’m still here.”

*Though I usually had a powerful sponsor behind me,* he admitted silently. *This time I’m playing solo.*

He wasn’t overly concerned; attempted murder in broad daylight was too much even for organized crime. The Syndicate existed, oppressed Bronzes and Plastics, but rarely killed — and certainly not silver citizens.

“Are you all right, miss?” he repeated.

He scanned her quickly, as he was used to. She was striking — nearly thirty, with russet hair the sun turned to gold. Her eyes were green, elongated, a little fox‑like, and her heart‑shaped face had high cheekbones emphasized by makeup.

Her silver badge rested beneath her blouse, discreetly tucked under the buttons, now slipped out with her identifier. Her outfit was half formal, half comfortable. She held her leather briefcase like a shield, white documents peeking through the slashes.

“Director,” Jacob Lickboothe called timidly from behind. “Are you all right?”

The woman shot him a disgusted look.

“Perfectly,” she snapped. “Thank you, Jacob, for your heroic screaming. I would have appreciated more support.”

“They had wasp masks,” Jacob muttered. “I didn’t know what to do.”

“You did well,” Xavier said, patting his shoulder. “Society expects us to look after ourselves first. You didn’t want to get tangled with the Syndicate. Who could blame you?”

“I still feel ashamed.”

To Jacob’s credit, he didn’t settle for the excuse offered.

“Do you really believe that?” the woman asked, her voice edged with hostility. “If so, why did you bother?”

“Odd way to thank me, miss.”

“And do I even have anything to thank you for, Mr. Valehart?”

Hearing his name, Xavier turned sharply, paranoia spiking. *I’ve never met her in my life.*

“How do you know me?” he asked coldly.

“Shouldn’t I?” she smirked. “I’m sure we both know exactly where we stand.”

*She wants to play games?* Xavier shook his head and turned to Jacob: “Who is she?”

“The director of the **Institute for Advanced Technologies**,” Jacob explained. “It’s part of our university. I’m sure this is a misunderstanding.”

“Her name?” Xavier barked.

Jacob hesitated. The woman laughed.

“Don’t torment our timid little lamb, Mr. Valehart. It’s no misunderstanding. Or do you want to pretend the name Lydia Carver means nothing to you? That you’re here by pure coincidence?”

*Carver? Lydia?*

“Memory like a sieve!”

When Xavier shook his head, the fairy waved her wand and conjured a hologram — a stylized but unmistakable scene of Xantippa Miller and her melodramatic performance.

*Ah.*

“What’s your relationship with young Alfred?” Xavier asked.

“My stepbrother,” Lydia replied coolly. “Why the theatrics, troublemaker? Weren’t you the one who sent those three thugs after me? Should I be grateful you saved me?”

“That’s right,” the captured thug snarled. “He sent me here.”

Xavier kicked him so hard the man slid a meter across the pavement.

“Nice try,” Xavier said. “And that’s why I captured you — so you could testify against me, right? Save it for the police.”

“Director,” Jacob said timidly. “I was with Mr. Valehart at the restaurant. We left because I wanted to speak with you. If I hadn’t, we’d still be sitting there and you would be…”

The student delicately left the sentence hanging.

“…dead,” Lydia finished coldly. “And I’m supposed to believe in such a charming coincidence? Either you’re a naïve fool or you’re working with him.”

“Believe whatever you want,” Xavier muttered. “You don’t owe me gratitude. I’ll hand this little Syndicate wannabe over to the police and leave.”

“I’ll testify against you,” the thug growled. “And she’ll back me up. She knows you’re behind this.”

Lydia struck him with her punctured briefcase so hard its contents spilled out.

Among the documents lay a familiar box with the GC logo. Xavier snatched it up quickly and handed it to her, hidden in his palm. *Another Player?* he thought. *This doesn’t look like coincidence at all.*

“Careful,” he warned.

With a suspicious — though not entirely hostile — look, Lydia tucked the box into her pocket. “You know what this is?” she whispered.

“For the same reason you do,” he nodded.

Police sirens grew louder. Two cars arrived, while more sirens echoed from afar — likely chasing the other two attackers.

With omnipresent cameras, the police had near‑perfect oversight, making such street attacks almost impossible.

Xavier had no doubt the fugitives wouldn’t escape. But he doubted the Syndicate had anything to do with the attack.

“In the end,” he said, examining his captive, “anyone can wear a wasp mask. I’d say you’re just a street punk, boy. Show me your neck.”

As expected, beneath the man’s shirt he found gang tattoos — clear proof the Syndicate wasn’t involved.

The police stepped out of their vehicle, batons drawn.

“Sir,” one officer said, “please step away from the detainee.”

“Not so fast,” Xavier replied.

He waited while the fairy prepared the detention confirmation, including a full recording of everything that had happened — except the detail about the HC box.

“I assume,” Xavier said, “you’ve already caught his two friends.”

“Of course.”

“Excellent. At least something went right.”

Xavier made sure to sound thoroughly displeased.

“You took your time. Director Carver will want to discuss compensation with you. As will this young man,” he pointed at Jacob, “who risked his life calling for help. As for me, civic points will suffice.”

Lydia and Jacob stared in shock. Compensation? For what?  
The officer merely nodded with a sour expression. The police, like almost everything in society, belonged to the corporate sphere. Their duty was to ensure safety — and if they failed, citizens exposed to danger were entitled to compensation, financial or in civic points deducted from the police’s own account.

Naturally, such attacks were rare, so few people knew about this — and the police certainly didn’t advertise it.

“Why are you helping me?” Lydia whispered. “Is this another trick? Do you think I need money or a handful of points?”
