# **Are You Sure You’re Not Insane?**

While Jolana danced, stomped, and waved her arms, her two companions stared at her as if she’d lost her mind. Luckily for them, even the lizards seemed perplexed by the situation, so the stalemate continued.

“I’m not crazy,” their companion sang in a high, clear voice. “I’m running out of strength and shaking with fear. Join in.”

“Are you sure you’re not insane?”

“Not at all, not at all,” Jolana sang, then whispered quickly, “Can the mutant understand human speech?”

“If it swallowed a human memory crystal, then yes.”

“Then I have no choice but to keep singing and hope you join me.”

“Join you?”

For her commander, the idea that he should participate in her madness was so revolutionary he couldn’t muster a reply. Suddenly the third, silent member of their group — who hadn’t taken his eyes off the rocky overhang — timidly stomped and performed a few clumsy hops.

*Is the dancing contagious?* the commander wondered. But since he’d known the quiet man for years, he knew he wouldn’t lose his nerve. When he too looked toward the rocky overhang, something caught his attention.

*A living rock?*

There, in a crack directly above the mutated varran, a stone moved. *No — that’s not a stone! That’s a person! A high‑tier forester!* The commander had seen a chameleon suit only twice in his life, and always on men and women who belonged to the elite among wilderness scouts. The fabric alone cost gold crowns, and those who could afford to wear it were usually sponsored by noble houses and entitled to respectful address.

*What would someone like that be doing in our backwater?* the commander wondered. *Jolana stirred her inner flame — that’s why she saw him first.*

He didn’t know whether to laugh or cry. Sure, Jolana might hope the unknown forester had come to help, and that was why she was desperately distracting the lizards — but the commander had little hope. For a specialist of that rank, rescuing an insignificant trio might not be on his priority list. It was even possible that his mission in the *Forest of Adorable Owlets* required absolute secrecy, leaving no room for witnesses.

If the stranger wanted to help, why would he be sneaking above their heads instead of choosing a safer long‑range attack? Still, the commander said nothing — more out of lack of alternatives than conviction.

When the mutated lizard finally had enough of the undignified dancing and opened its jaws full of massive teeth, the unknown forester leapt from the rock. His chameleon suit couldn’t adapt to the sudden movement, so it looked as if a chunk of stone had fallen onto the prey.

Blue light flashed in his left hand, stunning the creature, while his right — holding a long fang‑knife — struck the lizard’s neck.

“Fire!”

There was no reason to wait. The two foresters stopped stomping and released their arrows. Despite their seemingly lazy nature, the lizards reacted like a military unit and charged forward with furious hissing.

A brief battle erupted, in which the beasts gained the upper hand. The only thing keeping them from reaching the three foresters was Jolana’s staff, which sent out short stunning bolts.

“Enough!”

When the situation looked hopeless, the unknown forester — having finished off the mutant — rushed to help them. Ignoring his own safety, he threw himself into the swarm of lizards, striking left and right. With hostile hissing, the creatures retreated before him like fire before water, then scattered and vanished into the forest.

“Thank you, honored sir,” the commander said. But when the stranger turned toward him, the next words died in his throat.

“He’s burning red stones!” Jolana cried, then immediately covered her mouth. “Forgive me, honored sir — I have inner sight, I couldn’t help noticing that…”

The man’s face bore signs of madness — uncontrolled wildness in his half‑present gaze, bared teeth, and rapid breathing.

“You!” he growled at her.

“Yes, honored sir?”

“You know?” The man tapped his belly.

“Yes, honored sir. I’m almost a certified temple healer,” Jolana said with a hint of pride. “A red stone is burning inside you. I don’t understand how it could have happened.”

“I ate it,” the stranger said.

“You ate it? Surely by accident,” Jolana said, hoping it was mere misfortune. But the stranger shook his head and leaned toward her, jaw thrust forward, tongue flicking like a varran scenting prey. “I ate the red stone… the gray crystal…”

“Why would you do that?”

“Why not?”

“Forgive me, honored sir — I’m sure you had your reasons. It’s just that everyone knows the consequences.”

“Con-sequences?”

The stranger clearly struggled to focus on her words. *No wonder,* Jolana thought. *It’s a miracle he’s even partly sane.*

“What is your name, honored sir?” she asked, trying to pull him out of the beast’s wildness. “Your name, honored sir?”

“Name?”

“Yes, name,” she repeated patiently. She remembered very little from temple lessons about how to handle such cases. It was generally assumed no one would ever do something so foolish — except perhaps in life‑or‑death situations. *I understand why he’d burn a red stone. But why absorb a memory crystal?*

“Xavier. I’m Xavier,” the stranger said. “It helps me… You speak… You what?”

*Does it help him when we talk? What does he want? To know what I am — or who I am?*

“I’m Jolana, honored Sir Xavier. These are my friends. We’re foresters.”

“They no name?” he asked with an almost human smirk. Jolana wasn’t sure whether helping him regain clarity was a good idea. Her commander signaled her to reveal as little as possible — standard procedure when foresters dealt with someone whose intentions were uncertain.

“Speak more, you,” he ordered when he noticed her hesitation. “Names not important. Speak only about me.”

*Only about him? Advanced narcissism? Should I flatter him?*

“Honored Sir Xavier has trouble expressing himself,” the commander said when he saw Jolana freeze. “I think he wants you to describe his condition. Use your inner sight.”

“Oh — apologies. If I’m to examine you, honored Sir Xavier, I must use my staff.”

“Staff? That stick you have? Magic?”

*Magic?*

Jolana was taken aback by the contradiction between his words and his advanced equipment. An expert shouldn’t use the word **magic** when referring to her staff. Only simple folk spoke that way — people for whom anything they didn’t understand became magic.

“Yes, magic,” she nodded. “For some,” she added, so she wouldn’t sound too impressed.

“I also can…”

With almost childlike enthusiasm, eager to prove he wasn’t lying, Xavier raised his left hand and let a burst of blue light explode in his palm.

All three foresters flinched and stepped back.  

“Did you see that?” the commander whispered. “Jolana, try not to anger him or we’re dead.”

Jolana nodded, thinking that facing a swarm of lizards hadn’t been the worst thing that happened today. A half‑mad specialist who could barely speak but could summon a stunning discharge by sheer will frightened her far more.

Xavier frowned: “No need fear. You, magic stick and magic. Speak only about me. You almost temple.”

Another smirk. Even though the mysterious Xavier struggled to form sentences, he didn’t miss how Jolana had called herself **almost certified**.

*So he understands complex words — and catches irony,* she thought, then said aloud: “I’ll examine you now, honored sir. Please stay calm.”

She activated the diagnostic function of her staff and slowly scanned Xavier, who watched her with curiosity. The more he focused on external stimuli, the more his wild frenzy faded.

“The red stone is almost burned out,” she exhaled with relief. “It caused some damage, but nothing a healer couldn’t fix.”

“You?”

“Me? No!” she yelped, horrified. *Does he not understand what **almost certified** means? Sure, I can wander the forest with a staff — but treating someone of his rank would ruin me forever.*

“It seems,” she continued quickly, “that your DNA has a lizard trace. It was luck in misfortune that you swallowed the stone and the crystal together. Otherwise the stone would have burned out your insides.”

“DNA?” Xavier asked incredulously, pointing at the staff. “Not magic?”

“Of course not,” Jolana replied, slightly offended. “The memory crystal you swallowed must be affecting you. Otherwise you wouldn’t believe in magic.”

This time Xavier looked more amused than confused. He muttered something to himself, then pressed the stone on his ring.

“Multidimensional space,” the three foresters gasped, overwhelmed with envy. Not even in the temple where Jolana had studied had she seen such a luxurious version of multidimensional storage.

*We should warn this Xavier,* she thought. *Someone will rob him. Does he think we’re so honest — or that we pose no threat?*

Meanwhile Xavier pulled out a red stone, a memory crystal, and a loaf of bread. Then he asked:

“What price?”

“I’d say,” the commander murmured, “that honored Sir Xavier must be a foreigner. That would explain a lot.”

Jolana nodded and pointed at the bread:  “Twenty copper pennies. Red stones sell for silver talers, and memory crystals are priced by the data they contain. This one has high information density, so I’d guess you could get a gold coin for it — maybe more. But until a specialist decodes it, it’s hard to judge.”
