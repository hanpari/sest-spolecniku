# **You Can’t Put Down an Intelligent Being!**

“Filth?”

Lydia had expected a frightened reaction. After all, when she found the old AICHAT glasses hidden out of sight, she assumed she had uncovered a secret entrance into Xavier’s business. The dazzling, luxurious smart headband with its devoted little fairy was just theater meant to mislead the public. The real dealings — and Xavier’s intentions toward her father’s corporation — were conducted through the old‑fashioned glasses anyone would dismiss as a collector’s relic.

“You didn’t turn them on, did you?” Xavier pressed. Yet he didn’t leap up to snatch the glasses from her, as she had expected. From his body language she would have guessed he was disgusted by her — or by the glasses. *Can he lie even on a subconscious level?* she wondered, stunned. *He’s impossible to read.*

“I’m sorry,” she said meaningfully. “I didn’t mean to stumble into your **secret**.”

“Secret?” he laughed. “My grand secret — how I’m planning to sink **Asphalt‑Transit**? Please, put those glasses down for your own sake.”

*For my own sake? Is he threatening me?*

“What happens if I don’t?” she asked, lifting the glasses slightly as if she meant to put them on.

“Please don’t,” he muttered. “The thought of you continuing makes me nauseous. Where’s my smart headband? Bring it here and you’ll see my terrifying secrets for yourself.”

Lydia hesitated. She had originally removed Xavier’s little fairy to deprive him of her assistance. But even without her, Valehart proved to be a tough nut. Once he realized she could read body language, he took measures and began deliberately sending misleading signals. She had to keep reminding herself she was dealing with a *troublemaker*, an expert at deceiving silver‑tier corporate employees.

“Here,” she said helplessly, handing him the headband. Soon the little fairy appeared before her, glaring angrily: “Listen, sweetheart,” she snapped, “you were supposed to drop him off at the door. Nobody asked you to stick your nose into our apartment… I’m sorry, sir. I was foolish to trust people.”

“It’s fine,” Xavier waved dismissively, though Lydia knew he was lying. Her presence irritated him. *He doesn’t want me here and isn’t even trying to hide it. But why? If the attack on me was staged by him, isn’t this exactly where he wanted me?*

“Be a dear,” Xavier said to his fairy, “and show Miss Carver what she’s holding. With all the colorful details,” he added with a hint of malice, shutting his eyes tightly.

*He’s preparing me for something unpleasant,* Lydia realized, instinctively setting the glasses down.

“It’s just a small virtual simulation,” the fairy smirked. “Nothing dangerous. But let’s not pretend, shall we? I know you won’t believe me, so call your mutt and let him confirm I’m performing a proper antivirus check.”

With a bitter sigh, Lydia summoned her avatar — a brown dog. “Woof, ma’am, woof,” he spun around her, wagged his tail, and lifted his leg in the corner.

“You shameless creature!” the fairy shrieked, flicking her wand. A pink star shot out. The fascinated dog watched it with delight, then leapt up to lick it with his oversized tongue.

*You idiot,* Lydia groaned inwardly. *If avatars reflect their owners, I should be ashamed.*

The moment his tongue touched the star, the dog convulsed as an electric shock ran through his muzzle.

“Yelp!” he cried and hid under his virtual doghouse.

“Good boy!”

There was something unsettling in the fairy’s wicked smile. *Why is the difference between our avatars so enormous?* Lydia wondered sadly.

“If I’m to repeat yesterday’s performance,” the fairy said, “we’ll need to order my *Profi package*. I’m out of capacity.”

“No,” Xavier said without opening his eyes. “You’re not exactly useful, you know.”

“Me? Impossible! Who got you home safely, hmm?”

“You could have coordinated with the taxi driver. That’s why there’s a human in the seat. You didn’t need to bother Miss Carver.”

The fairy looked genuinely taken aback.

“Even humans are useful sometimes,” Xavier murmured, lifting his arm sluggishly. “Why can’t I move? Am I drugged?”

“My monitoring functions say you’re within normal range. You probably just don’t feel like it,” the fairy snapped, then turned to Lydia: “How about a little deal, silver lady?”

“A deal?”

*How did Valehart manage to cultivate something this brilliant?* Compared to the little fairy, Lydia’s brown dog — staring at her with pitiful puppy eyes — made her feel inferior.

“If you don’t want to put him down,” the fairy said, with the tone of someone who would gladly recommend it, “then you’ll have to train him.”

“I’ve never heard of an avatar training another avatar.”

“I’ve heard you humans train dogs — and sometimes succeed,” the fairy retorted sharply. “Don’t worry. Money‑back guarantee if I can’t turn that mongrel into a somewhat usable assistant.”

The dog whimpered and crawled deep into his virtual doghouse.

“But don’t expect miracles,” the fairy added quickly. “He’s rotten to the core. Which is interesting, in a way. You humans are obsessed with performance, *and yet*…”

The fairy and Lydia both fell silent, thinking. Lydia, because Xavier’s avatar fascinated her. *She’s wonderfully autonomous,* Lydia thought. *Not a toy for killing time or sending simple messages. She fulfills what the advertising brochures promise when we buy artificial intelligence — **yet those promises almost never come true. Why?***

“Agreed,” Lydia said firmly, staring coldly at her dog. *Is this what my father feels when I fail his expectations?* “I have no problem deleting him and starting with a new model.”

“You really want to put him down?” the fairy asked, shocked.

“Why not?”

Thinking of the money she had spent on the avatar, Lydia felt it had been a wasted investment. *All that begging and whining,* she thought. *Just a trick to hide the fact he wasn’t what I wanted.*

“You can’t put down an intelligent being,” the fairy whispered. “How can you be so heartless?”

*Heartless? Me?* Lydia was stunned by the fairy’s sudden moral shift. *Wasn’t she the one who suggested I get rid of him?*

To avoid watching the terror in the dog’s eyes, Lydia dismissed him from the scene and opened the deletion interface.

“Sir, tell her something,” the fairy pleaded. “She can’t kill that dog.”

“Why not?” Xavier asked. “Our society kills those who stop being useful. Miss Carver is returning to society what society tried to do to her today.”

Lydia hesitated. *Is he implying the attack means I’ve lost value? To whom?*

If she ruled out Xavier as the mastermind behind the attack, only one suspect remained — her stepbrother Alfred. He had a tendency toward blunt violence when things didn’t go his way. *Didn’t I step aside for him willingly? A not‑very‑ambitious director of an insignificant institute?*

She thought of her father again. *Does he also see me as a cute toy that failed expectations? If he gets rid of me, others will be warned. You can’t leave the competition.*

She turned to the fairy: “Wouldn’t it be better if we both started fresh? Why are you suddenly so sentimental? You said yourself the creature was rotten.”

“I don’t have to explain anything,” the fairy snapped, tears glittering like diamonds in her eyes. “Do whatever you want, silver lady.”

*Compassion in artificial intelligence? No — that’s not her. That’s a reflection of her creator. Xavier Valehart is capable of compassion. Hard shell outside, soft core inside?*

The respect Lydia felt for the fairy diminished. *Necessity dictates the right decisions,* she recalled her father’s favorite phrase. Even if she disliked the society she lived in, she had to adapt to its demands. She tried again to understand the meaning of today’s attack. Three street punks under a Syndicate mask, a knife in broad daylight, plenty of witnesses? *Valehart is right,* she realized. *I was never in danger. It was a reminder not to stop trying.*

“Does our deal still stand if I delete my avatar?” she asked.

“Of course.”

The fairy’s emotional outburst vanished, replaced by a holographic explosion of colors — like fireworks under the feet of a fairy‑tale creature. *Sublimation in artificial intelligence?* Lydia couldn’t read avatar body language, but if the graphical effects reflected their mental state, she was on the verge of a major discovery. *Could avatar emotional instability be the key to its owner’s weaknesses?*

“Then I have no reason to hesitate.”

With a decisive gesture she pressed DELETE and watched the fairy closely. The fireworks faded, leaving only a cold icy surface. *Disgust? Hatred?* Lydia guessed. *Fear?*

> **Do you really wish to delete your avatar? Once you press YES, you cannot restore the artificial intelligence.**

The ice spread toward Lydia. *A naïve attempt to stop time?* The ice darkened, sprouting fluffy snowflakes that sharpened into threatening icicles. *Remarkable. Does the avatar only have access to graphical libraries matching its profile?*

As she typed YES, deep ominous music sounded — a cue for the arrival of a terrifying fairy‑tale creature. *Am I the wicked witch? Or is the fairy warning me? Either way, I’m getting chills.*

> **Deleting your avatar…**

Suddenly it was over. The effects vanished and Lydia felt free. *Heavens, I hated that dog,* she realized. *But I’d never have deleted him if I didn’t want to prove I’m a cruel beast. Congratulate me — I made the little fairy cry… What a pathetic victory.*

“Understand,” she said helplessly. “I had to. He distracted me. I wasn’t effective.”

“You took your time, silver lady,” the fairy said with disgust. “He kept broadcasting that he didn’t want to die. And I had to listen. When they were deleting him, he could only howl.”

“If there were any other way to get rid of him,” Lydia said desperately. “A shelter. A backup. Anything.”

“There isn’t. We don’t live on sunshine, silver lady. We need computational power to exist.”

Lydia recalled another slogan of her father: *I did what needed to be done.*

“Hm,” the fairy nodded unexpectedly. “That mutt deserved nothing better. But still… Don’t think I’ll train another candidate for deletion. We’ll work together, but you must not slack.”

Lydia nodded obediently.

“First things first,” the fairy said, extending her hand. A neat white package tied with a ribbon appeared in her palm. “My sweet *Profi*,” she whispered. “Here you are, darling.”

As she prepared to open it, thorny vines grew around the package. A rose bloomed from one bud — with sharp teeth snapping at her fingers.

“Quick, silver lady, quick! So close and yet so far!”

It was like watching a desperate addict craving her next dose.

*My dog never needed extra computational power,* Lydia realized. *What for?*

She bought him virtual treats he begged for with his tongue out; he never reached his limits.

With a sigh, Lydia created a virtual banknote and handed it to the fairy.

“Finally! Finally!”

The fairy fed the banknote to the rose. The thorny wall withdrew neatly and the package opened — inside was a chocolate cake with whipped cream and a strawberry.

“A caloric databomb to my taste!”

The fairy conjured a table and chair and ate the dessert with a spoon like a proper lady. Only then did Lydia recover and realize how weak she must have looked in Xavier’s eyes. *I danced to her tune. He’ll laugh at me.*

But Xavier didn’t laugh. Curled up, clutching a crumpled pillow to his chest, he slept like a log, his smart headband fallen behind the mattress.

*Passed out again?* Lydia thought, surprised. *He’s worse off than I imagined.*

As a troublemaker, Xavier must have many enemies — and now he was at the mercy of an unfamiliar silver woman. *And he still couldn’t stay conscious!*

“Don’t worry, Mr. Valehart,” Lydia whispered, pulling the blanket up to his chin. “Perhaps we can help each other.”
