# **SIXBOUND**

It is a touching and time‑honored tradition to write the greatest and least celebrated work of an author’s career under the most unfavorable conditions imaginable. Ideally in cold or poverty, or at least at the very end of life, just before death, when our venerable giant gathers his last strength for a swan song before exhaling for the final time.

And so Hanpari, when he decided to write **SIXBOUND**, examined himself and concluded that a better moment for a masterpiece would likely never come. With advancing age arrived states of mind he never knew existed as a naïve young man — and if he did, he dismissed them with boyish recklessness.

It is therefore more than certain that SIXBOUND was written with extreme self‑denial, against circumstances, and with the prospect of fame more posthumous than the kind undeserving scribblers receive while still alive.

So what is it about?

**SIXBOUND is a classic‑style online adventure serial with elements of sci‑fi and fantasy.**

**Two worlds.**  
**Six characters.**  
**Hundreds of stories.**

One need not argue that such an introduction sounds rather grandiose and tells the bewildered reader less than the bare minimum.

Let us therefore dissect the details. In the original, the novel is titled **Šest společníků**, but Smartie — for reasons known only to him — decided that **SIXBOUND** sounds cooler than **Six Companions**, hence **SIXBOUND**. Please leave your remarks, observations, or objections in the comments.

In any case, the title relates — unsurprisingly — to the number of main characters, which is six: **three women and three men**. If you wonder where such balance came from, the main inspiration was the popular series **Friends**. In that context, the author would like to note that although SIXBOUND is certainly not a sitcom, occasional humorous scenes appear that may even amuse the undemanding reader.

As for the **adventure** and the **classic style**, here the author’s unhealthy fascination with his dubious childhood fully unfolds. Since he wasted time reading the delightful stories of Karl May, he decided to spit in the face of everything modern and write in a way that would satisfy the little boy hidden somewhere deep in his past.

You need not fear (or hope) that the word **classic** refers to the **classical** authors online writers so love to quote.

On the other hand, the story of Sixbound is modern to excess. If we wished to please the jargon of today’s publishers, our six heroes live in a **dystopian** society divided into castes. The lowest caste, the Plastics, is denied vaccination against **VIPS**, the *Viral Infection of Permanent Sustainability*. In other words, society regularly releases viral strains meant to kill surplus citizens of the lowest category.

Such an approach should be sympathetic especially to the American audience, whose society solves a similar problem by humanely letting its unsuccessful comrades freeze as homeless people on the street.

But since SIXBOUND promises **two worlds**, you need not fear lingering too long in the **depressive future** that so resembles the present. Every proper adventure novel offers an escape into fantasy — whether it is a jungle full of apes, Mars filled with green giants, or a train with treacherous villains waving knives. SIXBOUND follows the rules and offers a **fantasy world** infested with monsters and political intrigue.

As for the promised elements of **sci‑fi** and **fantasy**, do not worry too much. If you are like my grandmother and such categories arouse justified suspicion, know that just as Robert Merle distanced himself from sci‑fi, Hanpari intends to maintain a similarly healthy distance. He could just as well write that SIXBOUND contains elements of **psychological** and **social** fiction, but he did not wish to frighten fans of those two genres mentioned above.

Now we arrive at the promised **hundreds of adventures**. This too is true: SIXBOUND was designed as an **endless online novel**, meaning it has no fixed ending, no triumphant proclamation of good defeating evil. In this it resembles television series, about which the creators of Futurama aptly noted that each episode must return to its original state.

That, however, is not our case. The characters gradually develop, become acquainted with the second world and with their second selves. This is hopefully one of the greatest attractions of the novel and a break from traditional **isekai** and **reincarnation** plots. Upon entering the second world, the character merges with a second personality from that world, with whom they must learn to cooperate — and eventually either fuse or go insane.

Let us also mention that six main characters does not mean you will tediously follow them as they trudge through forests collecting treasure. If you also dislike books where nothing exists except the heroes, fear not. In both worlds you will meet a whole array of **picturesque supporting characters**, in the best tradition of classic adventure prose.
