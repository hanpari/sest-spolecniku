# **Can We Talk in Private?**

*I'm a silver-nose at the bottom of the ladder. A failure in my own eyes and my father's. Only a fool would be interested in me.*

Lydia's thoughts were as dark as night.

She couldn't blame Medard for not wanting to return to the Game. Had his reasons not been so ridiculous, she might even have sympathized with him.

*Infidelity. Virtual reality games. What century are we living in?*

Then again, wasn't the same true of her?

*Death in virtual reality? So what? Neither is real...*

What right did she have to lecture Medard?

From the position of a silver citizen?

Whatever she was, she was only that because of her father.

Compared to her, even with all her flaws, Xantippa Miller had worked her way up into the role of Alfred's obedient and efficient assistant. Above her stood the independent agents who had achieved the highest position society would allow them.

Silver citizens like Xavier Valehart never stayed at the top for long, yet nobody could deny they had reached it. Stepping into the ranks of the golds was impossible for them because their lives resembled swimming against a river's current. The moment they relaxed, the merciless flow dragged them backward.

*And Xavier is being swept away,* she realized while listening to her two new companions talk.

No silver-nose worthy of the title should have time to wander city streets under the pretense of protecting a damsel in distress.

*One reason he's escorting me is because he isn't looking for clients anymore. He's already leaving the stage.*

*Unlike me,* she thought bitterly, *he's making his exit with style, while I'm rolling along like a ball of anxiety from nowhere to nowhere. What's the point of despising society when I cling to my status with teeth and nails?*

She found proof of her dependence in her office the moment their trio arrived at the building she had once grandly named the **Institute for Advanced Construction Technologies**.

It wasn't exactly a secret that her father's corporation stood behind the institute, nor that it had been created as a temporary refuge for his academically minded daughter until she found enough motivation to enter the wonderful world of corporations.

*My time is running out as well,* Lydia thought.

From subtle hints to increasingly impatient reminders, Alfred Carver Sr. had been steadily increasing the pressure, perhaps even resorting to extreme measures such as hiring street thugs.

Waiting for her was **Jeremiah Hawthorne**, head of legal affairs at **Asphalt-Transit**, currently the man her father referred to as his right hand.

Calm as still water, Hawthorne had long mastered the unhurried charm of a successful man who no longer needed to pretend to be busy because his importance was self-evident.

Yet the moment she entered, accompanied by Xavier (Medard naturally didn't count), his body language betrayed mild surprise and disapproving shock.

*So he knows who Xavier is. And where I spent the night,* she sighed.

Associating with a troublemaker, regardless of the reason, amounted to declaring treason.

“I assume, Jeremiah,” she said while studying the lawyer carefully, “that Father sent you here after yesterday's events to make sure I'm all right.”

Reading Hawthorne was not easy.

His profession had taught him how to maintain a perfect poker face, and he knew all about her abilities.

This time, however, his carefully maintained silver-tier body betrayed him. The minimal layer of fat beneath his skin could not conceal the tension in his muscles, the subtle tremor of his clenched jaw, or the stiffness in his arms.

“Yes, exactly.”

“Exactly?” Lydia repeated pointedly.

Hawthorne realized he had walked into a trap.

If her father had truly been worried about her, he would have contacted her immediately. Since he hadn't bothered, she could safely assume this wasn't an extraordinary event but a calculated reminder involving an acceptable level of risk.

“His concern is touching,” she said flatly. “Please tell Father that I'm perfectly fine.”

She pointed toward Xavier, who was examining the exhibits displayed inside the glass cases.

“Mr. Valehart took care of me.”

“That's commendable.”

Xavier's indifference irritated Hawthorne. He approached him and extended a hand with a friendly smile.

“It seems, sir, that our **Asphalt-Transit** owes you a debt.”

“Think nothing of it,” Xavier replied casually. “As human beings, it's our duty to help one another.”

The words hung in the air with bizarre awkwardness.

Society had relegated mutual human solidarity to the basements of history.

Individualism remained its highest ideal.

Even a fool could understand what Xavier was implying.

*My values differ from yours.*

Translated:

*We do not share common interests.*

“Of course,” Hawthorne continued, “people sometimes have different motivations. We have to be careful.”

Lydia frowned.

*Is he suggesting Xavier has ulterior motives? And perhaps that I should watch out for him? As if I were a little girl!*

“Personally,” she said, “I'm glad there are still people one can rely on. I expect nothing good from many others.”

“Perhaps it's all just a misunderstanding,” Hawthorne suggested, astonished that she was openly defying her father's authority now that he understood who those *many others* were.

“Some misunderstandings lead to unfortunate consequences,” Lydia shot back. “There are lines we should never cross, especially when knives are involved.”

Translated:

*As far as I'm concerned, you've gone too far.*

Hawthorne wisely changed the subject.

“I heard the attackers were wearing wasp masks.”

“A disguise. I'm quite certain the person who hired them never expected that level of creativity from common street thugs.”

“Unfortunately, the investigation has reached a dead end,” Hawthorne remarked, directing a meaningful glance toward Xavier. “We're cooperating with the police to determine who is responsible.”

*Nicely played. The official version for the rest of the family is: it can't be a coincidence that a troublemaker appeared at the scene.*

“Some incompetent bungler,” Lydia smiled. “Those three idiots were pretending to be Syndicate members. I wouldn't want to be in the employer's shoes. What do you think, Xavier?”

“I wouldn't worry,” Xavier replied. “I'm sure the Syndicate has ways of learning the truth. I've seen similar situations before.”

“Really?”

“Certainly. In my line of work, I have to anticipate everyone's reactions. Whenever possible, the Syndicate operates on the edge of the law, not beyond it. And if they wanted a silver-nose dead, they wouldn't be wearing wasp masks. They don't need that sort of advertisement.”

Hawthorne visibly paled.

To Lydia's surprise, she found his fear oddly satisfying.

More importantly, she'd obtained the alibi she needed.

If her father had ordered the attack, then there was a chance Xavier was not collaborating in the destruction of **Asphalt-Transit**.

*Not that it changes anything,* she realized. *In cases like this, guilt is presumed. Here comes the real blow.*

“I bring bad news,” Hawthorne continued once he recovered. “I'm sorry, Miss Lydia. Can we speak in private?”

“Please, Jeremiah. Why all the secrecy?”

The last thing she wanted was to be alone with him.

Such a conversation would become far more honest and considerably less pleasant.

“Let me guess,” she continued quickly. “You're terminating cooperation with my institute. Shouldn't you be contacting the university instead?”

“That will happen. However, we felt it appropriate to warn you beforehand, Miss Lydia. During the upcoming period the university may wish to make personnel changes.”

*So I'm losing my position,* Lydia realized.

*At last the inevitable has happened. My little game of independence is ending. I have perhaps two months left.*

“I understand,” she sighed. “Well then, Jeremiah, if that's everything, consider me informed.”

Hawthorne blinked.

Perhaps he had expected her usual defense, where she argued that the institute and **her father's** corporation still had much to offer each other.

“I came to discuss your future,” he said with dignity. “As they say, every problem is also an opportunity.”

“I'm all ears.”

“I must repeat that we should discuss these matters privately.”

At that, Medard started toward the door.

Xavier moved in the opposite direction.

A silent gesture of support that she desperately needed.

“What is it?” she repeated calmly. “Ignore Mr. Valehart and Mosswell, Jeremiah.”

“You may consider us personal bodyguards,” Xavier added with a smirk. “Pretend we don't exist, Mr. Hawthorne. Whatever is said, we'll hear nothing... Medard, come back. We never know who might attack our companion or where. Constant vigilance.”

Lydia gave Medard credit for not hesitating.

Despite Hawthorne's disapproving look, he stepped back beside her.

Everything about his body language suggested he'd rather be anywhere else.

Yet he returned.

*The determination to do the right thing regardless of the consequences,* she reflected. *Is that the quality Xavier saw in him?*

“You'll have to forgive me, Jeremiah,” she said. “After yesterday, I dislike being alone.”

“You would be with me.”

“Exactly,” she smiled. “All the more reason.”

“Excuse me? Surely you're not suggesting that I...”

The unfinished sentence carried wounded indignation.

“If someone is targeting our corporation, we cannot rule out the possibility that you're next,” she explained aloud.

*Yes, Jeremiah, that's exactly what I'm suggesting. And I'm glad we understand each other.*

“We're available *gratis*, Mr. Hawthorne,” Xavier winked. “The moment a threat appears, we'll eliminate it. I hate to brag, but that's essentially my job description.”

“I know exactly what your job description is, Mr. Valehart,” Hawthorne snapped. “Surely you understand why a representative of a corporation might feel uncomfortable in your presence.”

“A corporation? **Asphalt-Transit**?” Xavier looked surprised. “All things considered, I suppose that qualifies if we're being generous. No offense, my friend, but most of my clients would classify you as a slightly oversized private company. Infantry in the corporate world.”

Lydia nearly choked.

*Infantry! If Father heard that...*

Still, it was true that the unofficial measure of corporate success was the number of gold citizens an organization could create and support.

**Asphalt-Transit** had only two gold citizens: her father and her stepbrother.

And even her brother had reached gold status with no small amount of luck. Whether he'd maintain it into the next cycle was another question entirely.

**First Savings**, the corporation that had made Xavier's reputation, belonged comfortably to the middle corporate tier and counted nearly a hundred gold citizens among its shareholders, most of them living abroad.

Even so, Lydia found such comparisons painful.

“My father built this company from nothing,” she said defensively. “We've been growing ever since it was founded.”

*We've been growing?* she realized immediately afterward. *Since when do I include myself as part of the ensemble?*

Her slip pleased Hawthorne.

“Yes, we have,” he agreed. “And now another opportunity for growth presents itself. Miss Lydia, I really must insist on speaking privately. This is a legal requirement. What I'm about to discuss concerns the Game. And as you know, information relating to the Game cannot be shared with people who are not Players. Therefore Mr. Valehart and his friend must leave the room.”
