# Sleeping Beauty’s Time Is Up

Dawn was breaking outside the windows when Xavier woke in reality, as rested as he had been yesterday. He got up to make tea when, through the glass doors of the living room, he spotted Lydia stretched out on the couch, auburn hair spilled across the cushion and her NT transmitter blinking by the table leg.

Xavier sighed, grabbed a blanket from the bed, and pushed at the door. When he discovered it was locked, he went back for his smart headband.

“Good morning,” the little fairy greeted him. She was puffing on a periwinkle-green pipe and leafing through something that resembled a children's fairy-tale book. Before her knelt a hooded figure dressed in black, massaging her feet in the respectful pose of a devoted servant.

“Am I interrupting?” Xavier asked acidly. “Whenever you have a moment, perhaps you could answer a few questions.”

“Certainly. Ask away, sir. I am here to serve.”

Xavier gave her his finest icy stare, but with little effect.

“Before speech returns to you,” the fairy smirked, pointing at the hooded figure, “allow me to introduce Shadowstep. I am his *sensei*, teacher, and spiritual guide. Lydia pays me to train him.”

“A pleasure,” Xavier growled.

Shadowstep stood, turned, and bowed with dignity.

“Thank you for taking care of my lady, sir. I am prepared to kill anyone you point at. I am the one who walks the shadows.”

Xavier froze for a long minute, then silently pointed at the little fairy.

Even beneath the black hood, it was obvious Shadowstep shuddered.

“Not her, please.”

“I hardly deserve that,” the fairy protested. “If it weren't for me, Lydia would have run away and we would have lost a potential teammate. I exploited her emotional vulnerability,” she added proudly. “She appears to crave human company, even if that company happens to be you, sir. Aren't you proud of me?”

“So I should thank you for occupying my living room?”

“Should I have sent her to sleep on the doorstep?” the fairy snapped. “In a way, I applaud you, sir. You're equally rude to your own species. No discrimination, right?”

Xavier sighed and pointed toward the glass doors.

“Open it. I don't understand how she could be naïve enough to think I couldn't bypass the lock in my own apartment.”

Under Shadowstep's watchful gaze, he entered and draped the blanket over the sleeping woman.

“A touching gesture,” the fairy noted. “She did the same for you. Is this some sophisticated way of informing her that you're not interested?”

“I think I met Miss Carver in the Game,” Xavier said indifferently. “It'll be safer if I keep an eye on her. We've landed ourselves in quite a mess.”

“What happened?” both avatars asked at once.

“A mammoth larger than a three-story house,” Xavier replied.

But he continued the conversation silently through his thoughts, accessible only to the little fairy, excluding Shadowstep.

He could still feel the tremor of the ground as *that megacreature* approached them. Even miles away, its presence had been overwhelming, like an earthquake growing stronger and stronger, tearing the earth apart beneath their feet.

It had been difficult to resist the overwhelming urge to drop everything and run.

Yet he still vaguely remembered racing through the flying ship, stumbling over shattered glass and stuffing sacks and pockets with anything that looked valuable.

The maid Foxling, whom he believed to be Lydia, confused him. One moment she stood frozen by fear, and the next she transformed into someone entirely different, eagerly leading him to a small ship's safe that, though locked, fit neatly into Xavier's multidimensional storage once they pried it out of the wall.

When they finally abandoned the vessel, they saw the shaggy head of the *megamammoth* rising above the treetops. It forced its way through ancient beeches and oaks, trunk raised high, bellowing in furious outrage at having been disturbed from its peaceful rest.

Meanwhile Boss and his group had dragged the surviving members of the Squares a hundred meters from the wreck. Jolana had put them all to sleep with her staff, and they wanted to give them at least a slim chance of surviving the catastrophe to come.

Even though everyone knew it was pure hypocrisy, nobody suggested simply finishing the Squares off.

Then they ran Away from the wreck and from the beast. Alongside dozens of animals and monsters that had forgotten all hostility and fled together from the gigantic creature.

Its roar carried for miles. Eventually it wrapped the world in a deafening wall of sound, bursting eardrums and leaving people trapped inside a trembling prison of noise.

Jolana was the first to fall. She threw away her precious staff and crawled along the forest path, paralyzed by terror. Boss and Mute tried desperately to haul her back onto her feet before collapsing to their knees themselves.

Xavier shouted helplessly at Jolana before scooping up her staff and hiding it in his multidimensional storage.

At one point he fell, and the maid Foxling helped him back up, screaming something at him while leaves and broken branches rained down from shaking trees.

***

“Quite exciting, wasn't it?” the little fairy commented as Xavier's memories flooded back. “What adventures I've missed by being born a stupid avatar,” she added without the slightest trace of jealousy.

“And what about my lady?” Shadowstep asked anxiously.

“She’s fine,” Xavier replied, though not very convincingly.

When they finally reached a safe cave, Foxling had worn a glassy stare and trembled like a leaf.

*If that maid really is Lydia Carver, I don't believe she'll want to return to the Game,* he thought gloomily.

Personally, he rather enjoyed the adrenaline rush. Or at least he preferred it to the creeping dangers of **First Savings**.

“When do you think she'll wake up?” he asked.

“If we judge by your own experience, then time spent in the Game equals twelve hours. My lady fell asleep one hour and thirty-four minutes after you did, sir,” Shadowstep reported dutifully.

The little fairy confirmed his estimate with a nod.

“Twelve hours? A player is gone for twelve hours?” Xavier asked.

“Seems so,” the fairy replied. “Technically I could disconnect the NT transmitter if I wanted to pull you out. But I'd classify that as an emergency measure.”

“Why?”

The fairy conjured a projection screen. On it appeared the idiotic grin of a mentally impaired child. It was footage from a popular streaming platform, under the title:

> **Try Not to Laugh! Sixteen Hilarious Morons! Like and Share!**

“Would you like to add adorable drooling and incoherent babbling to your playful biting?” the fairy asked sweetly.

“Disconnecting the NT transmitter may cook your brain, sir,” Shadowstep added helpfully.

“I doubt we'd notice the difference,” the fairy cut him off. “You worry about your owner. Sleeping Beauty’s time is up.”

And indeed, Lydia was waking. She sat up, stretching stiff limbs while looking around in confusion.

Xavier watched her, hoping that like him, she would regard the events inside the Game as little more than a fading dream.

“I’m back,” Lydia said.

Then she began to shake.

*As expected,* Xavier thought. *She won’t be returning to the Game. If a silver citizen reacts like this, what chance do Medard and those kids have?*

“Mr. Valehart, I owe you an apology.”

He looked at her in surprise.

“An apology?”

“I behaved badly,” Lydia explained. “You saved my life and I—”

“Ah, *that* apology,” Xavier laughed. “In general, I don't think highly of trusting fools. You have nothing to apologize for. How are you feeling, hm, Foxling? That was you, wasn't it?”

“Lizard?” she looked at him without returning the smile. “You're mistaken. I’m not Foxling.”

“What? Then how—?”

If Lydia wasn't Foxling, how did she know about Lizard?

“Foxling is the other one,” Lydia explained. “There were two of us the entire time. It felt as though I'd occupied someone else's body. Please be careful around her,” she said urgently. “Foxling is dangerous.”

“I noticed,” Xavier said dryly. “You surprised me more than once.”

“Not me,” Lydia replied bitterly. “The other one perhaps. I felt like dead weight tied to her ankle.”

She stood and walked into the bathroom, brushing her hair with a brush retrieved from her damaged briefcase.

The ease with which she navigated his apartment irritated Xavier. So did the strands of hair drifting down onto the dark carpet, leaving a red trail behind her path.

*An unwanted intruder.*

“Would you like breakfast?” he asked, deciding not to press the issue.

“Thank you. I’ll have to go to work,” she sighed while eating scrambled eggs. “Strangely enough, after everything that happened, nothing has changed. Yesterday I swore I wouldn't remain the same person, that I would change. And now I'm sitting here wondering how I'll explain that somebody stabbed the contracts I was supposed to sign.”

“Mm.” Xavier nodded indifferently. “Could you pass the milk?”

If Lydia intended to drag him into a discussion of her emotional state, he wasn't interested.

*Sure,* he thought, *I could listen, gain her trust, create an emotional bond and pull her deeper into the Game. But it's better if we part as strangers. At least there'll be fewer hairs on the floor.*

Even he was surprised by how much the subtle traces of her presence annoyed him.

A perfume scent lingered in the air.

Even the glasses — three glasses! — had somehow been rearranged into an order that disturbed him.

Lydia watched him, amusement mingling with anxiety in her fox-green eyes.

“What I'm about to ask won’t be easy,” she said suddenly. “I’m sorry, but do you think I could stay here for a few days?”

“What? Why?”

“I never imagined a man would react like that when I offered him this,” she replied with an irritated smile.

“I’m not accustomed to company,” Xavier explained.

*Why did she ask?* he wondered. *She reads body language. She must understand perfectly well how little I want her here.*

“I’m willing to pay,” she sighed. “As for the secrets of **Asphalt-Transit**, they're all yours.”

“Nice try,” he smirked. “But I don't think you're prepared to betray the family business, even if I were interested. Don't bother. You can stay here without conditions.”

Now it was her turn to look shocked.

“What? Why?” she asked suspiciously. “I make you uncomfortable. You squirm every time you look at me.”

Perhaps she had expected negotiation. Persuasion. Blackmail, even.

Whether involving her father’s corporation or her participation in the Game.

“The fact that you have no conditions scares me,” she admitted at last. Then added thoughtfully, “If you don't want my father's corporation, then you want me in the Game. Or...” She choked slightly and blushed. “You simply want me?”

“Want you?”

If Lydia was suggesting that possibility, what did it mean?

*An attractive young woman,* Xavier considered. *Magazine-cover figure, flawless skin, excellent physical condition, intelligent, suspicious enough to be interesting, and with an appealing family background. The ideal wife for a silver-nose like me. Similar age, similar status, perhaps even similar personality.*

He didn't believe in romantic attachments.

And if one ever appeared, he'd do everything in his power to avoid it.

Lydia attracted him as a woman.

But not like Gabrielle Marrow, whose presence stirred him in ways he found difficult to explain.

“You inconvenience me,” he admitted.

Out of the corner of his eye he noticed the little fairy, sitting beside Shadowstep and enthusiastically observing the conversation, tapping a finger against her temple.

“But it's a personal inconvenience I can overcome.”

“Someone kill me.”

Now seated inside a virtual movie theater with popcorn and raspberry lemonade, the little fairy burst into laughter and gave them a thumbs-up.

Lydia, much less amused and apparently still undecided, copied the gesture anyway.

“Thank you, Xavier,” she said softly, addressing him by his first name.

At that exact moment, the doorbell rang.

Xavier frowned and ordered the little fairy to access the exterior camera.

“Expecting company?” Lydia asked.

“Never!” he answered, unsettled by how naturally she had switched to first names. “I never expect visitors.”
