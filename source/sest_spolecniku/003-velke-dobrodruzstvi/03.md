# **That Girl Looks Familiar**

As they pushed through the forest — adorable only in name — Xavier tried in vain to decipher whether the path to the crashed ship came from his human or his lizard memory. If it was the former, it would explain how he got here: perhaps he was the only passenger who survived, and his Game storyline included amnesia caused by the impact. That would be a perfectly ordinary way for a virtual game to begin.

If it came from the lizard’s memory, then the question arose: how would a lizard even recognize a **flying ship**? The lizard relied on primitive intelligence, while the concept of a flying vessel had to lie far beyond animal comprehension. Even Xavier himself struggled to accept the existence of a flying ship in a world that otherwise obeyed natural physical laws. Yet from his *memories* he knew he would encounter a crashed sailing vessel that had fallen from the sky.

*I’d really prefer to know where I stand,* he thought. Reality and fantasy blurred inside the Game. When Xavier scratched himself on a thorny bush, he felt pain — but then he watched the reddened wound heal, warmed by the inner flame.

*Whatever Jolana says, if this isn’t magic, I’ll eat my filthy socks.*

The inconsistency of the Game frightened him. He understood all too well that if he had entered a World ruled by pseudo‑scientific jargon and inexplicable forces, he had stepped into a nightmare where nothing would make sense.

Yet no virtual reality he had ever experienced could simulate reality the way the Game did. In a way, the Game was more real than reality itself, because through the inner flame he could perceive more — and perceive it more precisely.

“Is it still far, honored sir?” Jolana asked after four hours of marching along animal trails, through tall grass, fallen trees — and especially through a parade of hostile creatures, from *gloom lizards* to peculiar *dwarf bears*.

Their group rested at a spot where they had been attacked by a pack of barking predators, which Commander classified as *forest hounds*. Aggressive creatures the size of collies, but resembling oversized foxes. Jolana explained that they communicated during hunts through weak telepathy.

*Telepathy? Psychotronic flashes?* Xavier mentally threw up his hands and swore he would not rely on common sense here.

“Our goal isn’t far,” he said as he accepted his share of memory crystals and red stones from Boss. “But at our pace, we’ll get there in a few hours.”

“A few hours of hell,” Jolana groaned. “I need a boost.”

She pulled a small red stone from her pouch and crushed it into powder in a tiny mortar; she mixed it into her flask with an unknown white substance that looked like powdered sugar. After shaking the flask, she took a cautious sip and grabbed her stomach.

“What is that?” Xavier asked. “I thought red stones were harmful to humans.”

“A desperate solution,” she said. “Don’t worry. I added an inhibitor that prevents the energy from absorbing too quickly. Want some?”

*An inhibitor? Why not dried salamander legs or powdered bat wings?* Xavier smirked inwardly.

He took the flask and drank. His inner flame flared, burning away the fatigue in his aching muscles.

“Excellent medicine,” he exhaled.

“It doesn’t hurt you?” Jolana asked, astonished. “It burns me like the devil. We were taught that the final potion needs a cooling agent and analgesics to make it bearable.”

“Really?”

Xavier offered the flask to Mute and watched his reaction to make sure Jolana wasn’t simply oversensitive. But even Mute, stoic as he was, couldn’t tolerate the drink any better.

“You must have a stomach of steel, honored sir,” Commander said admiringly. “This brew would knock out an elephant.”

*Or you just need a lizard’s stomach,* Xavier thought. He had already noticed that there were clear differences between him and the rangers — differences that couldn’t be explained by better equipment alone. *Sometimes I feel like a different species.*

“Shall we continue, honored sir?” Commander asked hesitantly.

This deep in the *Forest of Adorable Owlets*, the three rangers had no chance of survival. Only thanks to Xavier — his lizard senses and inherited memory — had they avoided several dangerous situations.

“We continue.”

Xavier had no reason to stop. They hadn’t encountered anything that could threaten him, so he assessed his chances differently than the rangers.

They marched on. As they approached the place where they hoped to find the flying ship, Xavier ordered another break. After several skirmishes with forest predators, Jolana was barely standing, leaning on her staff like a war invalid. This time Boss didn’t scold her — instead he greedily drank from the flask with crushed red stone.

“Forgive us, honored sir,” he muttered. “We’re only slowing you down.”

*In other words,* Xavier thought, *they want to know whether I intend to abandon their crew. They probably don’t understand why I’m wasting time with them.*

“I’m tired too,” he lied. “But we’re almost there. In any case,” he added sternly, “we came here together and we leave together.”

*No, I’m not a heartless monster who’ll leave you to die,* he added silently. It unsettled him that the rangers expected only the worst — and it frightened him how society in the Game must function. *Outside or inside, I’m not sure I’ll ever catch a break. Why did I expect? A peaceful utopia?*

“Thank you, honored sir,” Commander murmured. “You can rely on us.”

*Should I take that as an oath of loyalty?*

“Very well,” Xavier said aloud. “Boss, do you think we might run into your competition?”

“Hard to say, honored sir. No guild dares come this deep. If I were in their place, I’d have turned back long ago.”

At that moment, as if to contradict his words, distant shouting and explosions echoed through the forest. A flash lit the sky, followed by a pillar of blue light.

“Down,” Xavier ordered, dropping to the ground and rolling under the trunk of a thick oak.

“Competition?” he whispered.

“No, honored sir. Our guilds don’t have equipment like that.”

“What was it?”

“The explosions? Probably energy grenades. And the rest?” Commander shrugged. “No idea. Nothing I recognize.”

“In any case, we’re close,” Xavier nodded. “We move forward carefully. Stay covered and keep ten‑meter spacing so you can see the person ahead. I go first, then Mute, Jolana, and Commander last. If anything goes wrong, we retreat immediately. Check your wings.”

“Wings?” Jolana whispered, confused.

“Left and right flank,” Xavier clarified. “Boss covers our retreat.”

He then eyed the men’s bows skeptically. In dense forest, their effectiveness was limited.

…

Among the uprooted trees lay the fallen flying ship. It looked like a luxurious sailing vessel, richly decorated with carvings and enormous crystals — the embodiment of a painter’s wild imagination unconcerned with physics. None of its three masts had survived the fall, and a black hole gaped in its starboard side as if bitten out by a giant beast.

Beneath the wreck crouched a soldier in uniform and a woman dressed in clothes that resembled a maid’s outfit. The soldier bled from several wounds, an arrow protruding from his side, yet he tirelessly fired two crossbows, which the maid clumsily reloaded using a pulley mechanism.

The clearing before them was scorched by explosions, and among the charred branches lay the clothes and weapons of attackers who had likely died there, judging by the scattered red stones and memory crystals.

The attackers, not unlike Boss’s group of rangers, hid behind trees, out of grenade range, firing arrow after arrow while some tried to circle around the defenders.

“Stop!” shouted one of the attackers — a barrel‑chested man with a rough, unshaven face. “What are you doing? We’re from the **Squares** guild and we came to help you. We have no hostile intentions.”

“And I repeat that we don’t need help,” the soldier yelled back. “I told you to get lost, that our reinforcements are already on the way. We warned you not to approach.”

“You killed five of our people!”

“Exactly as promised,” the soldier growled darkly. “This flying ship belongs to a highly ranked individual. Rabble like you have no business near it.”

“Without us you won’t survive the forest, friend. How about we make a deal? I’m Vice‑Master Brock Prudell, and I’m known as a man who keeps his word.”

“There’s nothing to negotiate. You reach this ship only over my dead body.”

Xavier, watching the scene from beneath thick bushes, turned to Mute. “The **Squares** guild?” he whispered. “Good guys?”

Mute smirked and pointed his thumb downward. Then, perhaps thinking the gesture wasn’t clear enough, he slid his thumb across his throat.

*Cutthroats,* Xavier concluded. *Likely the competition Boss mentioned.*

Even a fool could see Vice‑Master Brock Prudell was stalling for time, waiting to strike the soldier from behind.

Xavier suddenly didn’t know how to proceed. Even if he sided with the defenders, he would have to kill the **Squares** — and then eliminate the soldier and the maid to loot the flying ship without witnesses. He had no illusions: neither Boss nor the **Squares** were here to rescue passengers. Everyone wanted the treasures aboard — and even a blind man could see there would be plenty.

*The soldier spoke of a highly ranked person but didn’t mention a name. Why?* Xavier wondered. *Was the ship on a secret mission?*

In any case, he had no intention of playing assassin or bandit. Fighting beasts was one thing — killing humans, whether in the Game or anywhere else, was another.

*The easiest thing would be to wait and see how it plays out,* he thought. *Then deal with the **Squares**.*

He had no doubt which side would win. But when he saw the soldier’s brave resistance — and especially the trembling maid, terrified out of her mind — he couldn’t bring himself to simply watch.

*That girl looks familiar,* he realized. At one moment the maid grabbed a shield lying on the ground and deflected an arrow aimed at the soldier. Something in her movements reminded Xavier of Lydia — of how she had defended herself with her leather briefcase against knife‑wielding thugs.

*Could it be her?* he wondered. *Didn’t she say she hadn’t logged into the Game yet? Maybe I’m just looking for excuses to save them.*

But it was already too late. One of the **Squares** got behind the pair and without hesitation sent a long arrow straight into the soldier’s heart. The soldier turned in surprise, raised his loaded crossbow, and in his final moment shot the ranger in the leg. Then he collapsed and transformed into a pile of equipment, red stones, and crystals.

*No, that’s not Lydia,* Xavier thought as he watched the maid turn, leap forward with several unbelievable jumps, close in on the wounded **Square**, and fling something into his face with a swift motion. The man screamed — and screamed until the maid silenced him forever.

The scene chilled Xavier. *Why did I think she was Lydia?* he wondered. *Was I wrong about her?*

He had never considered Lydia Carver dangerous *per se*. If he feared anything, it was her knowledge of his conflict with **First Savings**, not that she would smother him with a pillow in his sleep.

*But she does behave strangely,* he thought. As soon as the maid finished her attack, she didn’t continue — she froze oddly over the body that was turning into red stone and a memory crystal. The **Squares** showered her with arrows — and only the pain of a hit to her arm forced her to seek cover among the trees.
