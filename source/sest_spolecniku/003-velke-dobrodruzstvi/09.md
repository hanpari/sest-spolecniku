# Should I Behave Like an Adulterer?

After a long and exhausting meeting filled with vague hints, veiled threats, and carefully crafted incentives aimed primarily at Lydia, Xavier and Medard left both the institute and the university grounds none the wiser than they had been that morning.

Xavier walked away with the impression that **Asphalt Travel** had vastly overpaid its informants, or that Hawthorne was remarkably skilled at pretending ignorance.

Whenever he spoke of exploiting the Game, the lawyer seemed to have no idea what was meant to be exploited. Whenever he spoke of selling, he appeared to have no idea what was to be sold. Whenever he spoke of advancement and development, he sounded as though he had no clue where anyone was supposed to advance or develop.

*I've never met a man capable of saying so little in so many words,* Xavier reflected. *And I've never met anyone more terrified.*

Hawthorne's fear seemed to grow with every sentence. Careful never to reveal anything specific, he gave the impression of someone haunted by a creeping specter that grew larger every time the Game was mentioned.

*We learned nothing we didn't already know,* he thought at the tiny fairy floating beside his head, seated cross-legged with a hookah in her lap, the very embodiment of comfort and leisure.

"Not quite," she corrected wearily. "He hinted at plenty. But following your train of thought is exhausting work. I'm amazed you still remember to breathe while you're using your brain. I bet I can prove my intellectual superiority within five minutes."

*Naturally,* Xavier sighed. *First question. What did Hawthorne mean by exploitation?*

"Memory crystals," the fairy shot back. "You could have figured that out yourself. What else would you be mining if not information?"

*Virtual materials or items?* he suggested.

"And what use would those be? Remember, this isn't a virtual world populated by millions of paying customers. Participation in the Game is minimal. You can't build an economic model around that. Besides, no interface exists that would let you trade any of it."

*Fair enough,* Xavier conceded. *But I can't exactly bring memory crystals into our world.*

"Are you sure about that?" the fairy asked smugly as she pulled a holographic display in front of herself. "Then what would you call this?"

It was the online store of the **Game Centre**, containing exactly three products.

"Well, there goes your intellectual superiority," Xavier muttered. "You cheated."

"What?" Medard asked, walking silently beside him, lost in his own thoughts.

"Nothing, nothing," Xavier waved him off, grateful that the bearded man used no device capable of perceiving the ever-present avatars. "Just a little disagreement with an artificial intelligence."

"Really? My children keep telling me I should buy one."

"If you feel your life doesn't contain enough problems already, go right ahead," Xavier replied sourly. "Now excuse me, would you? I haven't finished with that talkative fool yet."

"That was mean," the fairy protested.

*But honest,* Xavier assured her. *So your brilliant deduction comes from browsing the internet? In that case, perhaps you'll tell me how to transfer a memory crystal onto a data disk?*

"When you're as clever as you claim to be, I'm sure you'll figure it out."

*So you don't know. As expected. Any other brilliant observations?*

The only response he received was offended silence, from which Xavier concluded that his miniature adviser had exhausted her expertise.

*There we have it. A genius straight out of remedial school.*

"Excellent," he sighed aloud. "Then experimentation it is. Could you contact our genderless courier, number 143, I think, and order three small data disks? Let's not risk the medium-sized ones. As for the large model that costs a million, let's not even discuss it."

"As you wish, O Forgetful One," the fairy replied triumphantly. "Your courier is number 443, for your information. Where would you be without me? Calling *me* the remedial-school genius!"

She never got to savor her victory.

Medard stopped abruptly and asked in alarm, "A million? What exactly are we talking about, Xavier? I don't have investment capital. But I can return the money you lent me."

"I gave you that money, I didn't lend it," Xavier corrected him. "We're talking about memory crystals and how to transfer them into our world. Other than spending time with your... ahem... second wife, did you discover anything useful?"

"For heaven's sake, don't put it like that. If Laura heard you, she might think I'm a *bigamist*."

Xavier remembered his brief conversation with Laura and her eager appetite for anything remotely exciting.

*Perhaps she'd find the existence of a virtual rival amusing,* he thought, though he wisely kept the observation to himself.

"Baroness Sylvie is a highly educated woman," Medard continued quickly. "You mustn't imagine anything improper."

"Baroness?"

"Did I forget to mention that? It appears I've recently been promoted to Baron of Mosswell Hill. Baroness Sylvie, that is to say my, well, virtual wife, seemed quite pleased by my achievement. Which is why we spent the morning in a... *tête-à-tête*. What exactly does that mean, Xavier?"

"*Tête-à-tête*? A private conversation between two people."

"Oh." Medard visibly relaxed, reassured that Lydia had not implied anything more scandalous. "After we finished talking, the Baroness left to continue her research, while I was tasked with supervising work on our... Well, promise you won't laugh?"

"Go on."

"I somehow ended up becoming the lord of a castle. With my promotion, I was put in charge of the estate and the surrounding lands. Most of my time was spent reading contracts and development plans."

"Congratulations," Xavier remarked dryly, noticing the tenderness with which the bearded man recalled his newfound status.

It was astonishing how the Game, so ruthless toward him and Lydia, seemed determined to pamper Medard.

*This idyll won't last,* he thought with uneasy certainty.

"You said the Baroness is conducting research. Research into what?"

"It sounded like wizardry to me. When I said as much, she laughed and told me that I was the muscles while she was the brains."

"I already like that woman," the fairy commented.

"And what qualifies as wizardry in your book?" Xavier asked patiently.

"I'll try to quote her. She spoke about genotype reformatting and the influence of genetic structure on Inner Flame transformation. Then she rather pointedly observed that she'd already explained it to me ten times and asked whether I'd at least grasped the first part."

*I understand that woman completely!* the fairy exclaimed, directing a meaningful look at Xavier. "A fellow sufferer."

*Is the Game about genetic engineering?* Xavier wondered. *But the Inner Flame? What possible value could information about Game-related genetics have in our world?*

"Perhaps you should ask this fool how Baroness Sylvie defines the Inner Flame," the fairy suggested. "She can hardly expect reading technical encyclopedias to be one of her beloved bigamist's hobbies."

*Good point,* Xavier admitted and repeated the question aloud.

"The Inner Flame? You mean that hot thing people feel beneath their ribs?" Medard asked.

"I'm relieved you know what it is," Xavier replied.

According to Jolana, every living creature possessed an Inner Flame. But if Xavier had understood correctly, it had to be cultivated and nourished before it became useful, and such cultivation required substantial resources. Not everyone could afford it.

"I'm sorry, but I don't know," Medard admitted with the tone of a student who had disappointed his favorite teacher. "I'm sorry."

"From now on," Xavier said sternly, "pay attention to every little detail. Anything related to memory crystals or the Inner Flame, memorize it. Even if you don't understand a word of it."

"I understand."

"The moment you wake up, you'll write everything down. Encrypt the files. Also, lock your phone so your wife can't access it."

"But—"

"I'm not finished. Do you call your wife by her name?"

"Of course."

"Then get used to other forms of address. Darling, sweetheart, flower, whatever comes naturally. Just not her name."

"But why?"

"I'm no expert on marriage, but I doubt any wife would appreciate being addressed by the name of the other one."

Medard came to an abrupt halt.

"Am I supposed to behave like an adulterer?"

"You'll behave like a responsible associate who respects corporate confidentiality."

"The part about phones and encryption doesn't bother me," Medard sighed. "It's the part about the names that worries me."

*Would you look at that,* Xavier thought approvingly. *This morning we were dealing with a moral dilemma, and now it's apparently resolved. The poor fellow hasn't even realized he's silently agreed to return to his Game alter ego.*

"Handle it however you see fit," he said with a shrug. "The Baroness is far too valuable for us to lose because of a stupid slip of the tongue."

"But what about Laura?"

"At least you'll provide her with some entertainment. What can she possibly suspect when you fall asleep beside her like a log? Didn't you say she'd been bored lately?"

To end the conversation before it drifted into dangerous territory, Xavier tried once more to call the number he'd obtained from Jacob Lickboothe.

It was his third attempt.

Svetlana had firmly rejected both previous calls, though fortunately she had not blocked the unfamiliar number.

"I should have asked for her address too," Xavier growled irritably. "Stubborn girl. Isn't she even curious who's calling?"

At that very moment, the student answered.

"Not in the slightest," came a furious voice from the speaker. "Listen, whoever you are, I have no idea how you got my number, and I'm not interested. Stop calling me!"

"Svetlana, this is Xavier Valehart. Do you remember me?"

A long silence followed.

In the background, Xavier could hear shouting and angry voices.

"One moment," Svetlana said in a muted voice. "I'm trying to find somewhere quieter. Whatever is happening right now, you've got your fingerprints all over it. The devil must have owed us something when we crossed paths with you."
