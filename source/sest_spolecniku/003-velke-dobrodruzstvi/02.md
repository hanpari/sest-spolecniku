# **Mother Most Merciful!**

> *They mistakenly believe that reality is real.*  
> — *Mijena Adloff*

When Xavier woke again in the treetop, tied into the crown in his newly enhanced body, panic hit him. *Not now. Not now!*

Lydia Carver was still in his apartment, suspecting him of preparing an attack on her father’s corporation. If she decided to harm him in any way, he had no means of defending himself. All she had to do was contact **First Savings** and let their specialists into his flat.

*At least I know what knocked me out,* he thought bitterly. The **Game Center**’s *TN‑transmitter* had stayed active, and the dazed state he had mistaken for a drug effect was actually the beginning of the connection between him and the World.

Just like yesterday, he tried in vain to log out of the Game.

*Next time I need to be more careful — if there is a next time!* he added silently. *I have to hope young Carver understands the meaning of gratitude… Heaven help me, I do not want to go back there!*

Euphoria — that blissful awakening in a young, rested body — pushed him to move. The lizard stirred, the suppressed part of his mind that longed to chase and hunt.

Dawn was breaking in the World, its light filtering through the dense leaves of the *Forest of Adorable Owlets*. This time nature didn’t frighten him; on the contrary, he looked forward to exploring it further.

*I need to find Jolana and the others,* he planned. The three rangers were his only source of information here. *Medard hasn’t contacted me. I didn’t manage to call Svetlana. I can only hope they’re safe — and meanwhile learn what I can.*

He hurried toward the cave where Jolana’s group had planned to sleep. On the way he experimented with his *inner flame*. In his body it functioned like a new organ — a shapeless extension of human senses. Through it he could connect to his fang, manipulate multidimensional space, and control *magical* items.

When he linked with his camouflage suit, he could change its color at will, from bright white to wild rainbow patterns. More than that — using the flame and the suit, he could dampen the sound of his steps, so even though he constantly stepped on dry twigs and leaves, he moved through the forest like a ghost.

On the other hand, controlling the *inner flame* required deep, constant focus. While he could change the suit’s color once and forget it, using its advanced abilities like **Silence** or **Chameleon** demanded his ongoing attention.

*Chameleon? Silence?* he rolled the words on his tongue, amazed at how easily he found those names. He still felt the presence of both man and lizard within himself — and he was certain those names came from his foreign human half. *Did I gain not only the Game body but also the memory of the character I became? If so, why don’t I remember more? Could eating the lizard’s crystal have overwritten part of my memories? That woman, Jolana, looked terrified when she realized what I’d done.*

Meanwhile, the three rangers in the nearby cave were packing up their camp. While the men — whom Xavier mentally labeled Boss and Mute — worked, Jolana sat on a round stone, staff in hand, praying softly.

“Mother most merciful, your unworthy daughter longs for your presence.”

Then she struck the ground with her staff and bowed deeply.

*Strange,* Xavier thought, invisible and silent in his suit. *Yesterday she laughed at me for thinking her staff was a magical item — and now she prays like a medieval peasant.*

Logically, if the staff was a technologically advanced product, no rituals were needed. People in Xavier’s world didn’t bow to their computers before logging into the network.

“Are you done, Jolana?” Boss asked. “We need to get out of here before that man shows up.”

“I know. But my morning ritual is important. We can’t afford to skip connecting with my goddess.”

*Connecting with a goddess?* Xavier nearly burst out laughing at the thought of stubborn believers calling their prayers that. *Is this a rationalization of faith?*

“If you’d gotten up when we woke you, we’d be long gone,” Boss grumbled.

“Hm.” Jolana combed her thick brown hair with a bone brush, leaning over the cold ashes of the fire, a clasp between her teeth.

“Why are we running from him?” she asked when she finished.

“We’re not running,” Boss corrected quickly. “We have duties and can’t afford to wait for him. Move it.”

The three rangers, lacking the comfort of multidimensional storage, carried their gear on their backs. When they lined up to leave, Xavier stepped out of the shadows.

“Leaving somewhere?” he asked quietly.

The woman screamed in terror and jumped a meter into the air. Her companions stared at Xavier as if he were a monster.

“Y-you’re already here, honored sir,” Boss stammered. Mute, naturally a phlegmatic, bent down to pick up a fallen piece of bread, dusted it off, and began chewing with an apologetic smile.

“I’m here,” Xavier said calmly. “It would be unpleasant if we missed each other.”

*Unpleasant **for you**,* his cold tone implied.

“You see, honored sir, we would gladly wait, but our duties… We had to hurry.”

“Of course,” Xavier said. “We all have duties. Understandable.”

*Though mine are more important than yours.*

He realized he was slipping back into the role of an arrogant silver‑nose, encouraged by the trio’s submissive posture.

“Tell me why you’re here,” he suggested instead. “Perhaps we can help each other.”

“Nothing important, honored sir. Nothing important,” Boss blurted, then realized he contradicted himself.

“You were in quite a hurry,” Xavier smirked. “Has something changed?”

“I mean nothing important that would interest you. Just a routine expedition our guild sent us on.”

*Expedition? Guild? This is starting to look like a virtual game,* Xavier thought.

“Are you gaining experience?” he asked to confirm his suspicion.

“Experience? You jest, honored sir? With all respect, we have experience to spare,” Jolana puffed up. Then corrected herself: “My friends outmatch even rangers from the great guilds. And I am nearly a consecrated healer.”

“Then why stay in your guild? Aren’t you stepping on your own luck?”

Xavier deduced that guilds differed in prestige and that joining a larger guild meant higher status. *Maybe they think I’m a member of such an organization.*

“We…”

“Quiet, Jolana,” Boss cut her off and gestured awkwardly at their gear. “You’re right, honored sir. We’d do better in a great guild. But ours isn’t bad — we just had bad luck.”

“Go on.”

“Our guild is a joint venture. We have shares in it. We founded it two years ago and did well. But then competition appeared and crushed us. They bought out many of our members and we fell to the bottom. That’s why we have to work even with people like Jolana.”

“Hey, Boss!”

“Nothing against you, girl. But you don’t have much experience and in the forest you’re asking for trouble.”

*No names, no details,* Xavier noted. *Just vague information. They’re hiding something.* He liked Boss’s secretive approach. *On the other hand — getting involved in a shady venture?*

He imagined the system asking whether he wanted to continue the expedition with the suspicious trio or leave them. Unfortunately, the Game offered no prompts — he had to rely on his own judgment.

*I saved their lives. They should trust me at least a little.*

“You won’t survive here alone,” he said finally, ambiguously. *Either with me — or prepare for a bad end.*

Boss understood the hint. “We’d be very grateful if you joined us, honored sir,” he muttered without enthusiasm. “But you’ll waste your time with us. We don’t have much hope of success.”

“Why?”

“We’re verifying information someone sold us. It’s probably nonsense.”

“Nonsense that almost got you killed? You must be desperate.”

“We are. To someone of your standing our efforts must seem laughable.”

*What standing do you think I have?* Xavier didn’t ask — he only nodded wisely.

“Still, things could be worse,” Boss continued thoughtfully.

“But?”

“It might turn out the information was true.”

*Shouldn’t that be a good thing?* Xavier wondered. *If the guild bought a true report, it should be worth what they paid. Why would that trouble him? Unless…*

“You’re not alone here?”

“Exactly,” Boss nodded. “I can’t imagine our competition doesn’t know.”

“Yet you’re here.”

Boss shrugged. “We must hope for the best.”

*They hope for the best but prepare for the worst,* Xavier realized. *Mute knows it too. Only Jolana doesn’t understand she’s in a suicide squad.*

“When you meet the competition,” he asked with a hint of irony, “will you greet them politely and go your separate ways?”

Mute smirked and Boss looked aside. “I’d rather avoid them,” he said. “Not many witnesses remain in the wilderness.”

*So in the civilized parts of the World there is law and order,* Xavier thought with relief. He wouldn’t like ending up in a dystopian apocalypse where the strong oppress the weak without consequence.

“It always depends on the person,” Boss added quickly. “But we might meet ruthless bastards. Be careful, honored sir. Your equipment might inspire violent behavior.”

“Just like yours?”

“I won’t deny I had inappropriate thoughts,” Boss admitted. “But thoughts aren’t actions. They might not have such restraint.”

“Especially when it concerns…?” Xavier left the sentence hanging and gestured vaguely. As he hoped, Boss interpreted it completely wrong.

“Yes, honored sir. Especially when it concerns a crashed flying ship,” Boss said, frowning. “From the start I suspected you didn’t end up here by accident.”

*A flying ship?*

A foreign memory surfaced — a shattered hull wedged between broken trees.

“In that case,” Xavier smiled and encouragingly patted Boss’s back, “you were born under a lucky star. Follow me and you won’t regret it!”
