# **Maybe I’m Not Just a Maid!**

*Help!*

Lydia knew no Xavier Valehart would appear this time to save her. *If I get out of this alive, I’ll apologize to him,* she thought in terror as she ran between the trees, fingers pressed to the wound where an arrow stuck out and blood trickled down. *So this is selfishness in its purest form?* she realized. *I’d do anything to survive.*

She had never seen anyone die — certainly not violently. Now she had witnessed two deaths, and she wasn’t sure which shocked her more. The soldier had fallen quietly, resigned, as if the idea of fleeing into the forest and leaving the ship’s treasures behind had never even crossed his mind.

Then there was the man who had cold‑bloodedly shot the soldier — and whom she had killed. *No, not me,* she corrected herself. *It was **the other one**. The one who appears when… When what?*

She was certain she carried a second personality inside her, an *alter ego* suited for this filthy situation — a ruthless creature with ice in her veins. Unfortunately, that other self surfaced irregularly, hidden behind a veil of the subconscious, unreachable when Lydia needed her most.

*Hey, you,* she called out furiously. *This is your playground. Show up or we’re dead.*

In a tight formation, weapons ready, the remaining Squares advanced while she prayed for her schizophrenic counterpart to emerge. But the other one stayed silent, perhaps unconvinced she could do anything against such numbers.

*Rely on yourself,* she heard a familiar voice. It was an echo of her father lecturing her about independence, surrounded by subordinates eager to fulfill his every wish.

“Of course, sir,” she muttered. As the daughter of a golden citizen, she had gone through countless self‑defense and self‑improvement courses — only to discover that the one thing she could reliably do was panic.

*I can’t keep running,* she realized as she assessed her options. The maid’s dress with its puffed skirts barely fit into the narrow corridors of the flying ship. In the dense forest it was a nightmare, every movement accompanied by the sound of tearing fabric.

“Miss, miss!” shouted the man who had introduced himself as Brundle. “Please don’t hide. This is a misunderstanding. We don’t want to hurt you. We only defended ourselves when that soldier threw grenades.”

*Technically, he’s right,* she admitted. *Our side attacked first.*

“Miss, we can come to a reasonable agreement,” Brundle called, his voice getting closer. Hidden behind a tree trunk, she desperately fought with the fastenings of her dress to get down to her underclothes.

“Miss, I’m not sure you understand our situation,” Brundle continued. “We’re in trouble.”

*Our situation? We’re in trouble? Me and them?*

“Those grenades were a bad idea. Really bad. That soldier doesn’t care, but there’s a reason we use bows in the forest. Judging by your looks, you’re a city girl — you have no idea what horrors roam around here. Monsters bigger than houses!”

*Monsters bigger than houses? Wonderful. I didn’t know I could be scared one level higher.*

“Believe me, miss.” Brundle took her silence as disagreement and continued with the persistence of an annoying salesman. “There’s a good chance the explosions attracted something none of us want to meet. Something knocked this ship out of the sky, and I’d rather not stay here long enough to find out what.”

*To hell with you,* she thought. *I’m not delaying you. Leave me alone.*

She peeked out from behind the tree. The moment her head appeared, one of the Squares raised his bow, but Brundle stopped him with an angry gesture.

“If you’re so harmless,” she shouted, “then stay back. You’re stalking me like hungry dogs.”

“Of course, miss. Just no more grenades, please.”

“Why not? I have nothing to lose. Keep your distance!”

*If only they knew all the grenades were thrown by that damned bastard.*

Five minutes ago she had thought the soldier a hero who died defending a fortress. Now she cursed him. She understood he hadn’t intended to save them — he had wanted to lure some unspeakable monster with the logic: *You won’t get my treasures!*

“Take whatever you want from the ship,” she shouted, tearing off her dress. She kicked her high‑heeled shoes far away. “I’m going my own way.”

In a flash of inspiration she bent down to grab the boots left behind by the dead Square. As she put them on, a penetrating stench hit her, making her toes curl.

“Miss,” Brundle urged, “you won’t survive alone in the forest.”

“Thank you kindly, I’ll manage.”

She felt as if she had stepped into a plague pit. She lived in a society where accidental touch caused nausea — so putting on warm, sweaty leather boots made her gag.

*Don’t be so sensitive,* she scolded herself. *You don’t want to run through the forest barefoot.*

To distract herself, she examined the long needle of hardened steel she had found where the Square had died. The other one had thrown it. *Is this even a weapon?* she wondered. A red stone was set into the eye of the needle, connected by a silver groove. She felt a tingling in her fingers that spread under her ribs, where her inner flame smoldered.

*Strange feeling!*

She peeked out again. The Squares seemed to keep their word and weren’t advancing. *Isn’t there one fewer?*

Vice‑Master Brundle smiled encouragingly, but on his rough, scarred face the smile twisted into a disturbing, threatening grimace supported by aggressive body language.

“We have the best intentions,” he assured her, arms spread.

*No, he’s not coming to help,* she realized. Then she heard rustling and sensed a sudden movement behind her. *Damn!*

“Good job, Vice‑Master. She swallowed it whole. She won’t be throwing any more grenades.”

A strong arm hooked around her neck, yanking her back, tightening like a noose. *It’s over!* As Lydia lost consciousness, her inner flame flared — and **the other one** finally decided to act.

Half‑conscious, Lydia felt her alter ego take command. Without hesitation, it drove the needle deep into the Square’s arm near the shoulder and let the inner flame flow into the open bloodstream.

“What have you done! What have you done, you bitch!”

The grip loosened and she turned to face the staggering Square.

“Why use grenades,” the other one smirked, “when a simple nerve toxin will do? Is the paralysis starting?” she asked with interest. “No, don’t bother. Let’s enjoy our little intimate moment,” she added with sensual softness that made Lydia nauseous. *I’m sharing a body with a deranged sadist?*

“How… can… a… maid… have… an… inner… flame?” the Square gasped.

“Maybe I’m not just a maid,” the other one said and faded into the background as Lydia regained control of her body.

*Is it even my body?* she wondered. *Maybe it’s the other way around. Maybe I’m occupying hers.*

“Hanz!” Brundle shouted. “Everything alright?”

But poor Hanz didn’t answer — he was dissolving into particles of light. *No inconvenient corpse,* Lydia thought with a hint of relief. Even though it was comforting not to see the aftermath, she couldn’t shake the memory of the horror with which Hanz had fought the advancing paralysis that reached his chest and suffocated him.

“Hanz? Hanz?”

*Leave me alone!* Lydia prayed. *I want out of here. Someone help me! Anyone!*

When three Syndicate thugs had attacked her on the street, she could still hope the omnipresent cameras and police would intervene. Here she couldn’t rely on miracles. She bitterly regretted how suspicious she had been of Valehart.

She didn’t dare step out from behind the tree — nor run, lest she end with an arrow in her back.

*You, the other one,* she called inwardly. *What do I have to do to make you appear? Please, answer.*

But her inner world remained silent; only the inner flame flickered under her ribs, reaching toward the needle where new drops of poison were forming.

“Vice‑Master, watch out! Behind you!”

The panicked shouts snapped her back to reality. When she cautiously leaned out, she stared in shock as the Squares turned and raised their bows. Behind them emerged something indistinct — a transparent, invisible outline of a human body — which slammed into the frozen Brundle.

"Run!"

The other one finally spoke — and even she had lost her icy calm. The newcomer had dragged her out of the depths of Lydia’s subconscious.

Frozen, Lydia watched the group of Squares — who had terrified her moments ago — fall one by one under strikes of blue light. Like an apostle of humanity in a twisted world, the semi‑transparent being didn’t kill, only stunned — at least that’s what she assumed, since the fallen bodies remained lying in the undergrowth instead of dissolving into particles of light.

From behind the trees on the left edge of the clearing — carved out by the ship’s crash — another group of rangers emerged: two men with bows and a woman with a massive staff.

*More Squares?* she wondered.

“Watch out!” she shouted, convinced that the enemy of her enemy must be her friend.

But the warning was pointless — the trio stood on the opposite side. The woman with the staff stopped over each stunned attacker and cast a white cloud on them, its purpose unknown to Lydia.

Then the semi‑transparent being stepped out of its camouflage, revealing a tall, perhaps too handsome man with a high forehead, sharp nose, and pointed chin. As he approached her, she noticed the eyes — vertical reptilian pupils with yellow‑red irises — giving him a terrifying look, like a half‑man from a nightmare.

In his right hand he held a long fang, and in the other a sphere of blue light. But he approached slowly, alert yet not threatening, and she relaxed.

*Are we going to negotiate?*

“I assume,” she said with a calm she didn’t feel, “that **you also** don’t want to hurt me, Mister Lizard.”

Her words surprised him.

“Lizard? Why do you call me that?” he asked.

“Because you have lizard eyes,” she answered, though she knew she was lying. The name had come from the other one, rising from the depths of her memory — and likely meant more than a simple description.

“I have lizard eyes?” he repeated thoughtfully. “Could be. I’m missing a mirror out here. And what do they call you?”

“Ly… Foxling,” she corrected herself quickly. “I’m a maid, and they call me Foxling because of my hair.”

She showed him her red braid while her other hand clutched the treacherous needle.

“Foxling?” he repeated, as if expecting a different answer. “Then I see no reason you couldn’t call me Lizard. Seems we have a lot in common. Lately I’ve had good luck with redheads.”

She sensed a hint — an unspoken question. *Redheads? A lot in common?*

“You’re…?”

But when she tried to say **Player**, she twisted in pain.

“Some truths cannot be spoken,” he noted with a familiar smirk. *Valehart?* she wondered. Even though the face differed, the characteristic arrogant curl of the lips — the expression of a man pretending to know everything better than she did — was unmistakable.

“That thing you’re hiding, you won’t need it,” he continued, extinguishing the blue light in his palm. “I’m offering cooperation, little abandoned Foxling.”

At that moment thunder rolled and trees cracked. Something mammoth‑sized, unimaginably huge, had awakened in the distance — and was coming toward them.
