# People Believe What They Want to Believe

While ordering an e-taxi to the address Svetlana had given him, Xavier silently thanked his lucky stars.

He possessed a kind of determination that had little to do with kindness. If the Game was to become the foundation of his future, and if the number of Players was limited not only by the ability to enter the Game but also by the ability to remain in it, then he was prepared to move heaven and earth to recruit the three students onto his team.

"What happened?" Medard asked curiously as he climbed into the vehicle.

"Do you remember those kids we met?"

"Yes. Do you think they got in?"

"I don't know. But they're in trouble."

"Who did you talk to?"

"That blonde girl, Svetlana."

"What kind of trouble? Don't tell me..."

The unfinished question made it obvious where Medard's imagination had gone. To a forty-year-old family man, Svetlana and the others were still children who needed protecting. Their presence in the Game, with all its dark dangers, was unacceptable to a conventional mind.

Xavier suffered no such prejudice. As a teenager, he had lived through horrors beside which an oversized mammoth would have seemed a charming distraction.

*Ever since I met Gabrielle,* he thought, *a great many unpleasant memories have been coming back.*

"I'd say it's family drama," he remarked. "Svetlana hinted as much. I could hear an argument in the background."

"I see. But is it wise to interfere in other people's family affairs?" Medard asked hesitantly.

*Live and let live,* Xavier thought. *Medard genuinely believes that if he doesn't bother other people, they'll return the favor.*

"Those kids have potential. If they're in, we need to secure them before anyone else does."

"Secure them?"

Xavier smiled and patted him on the shoulder.

"Acquire them. Hook them. Reel them in," he explained. "Why do you think Hawthorne praised you so enthusiastically? If you hadn't belonged to Lydia's side, he'd have swept you off your feet like a pretty girl."

"I did notice that I made an impression on him," Medard admitted with a trace of vanity. "But those kids mustn't enter the Game. Not after what happened to the two of you."

He modestly excluded himself from the statement.

*Mustn't?*

Xavier recalled his conversation with Svetlana.

*Didn't her father insist that she stay away from the **Game Centre**? Why? At a time when crowds were desperately trying to get inside, the elder Troyak held the exact opposite opinion. If he didn't possess inside information, then only one explanation remained. He became a Player himself and thoroughly disliked what followed.*

"Stop! Stop! Pull over! Right now!" Medard suddenly shouted in an unusually commanding voice, pounding on the plastic partition separating the passengers from the support driver.

The driver, seeing no reason for such a dramatic stop, did not react. The taxi's artificial intelligence, however, slammed on the brakes with the urgency of an engineer trying to prevent a railway disaster.

Thrown forward, Xavier was instantly reminded of anti-hijacking drills from his military days. He tumbled out of the vehicle and took cover behind the front wheel, scanning the surroundings.

"What are you doing?" Medard asked in confusion as he hurried out onto the street, alarmed by Xavier's reaction.

"Me? You tell me what's going on."

"I think I just saw that Gabrielle Marrow walking down the street," Medard explained, awkwardly scratching through his beard. "Maybe I startled everyone for nothing. Sorry."

"It's fine. Perfectly fine... Stay here!" Xavier shouted to the driver before setting off after a black-haired girl trudging miserably down the sidewalk with two enormous suitcases trailing behind her.

"Gabrielle! Gabrielle!"

When the girl glanced back to see who was calling her name, Xavier noticed tears running down her cheeks.

She had clearly dressed in a hurry. Her appearance was disheveled, one side of her shirt tucked into her trousers while the other hung loose, and the lace of her right shoe had come undone.

The moment she recognized him, however, she straightened her back and, faithful to the values of a society that frowned upon emotional displays, pretended that nothing was wrong.

"Mister Valehart," she muttered. "What a coincidence."

"No coincidence at all," Xavier assured her. "So the Troyaks threw you out? No more pretending you're their beloved adopted daughter?"

"I left on my own. I couldn't have expected anything else," the girl replied stiffly. "Mrs. Troyak was there when we brought the NT transmitters, and she immediately told her husband. Svetlana and Grigori are still arguing with their father."

"Do you have somewhere to go?"

"I thought I'd try the university. I'm a scholarship student. But the dormitories are probably already full."

"You can't afford a private apartment?"

"Maybe some hole where five people share a room," she shrugged.

Behind the casual posture lurked naked fear of the future.

*She's just a frightened young girl,* Xavier realized. *It's not her fault she resembles someone from my own past.*

"You still have your NT transmitter?" he asked.

"Of course. I couldn't afford to leave it behind. Laugh if you want, but it's the family silver. It's the most valuable thing I own."

*Family silver? Family? Is that wishful thinking? Did she really believe the Troyaks were going to adopt her?*

"We hadn't even registered," she continued bitterly, "but Mister Troyak accused me of exposing his children to terrible danger. He was absolutely furious."

"Do you know why?"

"No. He just kept shouting that he'd explicitly forbidden me from taking any interest in the Game. I couldn't explain that visiting the Centre had been Svetlana's idea. He wouldn't have believed me anyway. He didn't even believe Svetlana when she confessed and tried to take all the blame. Grigori backed her up, too."

"People believe what they want to believe," Xavier observed.

He noticed that beneath Gabrielle's hurt expression lurked genuine confusion, as though she could not comprehend how Troyak's furious conviction had survived every logical argument.

"Give me those suitcases," he added. "I've got a taxi waiting."

She stepped back immediately.

"Why would you help me?" she asked suspiciously.

"Perhaps I'm simply the next applicant looking to adopt a talented young daughter," Xavier smirked.

Seeing her tense up, he shook his head.

"No need to worry, my dear Gabrielle. Over there stands Mister Mosswell, born and bred compassion itself. He'd never allow me to turn you into a concubine. I have a business proposal for you. Interested?"

"I'm not in a position to listen to business proposals," she replied quietly but firmly. "Mister Troyak made me *business proposals* every other day. If I'd wanted to accept them, I wouldn't be standing on the street with suitcases."

"Are you sure we're talking about the same thing?"

"How should I know? What I can't understand is how you found me. They throw me out, and suddenly here you are. Have you been following me?"

"Not at all. Absolutely not."

At that moment Medard finally caught up with them. A mere hundred meters of running had turned his face beet red.

"I was the one who saw you," he wheezed. "We were on our way to see Svetlana."

"The born-and-bred compassionate one has arrived?" Gabrielle asked with mild amusement. "But, Mister Mosswell, you have no reason to rush off to see Svetlana. Why would either of you care about her or me? One chance meeting doesn't make us friends. Why would Svetlana call you of all people?"

Xavier could have provided a perfectly reasonable explanation.

Instead, he decided to step back and observe his potential companions.

As expected, in a duel between lumbering dignity and razor-sharp wit, Gabrielle held the advantage.

*That's her weakness,* he realized while listening to her tear Medard's defense into shreds. *She's like my little fairy. She has to win at all costs. No wonder Svetlana's mother didn't want her around. If she kept quiet more often, she'd learn far more.*

In the end, Medard's stubborn honesty convinced Gabrielle, even though the bearded man was unable to provide a single convincing reason why she should trust them.

"So it really was coincidence?" she asked Xavier hesitantly.

She received the reptilian grin that irritated Lydia so much.

"You shouldn't believe in coincidences," Xavier replied, grabbing both suitcases. "We're here to offer you a business proposal. Tracking down all three of you cost me considerable time, so I won't pretend Providence personally delivered you into my path."

"I'm not going with you," Gabrielle growled.

"Of course you're not. You're getting into that taxi, and it'll take you to the university and the Institute for Advanced Technology Research. A very pleasant Silver lady will take care of you there. We'll continue on foot."

"What?"

"I'm not interested only in you. I want the other two as well."

Though Gabrielle was far less prone to hasty conclusions than Medard, she was still shocked.

"What for?" she demanded. "What are you trying to drag them into? They're not like me, abandoned orphans with nobody to protect them. They have parents, and their father is a public figure."

"You want to advocate for them?" Xavier asked with amusement. "Protect them? Whatever you're imagining, I don't intend to hurt them. At least not in the way you're thinking."

"So you'll hurt them some other way?"

"I'm afraid so. Ask Medard if you don't believe me."

"Is that true, Mister Mosswell?"

Medard looked as though he had bitten into a lemon.

"I probably shouldn't say this," he said, glancing uncertainly at Xavier, "but yes. The proposal involves danger. But also opportunity," he added triumphantly, evidently inspired by Hawthorne's words before finishing with one of Lydia's observations. "If I can do it, so can you. You're far smarter than I am. You won't get lost in the Game."

"You ruined it," Xavier scolded him. "You can't talk about the Game in public. How do you know Gabrielle is actually inside? Have you seen her NT transmitter?"

"Oh. Sorry."

"This is about the Game?" Gabrielle asked in astonishment. "You've been tracking us because of that stupid Game?"

"You said yourself that the NT transmitter is your family silver. It's time to cash it in. Or did you honestly think I intended to adopt you?"

"I understand now. That's why you were so easy to persuade after the duel. I knew you didn't strike me as the sort of man who helps people out of pure altruism. You planned all this from the very beginning."

This time she sounded impressed rather than outraged.

"You're wrong," Medard hurried to say, another victim of cold-blooded manipulation. "Xavier is a good man."

The black-haired girl gave him a look that was half contempt, half pity.

It clearly said:

*You of all people shouldn't be saying that.*

"At least we're finally understanding each other," Xavier concluded, pointing toward the taxi. "Getting in or not?"

She hesitated.

"Is the Game really as terrible as Mister Troyak claims?"

"It's worse," Xavier replied without blinking. "Even Silvers don't want to go back."

"That was a rather cheap trick," Gabrielle said as she climbed into the vehicle. "You think I'm some naive little fool desperate to prove she's better than everyone else. I'll go, but I haven't signed anything. Don't complain if your investment turns out badly."

"You're wonderfully perceptive."

*To your own detriment,* Xavier thought.

The moment the taxi disappeared into the distance, he gestured for Medard to follow him.

"We're not far from the Troyaks' house. Five more minutes."

"Wait, wait. Was that really a trick?" Medard asked in amazement. "You told her the truth."

"I'm an apostle of honesty. That's why the girl's trapped. Nothing is easier than persuading someone who thinks rationally. How do you think she'll decide?"

"No idea. How?"

Remembering Gabrielle's condescending look, Xavier silently thanked heaven that Medard was so charmingly oblivious.

He patted him on the shoulder.

*Just like you, my dear friend. She'll decide exactly the way you and Lydia did.*

*You never had a choice.*
