# **If I Can Do It, So Can You!**

CRRR!

Medard stood at the front door, impatiently hammering the doorbell. Even though he was dressed in a blazer and meticulously groomed like a corporate employee he wasn't, something dramatic must have happened to him. He ignored the chirping birds in the aviary and behaved like a man for whom personal safety had ceased to matter.

“Stop it!” Xavier shouted through the intercom. “Stop that damned ringing. The door's open. Come upstairs.”

“Colleague? Employee? Client?” Lydia asked curiously. “He seems stressed.”

“A friend.”

“I can't imagine you having any,” Lydia remarked, and Xavier was beginning to find her ability to read him like an open book irritating.

*Isn't she right, though?* he wondered. *Strictly speaking, Medard is nothing more than a casual acquaintance.*

“Exactly,” he said coldly. “We met at the **Game Center**.”

“Ah. Then I understand why he's upset. He's a Plastic, isn't he?”

It wasn't difficult to understand Lydia's reasoning. Society regarded Plastics as incapable of success, and the Game had proven overwhelming even for someone like her. Yet Xavier couldn't understand how she had recognized Medard's civic status.

*Is it purely observation?* he wondered. *Or did I miss something?*

Yet no matter how hard he looked, even when Medard entered the apartment, he couldn't find anything that revealed it. Except perhaps that Medard wore his badge hidden beneath his white shirt.

“Xavier, I just can't continue,” the bearded man burst out miserably.

Then he froze and stared at the woman eating breakfast.

“I didn't know you had company,” he apologized. “Um, good morning, miss. I barged in like a flood.”

“Good morning, sir,” Lydia replied, offering her hand. “Lydia Carver. Please, have a seat.”

“She knows how to move in,” the little fairy commented sourly. “One would think she owns the place.”

Lydia blushed.

“Sorry, Xavier. I didn't realize that—”

“It's fine.”

There was no point dwelling on the habits of a corporate princess raised from childhood with an instinctive sense of superiority. Modesty and consideration were not virtues her family had considered essential.

*In her own way she's behaving exactly as expected,* Xavier thought. *Medard doesn't dare panic because he doesn't want to embarrass himself.*

After a short exchange of pleasantries, the bearded man realized the same thing.

“Could we talk in private?” he asked Xavier.

*Of course not. I'm not giving you the opportunity to fall apart.*

“That's unnecessary,” Xavier said aloud. “If this concerns the Game, Lydia and I have already discussed mutual cooperation. Consider her a partner in our enterprise.”

Presented with a finished decision, Lydia didn't object.

*At least that's one advantage of a superiority complex,* Xavier reflected. *But how do you deal with the opposite extreme? The conviction that you're a permanent failure?*

“Xavier, I simply can't continue,” Medard repeated. “I know I've disappointed you.”

“Calm down,” Xavier growled. Then added contemptuously, “As if you had anything to lose. It's your vaccine, not mine.”

“Don't be like that,” Lydia said, placing a comforting hand on his shoulder. “I'm sure every situation has a solution. What if Mr. Medard told us what happened?”

“Fine.”

Both of them understood the gesture meant:

*I'll play along with you if you play along with me.*

“Mr. Medard,” Lydia continued, “we have some idea of what to expect from the Game. But believe me, every challenge also carries opportunity. I can imagine the situation you're in. I'm going through exactly the same thing.”

“The same thing? Exactly the same thing?” Medard settled into a chair miserably. “I'm not so sure. I bet you're unmarried.”

“Unmarried?”

His two listeners frowned in unison.

“You don't have to worry about family,” he said at last. “No monsters are going to hurt them.”

“Monsters? What monsters?”

“You haven't encountered any?”

Lydia shivered, perhaps imagining something even worse that could have traumatized poor Medard. Executions? Torture chambers? Unspeakable horrors?

“You have to understand, I'm married,” Medard insisted. “I have children. There are lines I simply cannot and will not cross.”

*Lines no one can cross?* Xavier wondered. *Are we talking about chapters of human history we claim to have left behind?*

Society viewed itself as civilized. It protected the young regardless of status. And if it disposed of the useless and unnecessary, it did so quietly in hospitals. Those who failed to secure a vaccine did not deserve resources and died as a consequence of their own inadequacy.

The official philosophy was clear:

*Victor numquam cedit. The victor never yields.*

The entertainment industry churned out series about muscular Vikings, devoured by audiences sprawled across couches. Yet if those same viewers had to imitate them, they would likely vomit up their intestines.

*Did Medard become a raider in the Game? Were they forcing him to murder indiscriminately? To spear infants? Burn villages? Assault maidens barely out of childhood?*

Lydia had apparently reached the same conclusion. Her face turned pale and she shoved aside her unfinished plate.

“Please, tell us,” she urged gently, prepared for the worst.

“I don't know where to begin,” Medard replied. “I'm a married man, you see. My personal history, I'll admit, is one long failure. But I'd never sink low enough to betray Laura. She's the best thing that ever happened to me.”

*She must be,* Xavier thought, *if she tolerates such a concentrated bundle of self-reproach.*

“I spoke with her,” he said. “She impressed me. I hope you didn't get into trouble afterward.”

“No, no. Quite the opposite. She loves meeting new people. I think she gets a little bored in my company,” he admitted honestly.

“Right. But let's talk about the Game. What happened?”

“When I fell asleep, I woke up in a marriage bed. A luxurious canopy bed. Beside me was a woman. A beautiful woman. And she was behaving intimately. Very intimately, if you understand me.”

He swallowed.

“And I was still confused, so I let it happen. Worse than that. Before I understood what was going on... You see, it wasn't me. Some part of me believed she was my wife and that what was about to happen was completely natural. Legally sanctioned, so to speak.”

“Ha!” Lydia exclaimed, digging sharp fingernails into Xavier's shoulder. “You terrify me! Virtual infidelity!”

Medard misunderstood her reaction and continued gloomily.

“And the worst part was that even after I came to my senses, something inside me still wanted to continue. I... You see, Laura and I aren't young anymore. Age has left its mark on us. But there I felt strong. And that woman... she was breathtaking. Perhaps over forty like me, but everything about her was perfect. Almost regal. Honestly, Laura doesn't take care of herself that much. As a Bronze citizen she can't afford the cosmetics available to Silvers and Golds. But that woman... and me... I saw us in the mirror. We both looked magnificent, like Gold-noses. I was different there than I am here.”

He lowered his head.

“Suddenly I realized I never wanted to return to my real body. I wanted to forget my miserable existence and stay there forever.”

He slammed a fist against his knee.

“I'm disgusted with myself! I'm a filthy traitor! I'd rather die of VISP than give in to temptation. The Game isn't for me. You have to understand, Xavier.”

“That bastard,” Lydia hissed through clenched teeth.

Xavier winced as her nails dug into his skin.

“A canopy bed? A wife? Can you believe this?”

“You're right, miss,” Medard nodded sadly. “You have every right to condemn me.”

“Condemn you? Of course. What else?” Lydia snapped. “Just imagine. All that sympathy wasted on you.”

“I understand.”

“No, you absolutely do not understand. Do you know what we went through? They tried to kill me. I killed a man. A mammoth the size of a house chased me. And you're crying over a marital bed!”

“A mammoth the size of a house? You killed someone?”

The bearded man slowly began to realize that Lydia's outrage had a completely different source than he'd expected.

The avalanche followed immediately.

“It's unbelievable,” Lydia thundered, “how little it takes for someone to give up on saving their own life when the danger is just a tiny microscopic virus. A random one, no less. Some Plastics live to fifty. Some live even longer, thirty years after their last vaccination. Some don't even believe in VISP because they won the ten‑percent lottery and turned out to be immune. But the statistics say seventy percent of Plastics die within five to ten years after their final vaccination.”

“Miss Carver,” Medard protested. “I know those numbers. I worked for corporations.”

“Oh, wonderful,” Lydia continued mercilessly. “And here we have a perfect specimen of a fool who is willing to abandon wife and children to this miserable society because he can't stomach a little *tête-à-tête* with a virtual character. A collection of ones and zeroes.”

“A virtual character? I assure you, there was nothing virtual about her. She was extremely tangible. And it wasn't any *tet-a-tet*, whatever that means. It involved a full-scale invasion of her, uh, intimate territories.”

“I would switch places with you any day,” Lydia informed him. “Yesterday I faced a knife invasion. Later a hail of arrows planned to get intimate with me. Very intimate. Then a mammoth nearly flattened us.”

To illustrate the point, she stood and stomped dramatically across the carpet.

“It seems to me,” she continued, “that a little genuine mortal danger would do you good. Then you'd appreciate what you have. So let's keep it simple. You're continuing with the Game. If I can do it, so can you.”

Having delivered this devastatingly logical conclusion, Lydia gave Medard no opportunity whatsoever to defend himself. She marched into the hallway and started putting on her shoes.

“Time for work,” she announced firmly.

She opened the apartment door and suddenly froze.

Xavier assumed she'd caught sight of herself in the wall mirror. During her passionate speech she had wrecked her hairstyle and buttoned her blazer unevenly.

Yet she made no attempt to fix either problem.

“We're partners?” she asked, directing the question more at Xavier than Medard.

“Apparently.”

“In that case...” she hesitated. “How about a little walk, my new companions?”

And even a blind man could see that despite her confident declaration, Lydia was terrified of venturing out into the streets alone.
