# One Mistake After Another

"What are you still waiting for?"

Hawthorne's voice carried a generous measure of superior impatience. Yet nothing seemed capable of persuading Xavier and Medard to leave Lydia's side.

"Allow me to repeat myself," said Hawthorne firmly. "My conversation with Miss Lydia is protected by confidentiality under the Game Act. The fact that I am sharing this information with you could get you into serious trouble."

"We never forced you to share anything," Xavier replied dryly. Then he added, "Easy there, mate. I don't know how deep you're swimming in hot water, but the three of us are *bona fide* Players. Feel free to speak your mind."

It was hard to tell whether Hawthorne was more shocked by Xavier's brazen familiarity or by his sudden claim. Either way, he lost his composure and stared at him with a slack jaw.

"You are what?"

"You heard me. Are you trying to insult me by suggesting that I'm incapable of entering the Game?"

A dangerous edge crept into Xavier's voice, one that sent a chill through Medard.

*Good heavens,* he thought. *I'm not built for this.*

His peaceful life had changed beyond recognition. Suddenly he found himself drifting through a world of intrigue and adventure. Even his virtual cheating seemed insignificant compared to what he was facing now. Being in the company of three Silvers was something he had never imagined in his wildest dreams, and now he was beginning to realize it was hardly a blessing.

When he had first met Xavier, he could not understand how a man wandering the city in cheap, faded clothing could ever have earned a silver badge. Later, after the duel with Xantipa Miller, he revised his judgment somewhat, but he still regarded Xavier as the exception that proved the rule.

*A harmless Silver? Was I being naive?*

Even Jeremiah Hawthorne, a corporate lawyer, the sort of figure who inspired fear and dread among Bronzes and Plastics alike, was visibly rattled once he realized Xavier was angling for a duel.

"Of course I am not questioning your abilities, Mister Valehart," he said quickly. "It's simply unusual to find so many Players gathered in one place. Even so," he continued hesitantly, "I must verify your claim. It's a legal requirement, not a sign of distrust. If it turned out," he nodded toward Medard, by far the least dangerous creature in the room, "that Mister Mosswell had overheard information about the Game that he was not entitled to know, both he and I would find ourselves in a rather uncomfortable position."

*Don't mind me!* Medard groaned inwardly. *Just say the word and I'll vanish into thin air.*

The only things preventing him from giving in to Hawthorne's pressure were Xavier's order and Lydia's approving look.

*So this is my five minutes of glory,* he thought bitterly as he awkwardly reached for his plastic ID and handed it to the lawyer.

"Here you are, sir."

A consummate professional, Hawthorne neither raised an eyebrow nor made any disparaging remark about the badge's plastic material. On the contrary, the moment he finished scanning it, he gave an appreciative whistle.

"My hat off to you, Mister Mosswell. My hat off indeed. Confidentially speaking, very few citizens of your category made it into the Game during this recruitment campaign. You must be exceptional."

"I..."

Medard remembered with embarrassment how he had fled the Game Centre and broken down in Xavier's apartment.

"Naturally he's exceptional," Lydia said coldly, removing her own badge from around her neck. "Or did you think I pick random Plastics off the street?"

*That hurt,* Medard thought. *When all is said and done, I really am nothing more than a random Plastic picked up off the street.*

"I have always respected your abilities, Miss Lydia," Hawthorne hurried to reply. "Please, here's your badge back. Your father will be delighted to hear you've entered the Game. Not everyone was so fortunate."

*Laura always says I don't understand the world,* Medard sighed inwardly. *And she was right. What a bootlicker.*

If someone had asked him who the biggest predator in the room was, he would have pointed to the lawyer. Yet it was becoming increasingly clear that Hawthorne stood at the very bottom of the hierarchy.

*Who exactly is Lydia Carver? Is she really a corporate princess?*

Medard had only ever heard bad things about corporate heirs, yet Lydia seemed perfectly ordinary. Competent, certainly, but hardly larger than life.

"Well then," Hawthorne declared. "The formalities are behind us. Rather than repeat myself, Miss Lydia, I shall not insist on a private conversation."

"Thank you, Jeremiah."

"However, I should point out that family matters are bound to arise. Consider carefully whether you trust your friends. You should assume your father will learn every detail."

*Yes, please,* Medard thought wearily. *Go ahead and report me to a Gold. I'm sure he'll be thrilled that his precious daughter is associating with a Plastic.*

The veiled threat did little to disturb Xavier.

"As far as the Game is concerned," he replied calmly, "I represent our group. Family matters should be omitted, or at least restricted to a safe minimum."

"Are you saying I should treat you as a legal entity?"

"*De facto*, not *de iure*. We're only at the beginning. Several agreements involving additional stakeholders still need to be concluded."

"Is that so?" Hawthorne asked Lydia.

She nodded, though she appeared somewhat caught off guard.

*Additional stakeholders?* Medard wondered. *Me, Lydia, and who else?*

He remembered Svetlana, Gregori, and Gabrielle.

*Surely he doesn't plan to drag those kids into the Game as well. Has he lost his mind?*

"Very good," Hawthorne remarked admiringly. "Few people would have reacted so quickly. That will make my task much easier. Miss Lydia, may I assume you have no intention of giving up your inheritance rights in **Asphalt Travel**?"

"Of course not," Lydia replied cautiously. "That depends on the conditions Father sets. Does he still insist that his successor attain Gold citizenship status?"

"That hasn't changed."

"Then he's asking for something exceedingly difficult, and you know it, Jeremiah. Even if Father entrusted me with one of our branches, my chances would be slim."

"Young Master Alfred managed it."

"Father always says that becoming a Gold is difficult, but remaining one is harder. The deadline is approaching. What do you think the scores will look like afterward?"

"The outlook isn't particularly optimistic," Hawthorne admitted. "But that is precisely where your opportunity lies, Miss Lydia."

"We're talking about the Game?"

"I see we understand each other."

"But why?"

"Because the Game is an opportunity. Please don't take offense when I refuse to elaborate, but we paid an ungodly sum for information that isn't public. If you insist on entering the Game alongside your companions, I cannot share it."

"If that's the case, how am I supposed to compete for a corporate stake? I intend to enter into partnership with Mister Valehart, Mister Mosswell, and, ahem, the others as a private individual."

"That may not be such a bad idea," Hawthorne said with a smile. "The only advantage your rivals possess is that information. But I'm quite certain Mister Valehart will find a way around the information embargo."

When Lydia looked curiously at Xavier, he merely nodded. The confidence in that gesture was so absolute that it sent a chill down Medard's spine.

*Don't tell me I've become part of some grander scheme,* he thought. *Did Xavier trap me the moment he gave me that money? Do I even have the option of walking away?*

"Tell us what you can, Mister Hawthorne," said Xavier. "No one has ever accused me of ingratitude. And if you don't trust me, surely you can trust Lydia."

"Under normal circumstances, yes. But where you're concerned, caution is warranted. Nothing personal, of course. But your profession makes me uneasy. Can you provide a written guarantee that **Asphalt Travel** is not your objective?"

Xavier did not hurry to answer. Even Lydia looked at him in confusion.

*Objective? Profession?* Medard thought. *I've never actually asked what Xavier does for a living. One mistake after another.*

"I can assure you that I am taking no action against your corporation," Xavier said thoughtfully. "However, the moment you threaten my interests, including the interests or wellbeing of my associates, the situation changes."

"I could live with that," the lawyer replied with visible relief.

Medard noticed that a discussion which had originally been about Lydia Carver had somehow evolved into an agreement between two legal entities, one of which apparently represented him as well.

*What exactly am I a stakeholder in?*

*I've become part of something without even knowing what it is,* he continued uneasily. *This morning I was hesitating at the edge of an abyss, and now I'm falling without knowing why or how.*

"Wait!" he blurted out.

"Yes, my friend?" Xavier asked calmly.

*I have the right to ask questions,* Medard thought. *I have the right to refuse. I have the right to object. I mustn't stay silent!*

"Wait," he repeated, only to realize he had no idea how to continue.

*Protest? Against what? Against being given a chance to fix the mess I've made of my life?*

Instead, he walked over to the large French window bathed in the midday sun and threw it wide open.

"Much better," he declared, congratulating himself in an effort to disguise the absurdity of what he had just done. "Please, go on."

"Thank you, Medard," Lydia said, giving him a knowing nod. "I was beginning to feel warm myself."

Until then, everyone had remained standing. Now Lydia led them into a conference room, where they took seats around a glass meeting table. The room was long and elegant, decorated with the institute's achievements and the names of its academic collaborators.

"As for the Game," Hawthorne said, filing away the signed contract and sending a copy to Xavier, "I believe it's obvious to all of us that we're not dealing with some virtual pastime."

"Absolutely," Lydia drawled, winking at Medard, who immediately blushed.

"It's nothing for the faint-hearted," Hawthorne added, pouring himself a glass of mineral water.

Medard noticed that his hand was trembling.

"You're in the Game as well?" he blurted out.

"Unfortunately... I mean fortunately," the lawyer corrected himself with a faint smile. "Otherwise, I wouldn't be able to have this conversation with you. I truly admire you, Mister Mosswell. Some people suffered nervous breakdowns after their first visit, but you... No offense intended, but it's difficult to understand how a man with your potential ended up as a Plastic."

*A nervous breakdown? Is he talking about himself? He's shaking like a leaf just from discussing it,* Medard realized when it became clear that this was no clumsy attempt at irony. *What exactly happens to people in the Game? Surely it can't be limited to mere adultery.*

Suddenly he felt like an imposter, and for the first time he understood why Lydia Carver had dismissed his experience so curtly.

*Am I screwed?*
