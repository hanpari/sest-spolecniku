# You'll Be Eating Out of Her Hand Within a Week

No sooner had Xavier Valehart introduced himself than Laura gave him an angry once-over.

"You've got some nerve, sir," she said, folding her arms. "No one has ever hung up on me the way you did."

_You shouldn't answer your husband's phone,_ Xavier thought, but since he intended to stay on good terms with the Mosswells, he offered a friendly excuse.

"It was a corporate secret. I had no choice. Medard will explain everything."

"Me?"

"Look how quickly your little friend threw you overboard," Laura remarked to her husband. "The Silvernoses will use you and toss you aside."

"They certainly will not," Medard protested sharply. "Xavier has helped me tremendously and still does. It's only natural that he wants something in return."

"Hm." Laura remained unconvinced, though his vehemence caught her off guard. "How so?"

"Thanks to him, I'm on the best possible path toward the vaccine," Medard said gravely. "Xavier is saving my life. You could swallow one little phone call."

"Just like that?" she asked skeptically. "He simply met a desperate Plastic in the street and decided to help him? He must be terribly busy then. There are mountains of people like that."

Medard flushed.

"Thank you for the confidence. I know I'm not exceptional, but it still hurts hearing it from you."

Afraid he had barely escaped one family argument only to witness another, Grigori cleared his throat awkwardly.

"It's true, er, Mrs. Laura. Mr. Valehart helped all of us. Thanks to him, we're here."

_That sounded rather reproachful,_ Xavier thought with amusement.

"We're all partners in a small business venture," he said aloud. "Your husband has qualities we need."

"Such as? That he's a trusting fool? Do you need a scapegoat?"

"You...!"

Medard cried out, wounded.

"As for the agreement between us, you'll receive a contract," Xavier said quickly. "You can read through it at your leisure."

Laura bit her lip. Then her thoughts appeared to travel down tracks remarkably similar to those Xavier had followed earlier. Perhaps she had realized that having a Silver as a friend might not be a terrible idea _a priori_.

"Don't wave contracts at me," she smiled stiffly and offered her hand. "You brought those children back to me. That counts for enough. You can't be a complete bastard."

Just as he moved to suggest the era's customary air-handshake, she seized his hand and gave it a firm squeeze.

"You don't mind touching me, do you? I'm vaccinated. Haven't been sick a day in my life."

After that she directed all her attention toward the new lodgers, fussing over them with the devoted care of a lonely old lady who had suddenly discovered a litter of stray kittens on her doorstep.

She assigned Svetlana to the guest room, and since she considered brother and sister far too old to share a bedroom, she ordered Grigori to move in with Lucas.

"I would've put our boys together and given you a room of your own, but they can't stand each other. They'd tear the house down."

"That's alright," Grigori answered, sweating at the thought of how much both brothers would resent him if he received such privileges at their expense. "I'll try not to get in Lucas's way."

"Nonsense. Your assignment is to civilize him."

"I think you're mistaken. I'm not particularly civilized myself."

"Do you change your socks every day?" she asked.

"Yes. Of course."

"In that case, he can learn a great deal from you."

With that, Laura closed the door behind him and turned toward Svetlana with the eager focus reserved for her principal object of interest.

"That went smoothly," Medard observed once they were alone.

They settled at the garden table and began their first council of war. Both men compared experiences and observations, hoping to prepare instructions for Svetlana and the others. Apart from the speculations in which Xavier so delighted, they discovered little genuinely useful to pass on.

_We know that we know nothing,_ Xavier thought sourly as he rose and headed for the university, intending to pick up Lydia Carver.

On their way back they passed the flower shop, where Lucas was still sitting, bored to death and deprived of his guitar.

When the gloomy shopkeeper spotted them, he jumped up and hurried over.

"Where are they?" he demanded.

"Who?"

"The Troyaks," Lucas whispered, glancing around to make sure nobody overheard. "You were joking when you said they'd be living here, right?"

"Why?" Medard asked, puzzled by his son's lack of enthusiasm. "It wouldn't be the first time your friends have slept over. I understand you're annoyed about sharing a room with Grigori, but it can't be helped."

"That handsome doesn't bother me so long as he doesn't make a mess."

"More of a mess than what's already there? How could he possibly manage that?" Medard asked with genuine astonishment. "What's this really about? Svetlana is a lovely girl. Plenty of people would give their right arm to have her under their roof. Before long you'll be thick as thieves."

"Sacred nuts, Dad, pull yourself together! I'll never be thick as thieves with Goddess Troyak. The second the guys find out she's staying with us, I'm finished. My reputation is dead. My status is gone. I'll become a discarded alpha male."

"Son, I solemnly swear you possess neither a reputation nor a status that could actually be lost."

"Listen, alpha male," Xavier spoke up, "why do you call her a goddess?"

"Because she's a goddess," Lucas snapped. "And spare me the alpha-male jokes. Coming from you, they sound ridiculous."

"Coming from anyone, they sound ridiculous," Medard remarked and cuffed him around the head.

"You don't seem inclined to worship her. So why goddess?"

"Because that's what we call them. The stuck-up, overdressed, unbearable girls from the higher circles."

"You call them that, do you?" Medard slipped effortlessly into his son's _lingua franca_. "Rubbish. Ever heard the fable of the sour grapes?"

"Yeah. About a thousand times from you. Sacred nuts, do you really think we moon over them? We laugh at them in the band."

"At the goddesses?"

"It's an insult for us," Lucas grumbled. "Not our fault some idiot invented the term. Maybe he worshipped those birdbrains, but we don't. Those noble hens travel in flocks of followers. Disgusting! And now I'm supposed to have one living at home? You've completely ruined me!"

"Aren't you getting a bit old for permanent rebellion?" Medard sighed and sifted through the stack of lyrics Lucas had been setting to music.

"_Pack of Demons! Satan's Pride! One-Eyed Serpent!_ What if you spent half as much effort becoming somebody? Then some goddess might actually notice you. You could start by getting a haircut."

"Never!"

"Shame. Then at least try to make those kids feel welcome."

"I don't want them to feel welcome! I'll be polite, sure. But the moment she crosses the line, I'll tell her, 'Madam, you've gone too far.' Because I..."

"Of course you'll be polite to her," Medard cut him off with disdain. "You ought to thank her. Until school starts, she'll be working the shop instead of you."

"What? She pushed me out of my own spot too? That cow! I won't even acknowledge her existence."

"You'll be eating out of her hand within a week," Xavier laughed. "You'll be like a trained monkey... And burn this the first chance you get."

He bent down and picked up a stencil splattered with gold and silver paint. It was suspiciously similar to the hanged-man symbol Jacob Lickboothe had worked so hard to conceal on the university wall. The plastic stencil had fallen out when Medard was rummaging through the sheet music.

"A true resistance fighter doesn't leave evidence behind."

"Sacrament, what a predicament! You're as nosy as my father. Give that here!"

---

Several hours earlier, Gabrielle Marrow arrived at the university gates.

She stepped out of a taxi and left her two suitcases with the porters rather than carry them all the way to the institute where she was expected to report.

_Why her of all people?_ she wondered.

She vaguely remembered that Lydia Carver taught inorganic chemistry, which at least made her a familiar figure. Yet there was nothing about the woman suggesting she would devote herself to orphans _gratis pro Deo_.

Like every Silver, Lydia Carver seemed distant and cold, a figure from another world as far as citizen-candidates were concerned.

The road to Gabrielle's destination followed an old gravel path once used by horse-drawn carriages. It led into a gloomy lane shaded by broadleaf trees, some infected by yellow-orange fungi, their crowns heavy with mistletoe.

At this time of year, near summer's end and before the autumn term began, the area was peaceful. Only occasional voices and the distant chiming of equipment drifted out from the research laboratories.

A single student sat beneath the enormous oak in front of the institute.

Immersed in the twisting grain of the wood around him, pale fishlike eyes illuminated by the glow of a tablet, he flicked impatiently through page after page.

It was Jacob Lickboothe, chairman of the student council, whom Svetlana had once introduced as: "A diligent member of the academic community."

Originally, Jacob Lickboothe had shown little interest in Gabrielle. Later, however, he learned about the nature of her scholarship, one reserved for exceptionally gifted students, and began keeping in touch under the pretext that the poor ought to stick together.

He himself struggled desperately just to qualify for an ordinary scholarship, so Gabrielle, who breezed through semesters and skipped ahead as if academic hurdles were pebbles in the road, impressed him deeply.

Svetlana, for her part, never sought Jacob's company. She found him too relentless and _plebeian_, in the sense that _he always pushed far too hard_.

Gabrielle, socially awkward herself, admired the brazen persistence with which he fought his way into places where nobody had invited him.

After exchanging greetings and a few polite remarks, Jacob asked tensely:

"You're not here to see Director Carver, are you?"

Gabrielle nodded, astonished by his apparent gift for prophecy.

"Nothing complicated," he said with a bitter smile. "As you know, she was supposed to supervise my project. We disagreed, and she made it clear I was finished with her."

"Then what are you doing here?"

"Waiting for her so I can convince her I'm worth another chance. But I see she's already found a replacement. Congratulations."

"I'm here on a personal matter," she tried to explain.

He merely waved a hand, resigned to fate.

"We don't need to lie to each other. Me down below, you up above."

"I'm not here to steal your project," she replied, though she possessed enough common sense not to reveal any further details. "It's just a small favor. An acquaintance sent me to her."

"And what do you think she'll offer you? My project served up on a silver platter? Most likely one of our professors recommended you."

There was not a trace of envy in his voice.

An academic career held no appeal for him, and he made no secret of his dislike for chemistry, a subject he had ended up studying out of necessity rather than passion.

"You're remarkably calm," Gabrielle observed.

"Now," he corrected. "Yesterday I was a bundle of nerves. If I don't get that scholarship, I'm finished. Neither of us has anyone powerful watching our backs, so we understand each other. Between us, regarding our mutual acquaintance..."

He jerked his chin toward the institute windows.

"...her position is wobbling. According to reliable sources, the lawyer representing the corporation that sponsors the institute intends to remove the director today."

"Why?"

"Me down below, you up above," he replied with a crooked smile that carried more resignation than malice. "Apparently that applies to Silvernoses too. Only the Golds have nothing to fear."

Then he noticed the box she held so carefully.

"And what's that?"

The package was something Gabrielle had not dared leave with the porters. She clutched it like a treasure, remembering vividly how important the Game had been to Xavier Valehart.

"An NT transmitter," she admitted after a moment's hesitation.

When Jacob failed to react, she frowned.

"Nobody told you what it is?"

"No. Should they have?" he asked. "I spent the summer alone as a fence post in an empty field. Nobody invited me anywhere."

The complaint sounded painfully sincere.

"You and I both know how important connections are. I've done more for some of my acquaintances than they ever did for me. They'd wake me up at midnight whenever they needed help."

He spat into the gravel.

"And what came of it? Nothing but thunderous silence."

He looked at her carefully.

"What about you, Gabrielle? You and I are competitors. I'd understand if you wanted to guard your secrets. I'd certainly do the same."

She studied him thoughtfully.

_Of course. He wants to provoke sympathy,_ she thought.
_And yes, we're competitors. The question is what do I gain, and what do I lose?_

"I'm bound by confidentiality," she said.

Not because she had signed anything for Valehart, but because increasing the price of information always strengthened one's bargaining position.

"I understand."

_I'll be grateful,_ promised his expression.

"The stakes are high," she warned.

But Jacob interpreted the warning as bait.

"If you slip up, you'll get into trouble," she explained patiently. "The first part isn't much of a secret. The NT transmitter grants access to the Game. You've heard of it, haven't you?"

"Yes," he admitted awkwardly. "I assumed it was just another attraction for Plastics and Bronzes. The rumors claiming otherwise... this fool ignored them."

His voice dropped.

"And what's the second part?"

It was obvious Gabrielle was weighing how much trust she could place in him.

Just as obvious was the fact that he had nothing she needed.

"I've never betrayed anyone," he insisted.

"You've never betrayed anyone you couldn't afford to betray," she corrected.

_Is pity enough for me to endanger my own future?_ she wondered.
_I'm about to trade a secret that isn't mine with a man who possesses nothing I need._

"Have we reached a dead end?" Jacob asked thoughtfully. "There would be a debt between us, one I could simply refuse to repay. Correct?"

"Correct."

"Then there's a solution."

His voice hardened with resolve.

"I can give you a weapon that would destroy me if you ever chose to use it. You'll have me entirely in your grasp."

He met her gaze steadily.

"Would that be enough of a guarantee?"
