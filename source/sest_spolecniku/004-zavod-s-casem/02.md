# Were You Rummaging Through My Underwear?

The first to step onto the street-stage was a beautiful woman, an older and more mature reflection of Svetlana herself. Mother and daughter bore a striking resemblance. Not merely in appearance, but also in taste and style. Both favoured pale, subdued colours, with perhaps one notable exception.

Where Svetlana was content with plain gold jewellery, her mother embraced exotic extravagance, from African bracelets around her wrists and ankles to an ebony hair clasp studded with diamonds, holding together a hairstyle woven from nine braided strands.

"That woman was born to suffer," Xavier remarked after noticing heels so high that their owner was forced onto her tiptoes and constantly in danger of pitching forward.

"Good grief," Svetlana muttered through clenched teeth. "While we're tearing each other apart, she somehow found time to change clothes and do her hair."

"The resemblance is uncanny," Medard murmured as he exchanged an appreciative glance with Xavier.

Both men had noticed that Svetlana, too, had left her family home looking as though she had stepped straight out of a fashion catalogue.

"I have no idea what she's trying to achieve," the girl sighed, "but she's about to put on quite a performance. Medea herself would be jealous. At least she's wearing a new blouse, so she'll spare us the part where she tears her clothes apart."

With the expression of a long-suffering martyr, Svetlana then quoted:

> *"I am a woman, yet not weak, nor of a timid nature and feeble will."*

Madame Troyak delighted in sweeping, fluid gestures worthy of a ballet stage. Hands stretched before her, head tilted back, she appeared to be a tragic heroine battling fate itself, convinced that sheer determination could overcome even the displeasure of the gods.

The struggle was doomed.

The gate opened, and Grigori Troyak emerged, looking thoroughly uncomfortable in his assigned role. He casually nudged his mother aside and marched forward carrying a large hiking backpack. A wooden practice sword protruded from it like a raised antenna.

"Please go back inside," he said gently. "I never said I was leaving forever. I'm not abandoning Svetlana. Once things calm down, we'll come back."

"But what if you don't?" his mother cried. "All my children gone! What have I done to deserve such an ending?"

Walking toward the waiting taxi, Grigori looked back with mild disdain.

"No one's dying," he pointed out. "As if you didn't know Svetlana. Neither of you would ever pass up the chance to make a scene."

When his mother made as if to run after him, he shook his head.

"Careful in those heels. Try not to kill yourself."

Since a broken nose would have undermined the effect she was aiming for, the martyr settled for a dramatic pose and mournful sobbing.

"No need to worry," Grigori said as he passed his father. "I know these people. They're the ones who helped us get into... you know where."

He broke off and glanced meaningfully toward the waiting reporters.

*So their father trained them well,* Xavier thought approvingly.

"You haven't reassured me in the slightest," his father replied darkly. "Apparently you still don't understand what I was trying to prevent."

"And you still don't understand that we're adults," Grigori shot back. "It's time we became independent."

"Independent? Two students? Don't make me laugh. You have no idea what kind of world we're living in. I've protected you as best I could."

"It's long past time you stopped. You raised an arrogant princess."

Though no name had been mentioned, Svetlana shifted uneasily.

She refrained from commenting, yet despite the irritation on her face, Xavier could tell how grateful she was that her brother wasn't letting her walk away alone.

*A loyal hound in the guise of a brave brother,* he thought.

"Good lad," Medard said.

"Idiot," Svetlana growled, tears glistening in her eyes.

"You're both right," Xavier declared, helping Grigori load his luggage into the taxi. "You've brought half your house with you."

"None of it's mine," the young man explained sheepishly. "I had to pack for our prima donna as well."

Svetlana shot upright immediately.

"You did not."

Her eyes narrowed.

"Were you rummaging through my underwear?"

"Yes. Is that a problem?" Grigori asked calmly. "Or were you planning to go commando?"

Then he turned to Medard.

"Would you mind sitting beside her? I'd rather not lose my eyes."

Once the dependable barrier of a motionless bearded man had been installed between the siblings, an uneasy silence settled over the taxi.

Eugene Troyak approached the vehicle.

Now considerably calmer and far less red-faced than before, he spoke in a conciliatory tone.

"Whatever happened," he began, "I don't want either of you leaving with the impression that you have nowhere to come back to."

"We know," Grigori replied.

"Speak for yourself," Svetlana muttered.

Then she added venomously: "Please don't pretend you're leaving because of me. We both know Valehart isn't some selfless saviour."

"What does she mean by that?" Eugene Troyak asked suspiciously.

Xavier pricked up his ears as well.

Grigori shrugged.

"Just a theory. Jacob Lickboothe contacted us yesterday. He mentioned that a Silver was asking questions about us. He hoped we weren't in trouble."

"He wasn't hoping anything," Svetlana corrected sharply. "That slimy eel gave him my number. It was obvious the moment Valehart called me today. Isn't that right, brave rescuer?"

*You devious little bastard,* Xavier cursed inwardly, though not without admiration. *The moment he worked out what I was after, he decided to play both sides.*

"You can leave and go home whenever you like," he said coolly.

"It would be better for you," Medard added with genuine conviction. "You have loving parents. Why put them through this?"

"My loving parents can kiss my..."

Svetlana began.

Grigori promptly cut her off.

"Thank you, Mr. Mosswell, but we're staying."

The Troyak siblings clearly inherited their father's quick mind, because Eugene Troyak immediately put the pieces together.

"You're planning to bring my children into that world?" he asked Xavier accusingly. "Don't you have a conscience?"

"If not me, then why not you?" Xavier replied. "How long were you inside?"

"Me? Never again. Once was enough."

The actor looked every bit as horrified as Jeremiah Hawthorne had been.

"I nearly died," he whispered. "Actually died. Maybe I really stopped breathing. Who knows? The pain was real. They left me there like a carcass. If I hadn't woken up back in reality, I'm certain I would have died both there and here."

"You're exaggerating."

"Perhaps. I hope so. But what if I'm not? Every night I can still feel the knife cutting into my belly."

"Death in virtual reality?" Svetlana laughed contemptuously. "You couldn't invent a more ridiculous excuse if you tried."

"Ahem."

Medard cleared his throat.

"Trust me. This *virtual reality* isn't what you think it is."

"You too...?" Eugene Troyak asked quietly, drawing a finger across his throat.

"Something very similar," Medard admitted bashfully.

"But you're going back, aren't you?" Svetlana said. "Unlike that coward."

"You really shouldn't talk about your father that way," Medard told her sternly. "As far as I'm concerned, I'm on his side. It took every ounce of courage I had not to quit."

"Then you have my respect."

"Thank you. But that doesn't mean I approve of bringing two barely-grown children in there. You should get out of the taxi."

"Really?"

Svetlana turned to Xavier.

"And what do you think?"

"Exactly the same thing. You should go home and play with dolls."

"You don't think I'm good enough?"

"On the contrary. You're too good. Medard here is a Plastic without a vaccine. He might stop breathing tomorrow. There's a corporate bounty hanging over my head. Nobody would notice if Gabrielle vanished. We have nothing left to lose. But you? Think about it."

Svetlana pointed toward Grigori.

"It seems you've forgotten someone."

"Because I like him," Xavier said frankly. "I'd be happy to have him on the team."

"Hah. Let's just go."

At last she looked directly at her father.

"Did you hear that, Papa? I'll be in excellent hands. Despite all his speeches, Valehart won't let us leave even if we want to. Notice that I'm leaving because I intend to prove I'm worth more. And honestly, my idiotic little brother will swallow all that sweet talk hook, line, and sinker."

"Smart girl," Xavier remarked approvingly. "She won't be fooled."

"To her own detriment," Grigori muttered. "That's her in a nutshell. Every decision is her own, but every mistake is somebody else's fault."

"Do you owe her something?"

"No. I'm coming with you because I want to."

"You told mother the exact opposite," Svetlana hissed. "You even packed my underwear so I wouldn't have to come back."

Grigori flushed scarlet.

"No good deed goes unpunished. Can we please leave now?"

Realising that the conversation had drifted away from the Game entirely, Xavier lost interest and instructed the driver to get moving.

They left behind shocked parents and a flock of disappointed reporters.

"Where to, sir?" the driver asked.

"Medard, give him the address."

"Me?"

"Of course you. Or were you expecting me to move those two into my apartment? You heard them. They don't trust me. You live in a house."

*And I already have one unwanted tenant,* Xavier reflected uneasily.

Ever since Lydia's arrival, he no longer considered his own apartment a safe refuge.

"What about a hotel?" Medard suggested.

"Out of the question. You'd just abandon them?"

"We can take care of ourselves," Svetlana objected. "Maybe you missed the part where I said I wanted to be independent."

"No, you can't," Xavier assured her calmly. "If things go wrong, you'll need support when you wake up. Lydia spent two hours shaking after we almost..."

"Almost what?" Grigori asked.

"Almost got trampled by a megamammoth. The equivalent of standing in front of a steamroller."

"Who's Lydia?" Svetlana asked.

"My fiancée. My business partner."

"You really have a fiancée?"

"Shouldn't I?"

"You're not the type," Svetlana said wickedly. "I thought you made her up so I wouldn't worry about Gabrielle."

*Obnoxious psychologist,* Xavier thought and addressed Medard silently. *She's sleeping at your place, my friend. Even if you have to pitch her a tent in the garden.*

He merely held her gaze until she finally looked away.

"I'm sorry," she murmured. "I know you're not a bad person. You must really love her."

"Immensely," Xavier replied dryly.

Although he fully intended to inform Lydia about her newly acquired role, he planned to do so very gently and make it clear that the arrangement was temporary and carried absolutely no obligations.

**BANG! BANG!**

A small gate appeared in the side of the taxi.

Someone knocked.

Without waiting for permission, a tiny fairy entered, accompanied by a team of white horses and the sound of Mendelssohn's Wedding March.

Inside an ornate carriage sat two figures from *Beauty and the Beast*, posed like bride and groom.

"I've informed Shadowstep of your engagement. Congratulations," the little fairy announced primly, clapping her hands. "He accepted the news without batting an eye, like a true ninja. Once he finished crying, he informed his lady that you were no longer available."

*What?*

"Didn't you hear? I handled that awkward proposal business for you. I read your thoughts, remember? You were trying to work out how to present the situation to Lydia."

*And what did she say?* Xavier asked nervously.

The little fairy answered before he could finish worrying.

"Her?" the fairy repeated, raising an eyebrow. "She didn't cry. Heaven knows why. Most likely she suffered a breakdown."

"Why? What did she say?"

This time Xavier spoke aloud.

Everyone turned toward him, astonished. None of them possesed a smart band, so they could not see the avatar.

The little fairy left him hanging for several agonising seconds before carefully delivering each word: "Miss Lydia Carver thanked me, accepted gracefully your proposal, and announced that she would inform her parents and the corporate leadership about the engagement. If you ask my opinion, she's gone completely mad."
