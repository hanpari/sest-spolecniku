# I Will Become a School Goddess as well

When Gabrielle reached the castle courtyard, she realised they had been waiting only for her. The moment she appeared, the servants bowed, while she stared rigidly at the prepared horses.

They were tall animals, dancing across the stone paving with wild neighs.

When she was little, three small ponies had been brought to the orphanage and the children had been allowed to ride them for all of five minutes. That had been her only experience in the saddle.

And now she was expected to mount a creature that two grown men could barely manage?

*I'm dead,* she thought.

Her siblings seemed to share the same thought. Both looked as though they were attending a funeral.

*But are they really them? Svetlana and Grigori? How come?*

Had she not seen the plaque on the castle wall, she would never have guessed who the two of them were.

The only thing they had retained from their real appearance was their fair hair.

But the Game had played with them. Especially with Grigori, who had always suffered because he considered himself insufficiently masculine.

Now, like Svetlana, he possessed a majestic golden mane that gave him the appearance of a troubled lion in a cage. As compensation, he had gained broad shoulders, a square face, and an aquiline nose. He also wore steel armour, carried a shield, and had a sword at his waist.

*If not for the hair, he'd think he'd died and gone to heaven,* she thought. *A proper macho man.*

Svetlana had changed as well, though not as much.

Her face had become softer. Her chin was rounder, her cheekbones less pronounced, and her enormous blue eyes radiated gentle understanding.

Her characteristic flaws remained, however, so even now she was nervously biting her lower lip.

"My pride has arrived."

The speaker was a tall dark-skinned woman dressed in expensive travelling clothes made from dressed deerskin.

She looked like a beautiful evil queen from a television drama. The sort who condemned people to death with a wave of her hand.

She possessed sharp, perfect features and deep-set yellow-brown eyes in which her pupils stood out, vertical like those of a satanic goat.

*Baroness Sylvia? My supposed mother?*

Before Gabrielle could recover, the woman approached her, embraced her, and kissed both her cheeks.

"Did you sleep well, little one?"

"Yes, Mo-Mother," Gabrielle stammered, grateful that her other self returned the baroness's warm embrace. "And, erm, you? Did you have a good night?"

"Excellent," the woman smiled. "Your father and I slept very little. Isn't it wonderful to have an inexhaustible hero for a husband?"

"Oh, Mother!"

While Gabrielle remained *perplexed*, Svetlana spoke up, true to her habit of never minding her own business.

"Our eternally dissatisfied grumbler," sighed the baroness and embraced Svetlana as well.

Not nearly as warmly as Gabrielle, as she noticed with satisfaction.

"Silly, you should understand biological matters between a man and a woman. Even if you've made the choices you've made, you don't need to become a nun."

"This isn't about biology but decorum," Svetlana snapped. "The servants don't need to know how you spend your nights."

*The real Svetlana would never say that,* Gabrielle realised. *She cares far too much about her image as a woman of the people. She'd be the first to preach against hypocrisy.*

"They have ears but do not hear," declared the baroness coldly. "No one would dare eavesdrop on a private conversation between me and my daughters."

Gabrielle found the statement absurd, but she noticed that the servants wore faces of stone and did not dare look round.

*That woman is a demon!*

"And what about you, my young hero?" the baroness turned to Grigori and embraced him as well. "Strong and manly after your father. Remember that you must be ruthless, not soft-hearted like your sister."

"Yes, Mother," Grigori muttered, embarrassed and strangely red in the face.

"But what's this?" smiled the baroness and teasingly blew into his ear. "Still hard and ready for battle, it seems."

The young man turned scarlet, and Gabrielle was certain that if not for the metal armour, his body would have betrayed him lower down as well.

"Dear Stepmother," Svetlana spoke again, placing emphasis on *step*. "Although we're not related by blood, you should remember that you're a married woman."

"True," the baroness retreated, "especially since my husband is coming. Pay honour to the lord of the castle!"

The last words rang out loudly, and everyone in the courtyard bowed deeply, including the baroness and her children.

Gabrielle discovered something astonishing.

All she had to do was retreat into the background of her mind and her other self took over.

It was nothing like Lydia had described.

*Of course. Lydia is a Silvernose,* she realised. *Perhaps her inability to surrender control prevented her other self from emerging all along.*

That would also explain why Xavier had been practically unable to summon his other self and why he had been so astonished by Lydia's account.

*A person obsessed with holding the helm has no chance of succeeding,* she thought. *That's why Silvers are doomed to fail in the Game. Whereas I...*

Comfortably settled in a corner of her mind, careful to leave the reins to her *alter ego*, she watched the lord of Mosshill descend the stairs.

When she compared the pot-bellied Plastic from her memories with the man on the staircase, she struggled to find anything they had in common.

Medard, lord of Mosshill, was a huge two-metre-tall man with a square face.

With his shaved head and short black strip of hair, he resembled one of the Zaporozhian Cossacks from the famous painting of the letter to the Sultan.

Like Grigori, he possessed broad shoulders, an aquiline nose, and steel armour.

In his hand he carried an enormous mace inlaid with silvery metal.

With a weary expression he looked over the gathering, then at his demonic wife.

"We ride!"

Then he headed with leisurely steps, as though reluctant, toward a muscular war gelding.

Svetlana and Grigori regarded their own two white horses with equal unease.

Only Gabrielle followed the baroness without hesitation.

However, she panicked when she realised her other self was approaching a wild black stallion that tossed its head and lashed out with its hooves.

One of the grooms was practically hanging from its neck.

*Damn it! Why did I have to end up with this brute?*

"Are you sure, little sister?" asked Svetlana, keeping her distance.

"We can swap," Grigori offered with the expression of a martyr.

"Nonsense," declared the baroness. "What girl has ever failed to dream of having a wild stallion between her thighs?"

"It'll kill her!"

"And what excitement would there be if we risked nothing?" the baroness countered. "You, Silly, are too settled. My pride, show them."

*Thank you, no,* thought Gabrielle.

She crouched in a corner of her mind while her other self tried to earn the favour of her mad mother.

"Back off!" shouted Svetlana when Gabrielle came within reach of the furious hooves.

"Stay out of it, big sister," snapped Gabrielle's alter ego, concentrating on the animal, and touched it with the tip of her riding crop.

The inner flame flared up and penetrated through the horse's skin into its nerves, where it ignited an inferno of pain powerful enough to paralyse it.

The animal froze and sank toward the ground.

Before Gabrielle understood how, she was already in the saddle, ready for a wild bullride.

A crazy dance began. The grooms lost their grip on the stallion and tumbled across the stone paving.

Grigori desperately tried to catch the reins while the baroness laughed uproariously.

"You lunatic! You'll get her killed."

"Don't worry, my silly stepdaughter," baroness replied to Svetlana's reproach. "My blood doesn't lie. At her age I could do the same. If only she could pass some of her courage on to you."

Meanwhile Gabrielle lashed the horse with the crop and forced it into obedience.

After several minutes the animal calmed down, covered in sweat and foam around its mouth.

"I did it!" Gabrielle shouted and waved to the baroness. The joy she felt came not only from her success.

*I synchronised.*

She realised that during the passionate corrida, with her life at stake, she had stopped thinking in terms of herself and her other self.

A merging had taken place.

She named it *synchronisation*.

She had become a new person.

Perhaps the first in the entire Game.

*Neither Xavier nor Lydia managed this,* she thought proudly. *Eugene Troyak came close when he was dying. When he was afraid he would die here and there alike.*

She rode up to the baroness, jumped from the horse, and threw her arms around her neck.

"Did you see me? Did you see me? Am I amazing? Tell me I am!"

Thanks to complete synchronisation she had gained something that shocked her.

Although she had never had parents, she now felt warm affection for her demonic mother.

Suddenly, she remembered moments they had spent together.

*I'm no longer myself,* she thought. *I'm somebody else. When I log out of the Game, who will I be? Shouldn't I warn everyone?*

To her, what she had gained seemed wonderful.

But what about the others?

Lydia carried Foxling within her, a maid-assassin.

Xavier had absorbed a primitive creature, a lizard.

And from what Gabrielle understood, that lizard might gain control over him even in the real world.

*And Lydia's interest in poisons? Where did that come from?*

"Are you all right?" Svetlana hurried over.

"Perfectly."

"I was worried about you."

"So was I, but..."

She leaned toward her sister's ear and whispered:

"You mustn't be afraid. The second knows what she's doing. Do you understand me?"

Svetlana stepped away in confusion.

"Who are you?" she asked in disbelief.

"Your stepsister, Silly. More here than there."

When she understood who she was speaking to, Svetlana shook her head.

"If you could see yourself. You're different! You have eyes of your mother!"

*It's a pity I didn't look in a mirror,* Gabrielle thought.

An unspoken rivalry existed between the girls. Both were obsessed with appearance, but Svetlana had access to cosmetics Gabrielle could only dream of, and so Gabrielle had always remained in her shadow.

*But the Game can erase those differences,* she hoped.

*And not only in the Game.*

She remembered the cream Lydia had given her.

*I'll be perfect too. I will become a school goddess as well!*
