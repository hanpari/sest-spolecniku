# I'm Not Like the Others

The endless social gulf that had once seemed impossible to bridge had finally closed. Neither woman felt any urge to confide in the other, so they searched for a neutral topic they could share. Biochemistry turned out to be just that, and Lydia Carver showed an unexpected interest in it.

*She's obsessed with poisons,* Gabrielle noticed as she realized where their conversation was heading. *It must be a recent obsession, because her knowledge isn't particularly deep.*

While Lydia studied neurotoxins capable of causing respiratory arrest and instant death, Gabrielle's attention was caught by a mention of the *Gympie-gympie tree*:

*Dendrocnide moroides* is covered in silica-tipped hairs that snap off when touched and embed themselves in the skin. These microscopic needles break beneath the surface, and the skin closes over them, making them nearly impossible to remove by scraping or pulling. The neurotoxin carried by the fibers causes immediate pain that intensifies steadily over the next twenty to thirty minutes, eventually spreading to areas that were never directly exposed. The severity of the pain, which may linger in milder form for days, depends on how much toxin enters the body.

<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dendrocnide_moroides>

*Could I replace neural induction with a plant toxin?* Gabrielle wondered.

Further research quickly revealed a problem. The Gympie-gympie's sting could trigger chronic agony severe enough to drive its victims to suicide.

*Jacob wouldn't approve of that,* she sighed regretfully. *I'll have to find something gentler.*

Their academic enthusiasm, interrupted only occasionally by conversation, lasted until Xavier Valehart arrived. He collected them from the office and escorted them to a waiting e-taxi.

The subject of the engagement never resurfaced, but the moment they stepped into the university courtyard, the two Silvers drifted closer together. From a distance, an observer might even have assumed they were sharing an intimate touch.

Though the display was clearly intended for public consumption, Gabrielle's sense of aesthetics revolted against it. Her distaste reached its peak as they departed the university and she found herself comparing the hands of the supposed fiancés.

Lydia's skin was smooth, flawless, almost airbrushed by the *bioactive* products distributed exclusively to Silver citizens. Even Svetlana only ever acquired trace amounts through her father, who received larger allocations due to his work in the film industry.

Xavier's hands, by contrast, were neglected, veined, almost rough to the touch, covered with poorly healed scars.

*Poor Lydia,* Gabrielle thought. *If Valehart touched me, I'd die of disgust.*

Although she suspected Xavier had reasons for avoiding cosmetic clinics, as any respectable Silverblood would've frequented, she still couldn't stop staring at those two radically different hands.

But Lydia's attention was elsewhere.

"What's wrong?" she asked quietly. "You look awful."

"I feel awful too," Xavier replied, sinking wearily into his seat. "You don't recover from chronic insomnia in a few days."

"I understand."

"I'll be fine," he grumbled, closing his eyes. "Ignore me."

*He's hiding something,* Gabrielle thought.

She immediately sensed that Lydia had reached the same conclusion, yet deliberately chose not to press him. Then Lydia noticed Gabrielle staring and began studying the same thing.

"Let me see," she said.

With a touch of embarrassment, Gabrielle offered her palm.

Compared to Lydia's hand, it looked as out of place as Xavier's. Gabrielle had no scars, and her skin was young and healthy, yet she suddenly felt as though her palm resembled sandpaper.

The sensation was humiliating.

Certainly, Svetlana had shared some skincare products with her, but never enough to create that heavenly impression, that invitation to a gentler world conveyed by a single touch.

"Here."

Lydia reached into her handbag and produced a sealed plastic container trimmed with silver.

*Bioactive Skin Cream - Not for Retail Sale.*

"But..."

"No protests, please. As our companion, surely you're entitled to a little advance payment?"

Gabrielle nodded, as delighted as if she had been handed a chest of gold.

An unopened container of this quality sold for tens of thousands of dollars on the black market whenever one could be found at all. Most students would have done almost anything to get their hands on it.

Even Svetlana, who considered Gabrielle a sister and was fundamentally kind-hearted, had never managed to part with one herself. Every time, she'd be wracked with guilt afterward and swear that next time she'd do better.

Somewhere beneath the flood of happiness, a tiny worm of reason whispered a warning.

If she accepted this gift, she would have little choice but to accept Xavier's offer and enter the Game.

But the Game was distant.

The bioactive cream was right here.

"Thank you," she said gratefully. "You can count on me."

"I wasn't trying to buy you," Lydia smiled, green sparks dancing in her fox-like eyes.

"Besides, you'd be selling yourself far too cheaply."

Gabrielle realized Xavier had been watching the exchange the entire time.

"You'll get supertonic drinks too," he added with a trace of contempt. "Welcome to the marvelous world where nothing is impossible. You'll become perfect from your hair to the tips of your fingers."

Both women blushed.

His words shattered a fragile dream they had both cherished.

"Vanity of vanities, all is vanity," he muttered. "We're getting out. Put those trinkets away before you inspire envy."

There were no crowds to speak of.

The street stood empty apart from a delivery van bearing the HC logo.

A slender figure in a blue courier uniform paced impatiently beside it, a small parcel tucked beneath one arm. Behind the van, an aviary full of exotic birds screeched and shrieked like a reminder that Plastics weren't welcome here.

"You certainly took your time," the courier said, striding toward them. "I've been waiting for two hours."

"Ah, our genderless messenger," Xavier smirked. "One moment, please."

*Genderless?*

Gabrielle had never cared much for questions of sexual identity. She considered them largely irrelevant. Still, she was convinced the courier was a girl pretending to be a boy.

"I'm not genderless," the courier snapped in an artificially deep voice before adding mischievously, "And what about you, sir? Building yourself a harem?"

"Careful, 443," Xavier replied, entirely unbothered by a level of insolence most Silvers would never tolerate. "Someone might think you're jealous."

"Don't flatter yourself. I wouldn't touch you."

"Wouldn't touch me? Aren't you mixing up your grammatical genders again? Or are you playing the role of the dishonored maiden today?"

"I misspoke," the courier said in an even deeper voice, flushing scarlet. "I'm here on business."

"Don't you have anything better to do after standing here for two hours?" Xavier asked as he dragged Gabrielle's massive suitcases toward the building. "Alright, the *it* can wait."

"I'm not an *it*," the courier protested. "Do you want your package or not?"

"You didn't answer my question."

"I'm extremely busy. You're delaying me."

"Doesn't look that way. How many Players do you manage? Three? Four?"

"Six," the courier shot back. "We're only getting started, but we're growing."

"Really? And how many people like you are there? Five hundred?"

"Over a thousand already."

"Is being assigned so few clients a sign of incompetence?"

"Stop trying to insult me. Some people are happy if they get two."

"In a city of ten million? Sounds as if the Game isn't catching on."

"No worries. Players are simply joining slowly."

"Well, that's reassuring."

*Sly bastard,* Gabrielle thought with reluctant admiration.

A few simple questions had revealed information that **Gaming Center** might have preferred to keep secret. If her arithmetic was correct, fewer than five thousand Players existed in a city of ten million.

"What if I'm dissatisfied with you?" Xavier continued coolly while accepting the package. "Can I request a replacement?"

"I didn't mean to be rude," the courier blurted out nervously, completely forgetting the male act. "You wouldn't do that to me, would you?"

"Why so frightened? I'm very satisfied with you."

*Of course he is,* Gabrielle thought with a faint smile. *Where else would he find such a fool?*

"I might even refer you some Players."

The courier lit up instantly.

"That would be wonderful. Did you have someone in mind?"

Their speech immediately betrayed the social class they came from.

Xavier gestured toward his companions.

"Gabrielle, show her your little treasure."

When Gabrielle revealed the still-unopened NT transmitter, the courier practically leapt forward and shielded it with their body.

"Please put that away, miss. Nobody needs to know what you've got there. You can't imagine the competition among delivery services. Anyone with fewer than ten clients by the end of the month goes straight back onto the streets."

*I'm starting to feel like a VIP. Valehart got that girl talking within minutes.*

Gabrielle silently vowed to be extremely careful around Xavier.

"Not my problem," she said. "A courier is a courier, isn't that right?"

"No way!"

The fluid blurted the words in a high feminine voice.

"My name's Leontyne..."

Then they remembered their carefully maintained dual-gender identity and lowered their voice by an octave.

"...but Leo is fine."

Lydia, who had remained silent thus far, frowned.

"I see no reason to call **that** anything at all," she said with such lofty contempt that even Gabrielle felt a chill. "We're in a hurry, and this **creature** is wasting our time. Why are we still speaking with **it**? Gabrielle's right. It's just a corporate messenger. A clearly useless **thing**."

The brutal ease with which Lydia reduced the courier to an object received no response.

Gabrielle wasn't surprised.

Unlike Xavier, whose appearance inspired little respect, Lydia Carver was a bona fide Silverblood. For people like her, despising Bronzes and Plastics came as naturally as breathing.

*Who would've guessed I was chatting amicably with her a few minutes ago?* Gabrielle thought in shock. *The moment she ordered her own sister's execution should've been warning enough that she doesn't waste sympathy on anyone.*

Feeling deceived, Gabrielle softened her tone.

"Maybe she can be useful... or he can," she added miserably, defeated by the grammatical confusion.

"How?" Lydia asked dryly. "It can't even decide what sex it is. My dear," she said to Xavier sweetly, "I recommend replacing **it**."

"No, please, wait!" the courier cried. "My name is Leontyne and I'm a girl. I'm not like the others."

"We gathered that," Lydia replied. "But being different isn't exactly a recommendation. Now that we've settled your identity crisis, tell us what you'll do for us."

"Thank you, ma'am," Leontyne hurried to say, as though genuinely grateful that someone had solved a problem she'd struggled with for years.

She pointed silently at her van.

"I'd like to earn your favor..." she began, then stopped.

*What's wrong with her?* Gabrielle wondered. *If she wants to help, why is she standing there like a statue?*

"You get exactly one chance," Lydia said, turning on her heel. "Gabrielle, my dear, give her five minutes. Not a second longer."

When the two Silvers walked away, Leontyne released a sharp breath she had apparently been holding.

"Your mom is one terrifying lady," she said reproachfully. "I honestly feel sorry for you."

*My mom? Why in the world does she think Lydia is my mother?*

Not only was Lydia barely ten years older, but her pale complexion bore little resemblance to Gabrielle's darker skin.

*Unless she thinks I was adopted!*

Which wasn't an unreasonable assumption.

High-ranking women rarely bothered with having children of their own. It wasn't merely a matter of avoiding pregnancy, easily outsourced to surrogate mothers. Raising children consumed valuable time.

Silvers, and occasionally even Golds, preferred adopting promising teenagers they had observed for years, ensuring they brought ambitious and unspoiled individuals into the family.

*I shouldn't be surprised by that,* Gabrielle thought.

Before she could correct Leontyne, the courier had already produced a notepad and began scribbling furiously.

"Listen," she said, holding up the paper so Gabrielle could read it. "Will you cover for me and transfer to my service? Here's my card."

But the note actually read:

*I can't speak. The van's AI is spying on me. I don't know much, but I'll try to help you. I swear it! Tell that guy nobody knows what those data disks are for. But I've heard that a few have been successfully uploaded. Only charged ones are valuable. GC buys them for triple price, but apparently there are auctions where you can get even more.*

The moment Gabrielle finished reading, the note vanished as Leontyne tore it apart and crushed the scraps.

"I've got number 443!" she shouted while sprinting back toward her van. "Don't forget it!"
