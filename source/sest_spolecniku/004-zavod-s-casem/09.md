# Hey, young pretty lord

The column of riders, wagons, and pedestrians made its unhurried way toward the edge of the vast forest. The road was a sturdy, wide imitation of cobblestones, convincing from a distance and impressively medieval in appearance.

The moment Grigori looked more closely, however, he realised that the smooth surface consisted of a transparent resin-like substance with shapes resembling paving stones embedded inside it.

He watched in amazement as the road slowly absorbed every bit of dirt that fell upon it. A pebble, a broken twig, blown leaves, all vanished and disappeared as if sinking into a swamp.

At another time, such a sight would have fascinated him. Now he merely stared dully at the wonders of creation, having lost the ability to be surprised.

Ever since they had climbed into their saddles that morning and left the castle at Mosshill, they had ridden through several villages, accompanied by a growing crowd of peasants, craftsmen, and merchants.

"Hey, young pretty lord," called a young and attractive woman whose neckline was so scandalously low that from his saddle he could see almost to her stomach. A basket of goods she intended to sell in the city was strapped to her back.

*Shameless woman,* thought Grigori's other self, curling inward with embarrassment down to its fingertips.

Grigori himself, having grown up in a less prudish society, had no objection to the view; he had other concerns.

*Why do these villagers seem colourless?* he wondered. *What's missing?*

"What can I do for you, beauty?" he asked in a tone copied from his father, the popular charmer from *Cats and Thorns*.

"Handsome lord, your collector wants too much," complained the woman. "My money isn't enough if I wish to travel with your company. He wants my body as well. That's not fair, don't you think? He should want one or the other."

*Lustful woman,* the other self remarked with contempt.

"If you can afford the protection of the baron's men," said Grigori, "then you'll be treated with respect like any other paying customer. Do you understand?"

"No, my lord," the woman replied, confused.

*Of course she doesn't understand,* Grigori thought with a sigh. *People here don't think the way I do. The word customer isn't associated with any enforceable rights in their minds.*

Because Svetlana had advised him to step aside in such situations and allow his *alter ego* to handle them, he followed her advice.

The smile vanished from his face.

"I presume you are not one of my father's subjects," he said coldly and distantly. "That is why you pay for our protection. Our swords will protect your life and preserve your honour."

"As far as my honour is concerned," muttered the woman, "I'd rather save my money. Money disappears. My honour doesn't wear out."

"You vulgar..."

Grigori immediately pushed forward, kicked his other self away from the helm, and tossed her a few coins.

"Go and buy yourself a place, beauty," he said with a smile. "You amused me. But remember, you don't have to let any collector wear out your honour."

*This is going to be entertaining,* he thought.

He had been prepared for anything, but it had never occurred to him that he would share a body with a bashful teenager who blushed the moment an even moderately attractive woman spoke to him.

Which was a problem, because in the Game every woman, no matter how old, was attractive.

Yet not one of them could compare to the baroness and her daughters.

It wasn't their appearance. There was merely some indefinable quality the villagers lacked. A spark perhaps. They felt *colourless*.

He watched the woman walk over to the baron's collector, argue with him, and point in Grigori's direction.

When the man finally allowed her to join the procession, Grigori noticed that she had kept the coins he had given her.

So he discreetly summoned the collector.

"What did she tell you?" he asked.

"She said you had sent her, young lord," replied the collector.

"Nothing else? She didn't pay you?"

"No."

"Treacherous woman," muttered the other self.

"Shall I throw her out?" the collector asked when he noticed Grigori frown.

"Leave her be. But order her to dress more properly, if she has anything suitable to wear."

"As you command, young lord."

Then he added hesitantly: "Would you like me to send her to your tent this evening?"

At the question, Grigori's other self shattered into helpless fragments.

"No! Yes!" he squeaked.

"That won't be necessary," said Grigori firmly, and it took considerable effort not to roll his eyes.

His *alter ego* was useful when it came to riding horses or dealing with the baron's men, but when it came to what Baroness Sylvia had mockingly described to Svetlana as *biological matters*, the young lord was better left in the background.

"Keep an eye on her," he told the collector. "That woman is crafty. None of our men are to get involved with her. No nightly visits."

"You can count on it, young lord. I knew immediately she'd be trouble," the collector added, sounding strangely disappointed.

*The people here possess a kind of innocence our society lacks,* thought Grigori.

While it had probably never occurred to the collector that the beauty might be a planted agent, Grigori himself had never considered that she might simply want to save a few coins.

The fee demanded by the baron's cohort was not especially high.

Even paupers dressed in rags, or rather in carefully designed clothing meant to resemble rags, paid without hesitation.

*I'd wager she wanted my attention. A visit to the tent at night... and then what? Poison? A knife?*

After Gabrielle revealed what she had discovered in the tower of portraits and the increasingly short tenures of the castle's stewards, it had become clear that the Game had no intention of letting them spend their days peacefully with silver spoons in their mouths.

Though they could not speak openly, the local rules still allowed them to converse through hints and implications.

They had even tried warning Medard, but he was far too occupied with his demonic wife for them to dare interrupt him.

Nor could they exclude the possibility that Baroness Sylvia was a Player as well, or, as Svetlana carefully phrased it, *similar to us*, since words such as Player or Game simply could not be used in that context.

For some reason, the suggestion deeply disturbed Gabrielle, who refused to discuss the matter further.

When she rode off in agitation, Svetlana remarked: "Her eyes frighten me. She's different from the person we know."

*As if you're not jealous,* thought Grigori, who was well aware of the rivalry between them.

Now that the Game had removed the artificial differences, it was clear that neither had anything left to reproach the other for. *Both are remarkable beauties. But so what!*

He decided to change the subject.

"Why are the villagers missing that spark?" he wondered aloud. "Something's lacking. Even our men aren't that colourless."

He half expected Svetlana to leap to their defence, but she merely replied indifferently: "They lack the prominent presence of our inner flame. Didn't you notice? It barely smoulders inside them."

"No, I didn't. How did you...?"

But Svetlana had no interest in continuing the conversation.

In general, she had become distant, unusually withdrawn into herself.

*Like a quiet lunatic,* he thought as he rode away from her. *She looks drugged.*

It seemed to him that he was the only one who still appreciated the seriousness of their situation.

After meeting the village vixen, who he was convinced had tried to seduce him, he felt thoroughly on edge.

During their briefing, Medard had mentioned that the Game had waited over a week for Lydia before the mian event happened.

*The moment we joined, our safe haven started falling apart,* he thought. *Can that really be a coincidence?*

They advanced toward an endless green expanse known locally as **The Adorable Owlet Woods**.

When Grigori imagined a forest, he thought of something like a large grove, while this was an ocean.

The villages and castle were merely islands within it.

"Oy, young lord," sighed the collector riding beside him. "Oy, young lord."

The misery written across the man's face raised a number of ethical questions to which Grigori had no answers.

The people of the Game moved, spoke, and acted like rational beings of flesh and blood, not programmed entities whose existence was meaningless.

"What is it, Ignatius?" he asked gently.

"That cursed forest, young lord. I can hear the beasts sharpening their teeth for us. Those animals hate us."

"I know," Grigori replied absently.

According to Medard, Xavier had described **The Adorable Owlet Woods** as a battlefield full of predators that attacked humans in countless shapes and forms.

"Shouldn't the road be safe?" he asked.

"Young lord, your life is at risk anywhere and at any time. My good brother was bitten by a spider the size of a man's head. He swore the hairy bastard winked at him. Always keep an antidote close at hand."

Ignatius tapped the leather pouch hanging from his belt.

Grigori might have dismissed the story as the usual hunter's exaggeration, but the moment they approached the first trees, everyone in the company fell silent.

A heavy stillness settled over them.

Even the villagers, noisy and clucking like chickens until then, quieted down and huddled together like a frightened herd when a sign appeared.

**The Adorable Owlet Woods**

**Do Not Leave the Roads!**

**Under the Administration of Crimsonfall City**

A group of men emerged from the shadows wearing green camouflage clothing and pointed caps adorned with pheasant feathers.

"I am Rudolf, deputy guildmaster of the **Pheasants Guild**," said the first, giving a brief bow to the Baron of Mosshill and his wife. "Our guild has the honour, esteemed lord, of escorting you and your family to **Crimsonfall City**. Here are my credentials."

The Baron of Mosshill observed the man silently and made no move to accept the offered documents.

"This is ridiculous," said Baroness Sylvia. "We requested no such assistance. We have our own rangers. Is someone suggesting that the Baron of Mosshill requires protection on his own lands? This is a routine journey."

"With your permission, honoured lady," Rudolf replied with a deep sigh.

He had probably expected exactly this reaction.

"I was informed that this is not a routine journey. For many reasons."

"Then let us hear them," said the baron, cutting off another objection from his demonic wife.

It was the first time Grigori had heard Medard speak inside the Game, and the deep authoritative voice surprised him.

Through the other self, he felt how proud his *alter ego* was of the baron.

Grigori had never felt even a fraction of such pride when it came to Eugene Troyak.

*Of course, my father is a fame-obsessed performer,* he thought sourly. *Whereas the baron is a hardened warrior. Never mind that inside him lurks Medard Mosswell, an ordinary Plastic.*
