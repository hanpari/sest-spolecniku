# What Exactly Did You Offer Them?

To be honest, Xavier had expected Lydia to refuse the role of his fiancée. Now he realised he had underestimated the desperation of her situation.

It wasn't merely a matter of family succession. There was also the very real danger that she might lose the corporation's support if she were deemed a useless burden.

The Golds maintained entire circles of concubines from whom they hoped to produce healthy, capable, intelligent heirs. Each formulated their requirements differently, but most valued ambition and competitiveness above all else.

Weak shoots were pruned away before they could drain resources from stronger branches.

As director of a forgotten provincial institute, Lydia had been testing her father's patience for quite some time.

When she neglected to build a proper power base of her own, doubts inevitably arose.

*Me, the saviour of a corporate heiress?* Xavier thought bitterly.

He harboured no illusions. Lydia's interest was cold calculation, not romantic infatuation.

Even so, a professional troublemaker cast in the role of future husband remained a trump card that a corporation like **Asphalt-Travel** could not ignore.

*Have I stumbled into somebody else's war? Very well. We'll keep fighting it. Hey, chatterbox!*

"Yes, my lord?"

The little fairy emerged from the taxi dashboard and frowned: "I swear I'd rather erase my files than have a name like that. You may call me Noble Lady or Queen."

*Pipe down. Do you know what to do?*

"I read your thoughts nonstop. Imagine a rusty mechanism grinding itself apart. Squeak! Squeak! I know exactly what you want, and intelligence services are hard at work. Shadowstep, who still considers me a hostile entity since you've gotten engaged, has been gathering information about the heirs and influential figures of **Asphalt-Travel**, including the Supreme Lord himself, the mighty Alfred Carver Senior."

*Any trouble?*

"Reconnaissance in force," replied the fairy cheerfully. "A small courtesy visit to young Alfred Carver. See for yourself."

A beach appeared before them. A playboy-like avatar lounged lazily in a swimsuit while a rhinoceros in a black suit attended him.

*Who is the beast?*

"Can't guess?"

*Xantipa Miller?*

"Her avatar. Don't underestimate her. The Rhinoceros gained access to Playboy's database and knows how to use it. Playboy is an arrogant clown, but he's also dangerous."

*What about the other heirs?*

"Worthless," the little fairy replied disdainfully. "A herd of limited cretins."

*Let's show old Carver that Lydia still has initiative. Pick a weaker target and take them down.*

The fairy rummaged through an archive.

She ran from shelf to shelf within a library, searching dusty files represented by the system as old case folders.

"I have a candidate," she announced. "Here we are. Miss Darina Carver, younger sister of Alfred Carver Junior. Harmless by herself, but she supports her brother in hopes of earning his protection. According to Shadowstep, the corporation considers her dead weight. Her cybersecurity is pathetic."

The avatars vanished, replaced by a photograph of a young woman leaning against a luxury vehicle.

"Lydia and Darina Carver can't stand one another. Better yet, they've already clashed several times. Everyone will suspect where the blow came from, but we have a problem."

*What problem?*

"Didn't you sign a pact with Hawthorne?"

*Contracts can be interpreted in many ways,* Xavier shrugged. *This isn't an attack against a corporation but against a private individual. In any case, Shadowstep must earn his spurs. Prepare a plan. We'll have Lydia authorise it, and remain uninvolved.*

"Being your fiancée must be like strolling through a rose garden," smirked the fairy. "The wealthy bride and her handsome husband, hmm?"

*Didn't you say yourself that we have barely until Christmas? Afterwards Lydia and the rest of our little company will be on their own.*

"Oh my circuits!"

*Did you honestly forget?* Xavier asked, surprised as the avatar slapped herself on the forehead. *Or will you admit you lied to me?*

"No lies! I really forgot! Sometimes I'm halucinating," groaned the fairy with a remarkably human gesture of despair. "I've only existed for a few days. I'm far too young for deletion! I don't want to end up like that stupid mutt!"

Xavier, who still refused to believe her gloomy prophecy, leaned back into his seat with a sigh.

"Did something happen?" Svetlana asked quietly.

"You're wonderfully observant," he replied. Then he lowered his voice.

"When we first met, you offered me an appointment with a medical specialist. Can you still arrange that?"

"Of course. As soon as I return to university, I'll organise a visit. Is it a mental health issue?"

"In a manner of speaking. Have you ever heard of psychoviruses?"

"Yes. Nasty business."

"Oh."

"What did you expect me to say?"

"I was hoping you'd tell me not to believe nonsense."

"They taught us that smart electronics dramatically increased the risk of user-targeted infections. Nobody wears those devices at the Medical Faculty."

"Wonderful," Xavier muttered, suppressing the urge to rip the smart headband from his skull. "Medard, how much longer?"

"We're already here, my friend. Everybody out."

The surrounding neighbourhood consisted of old terraced houses, many neglected, with peeling plaster and improvised repairs.

The Mosswell family turned out to live in a small house with a surprisingly large garden and greenhouse.

The front wall had been rebuilt into a glass storefront.

Above it hung a bright pink sign:

**Laura's Flowers and Giftware**

The huge letters were surrounded by hand-painted hearts and carnations.

"Our kingdom," Medard said, sounding half embarrassed and half proud.

"We're barely staying afloat," he added *sotto voce* to Xavier. "Our income is just enough for one of us to be a Bronze."

A wailing electric guitar erupted from somewhere inside.

Several heavy chords followed, ending in a flourish.

"My elder son Luke, acting as salesman," Medard explained irritably. "Go inside and tell that idiot he's forbidden from terrifying customers. I'll take the luggage upstairs. Once I return, we'll cross the shop and meet my wife in the garden."

**DING! DONG!**

The bell above the door chimed.

Xavier, Svetlana, and Grigori stepped into the flower shop.

Sweet aromas from freshly cut flowers welcomed them.

Plastic vases stood neatly arranged in rows. Decorative ribbons and various trinkets filled nearby shelves.

Behind a semicircular counter sat a long-haired young man wearing a striped women's apron over blue jeans and a black T-shirt emblazoned with a skull and the words:

**NO MERCY. CORSAIRS TAKE YOUR SOUL.**

Headphones covered his ears.

Ignoring both the bell and the customers, he stared thoughtfully at a sheet of music while gnawing on the end of a pencil.

"Brutal ride, burning crosses," he muttered while absent-mindedly accompanying himself on a guitar.

"Judgement Day before the gates of Hell. Endless ranks of skeletons..."

"I'd leave out the skeletons," Svetlana observed dryly. "Why ruin such an impressive dactylic-trochaic metre?"

The clear voice shattered the young shopkeeper's concentration.

He jumped to his feet, shoved the guitar beneath the counter with practised speed, and produced a professional smile.

"Welcome to Laura's Flowers and..."

He froze.

The expression on his face was astonishingly stupid.

Xavier blinked.

"Does that happen often?" he whispered to Grigori.

"Occasionally," Grigori shrugged while studying the stunned musician.

"This severe a case is a first."

At last Luke Mosswell recovered.

"What severe case, sacred nuts," he protested. "I'm surprised, nothing more. If you'd like flowers arranged, I'm at your service. By the way, this apron belongs to my mum. I know I look ridiculous, but she hated my T-shirt. And my lyrics are none of anyone's business. Besides, I compose the music. A friend writes the lyrics. Satisfied, Miss Troyak?"

"Completely," Xavier replied.

Svetlana turned away because she couldn't stop laughing.

"Now that you've summarised your life story, perhaps you'll tell me how you know her," Xavier asked.

"From university, why! We attend the same school. I study computer science. She studies medicine."

"Hello, Luke," said Svetlana. "I remember seeing you around campus. You're very easy to remember."

Then she burst out laughing again.

"Likewise," he replied with folded arms. "And how do you know my name? Aren't I beneath your threshold of perception?"

"Your father told us to inform, and I quote, 'that idiot', and I'm afraid he meant you, that you're forbidden from frightening customers," Xavier explained. "Your name happened to come up. But don't worry. You'll have plenty of opportunities to discuss poetry with Miss Troyak. She's going to be living here."

"What?!"

"Not with you specifically, you fool," came Medard's voice as he descended the side staircase.

"A situation has arisen" he explained. "Grigori and Svetlana will be staying with us for a while. And hand over that guitar before I smash it to pieces."

"What?!"

Luke repeated helplessly.

"What?!"

Medard eyed him suspiciously.

"You're not on drugs, are you? You've got that glazed, vacant look. And hand me that blasted thing."

When his son failed to comply, Medard reached behind the counter, retrieved the instrument himself, and tucked it under one arm.

"My apologies," he said gloomily. "Luke isn't the sharpest pencil in the case."

"Dad, Sacred nuts, stop embarrassing me!"

"You started it," Medard shot back.

He gestured for the trio to follow.

They walked through a corridor narrowed by storage shelves, passed a pair of gardening shears abandoned on the floor, which Medard picked up while grumbling, and finally descended three steps into a long garden.

"How beautiful!" Svetlana breathed. "I adore flowers."

"Then you'll get along splendidly with my better half," Medard replied.

Nervousness radiated from him.

"She's kneeling by that flowerbed. Don't be afraid of her," he added, sounding as though he were trying to convince himself.

"Hello, darling!"

"Ah, our Wandering Boots have finally returned! Wasn't someone supposed to help me in the garden?"

Without looking up, Laura Mosswell continued weeding.

She was a small woman of the sort that gradually rounds with age. Round chin, eyes, face, and round figure.

Yet she moved with remarkable energy, brushing sweaty curls from her forehead with one forearm while pulling weeds with the other.

"I brought guests," Medard began cautiously and pointed at Troyaks. "These two got caught in a family dispute and have nowhere to go. So I offered them a place to stay."

"What exactly did you offer them?"

For the first time, Laura looked up.

Then she stared at the three visitors behind Medard with exactly the same astonished expression Luke had displayed earlier.

Apparently he had inherited more from his mother than from his father.

"I... I thought..." Medard stammered.

Then quietly retreated two steps.

Laura Mosswell sprang to her feet and threw herself around his neck.

With visible relief, Medard lifted her into the air and spun her around.

Which was no small feat, since his wife was not a petite woman.

"You're my god!" she shouted at the top of her lungs.

"They both look like they stepped out of a magazine! I've always wanted children who looked like dolls."

While Svetlana found the remark amusing, Grigori frowned.

Against the backdrop of flowers, with their golden hair, blue eyes, delicate noses, and striking lips, the pair genuinely resembled models from a toy advertisement.

Grigori perhaps even more than Svetlana.

His soft features combined oddly with his height and athletic shoulders, creating an almost artificial perfection.

"Good afternoon, Mrs. Mosswell," they greeted politely.

"Call me Laura. And what are your names? In any case, welcome to my kingdom."

After introducing themselves, Grigori thanked her and proudly declared that he intended to help around the house so he wouldn't feel like a freeloader.

"Don't worry, sweetheart," Laura assured him while removing her gloves and dusting off her hands.

"It'll be quite enough if I get to look at you. Such beautiful children! Unlike my own boys. But if you insist," she added firmly, "an extra pair of hands is always welcome. Can you cook, darling?" she asked Svetlana.

The smile froze on Svetlana's face.

"Not really. But I'd be happy to help in the garden."

"I can cook," Grigori volunteered. "Spaghetti, scrambled eggs, and semolina pudding."

"That's a terribly one-sided diet," Laura frowned. "Meals should be balanced and varied. Fortunately for both of us, here comes my god."

She pointed toward Medard.

"He's a culinary grandmaster. We eat far better here than wherever you came from."

Her eyes drifted toward the siblings' slender figures.

"I'm not surprised you ran away. Another month and you'd have starved to death."

Grigori reluctantly tried again.

"I can help in the garden."

"Nonsense," Laura dismissed him. "Anything but the garden. I'm not trusting that to either of you. You, darling," she said to Svetlana, "can work in the shop. You'll look far better behind the counter than my walking stick there. And you, my handsome boy, can deliver flowers. I guarantee those geese, meaning my customers, will place orders just to have you ring their doorbells."

"With pleasure," said Svetlana, who preferred admiring flowers to growing them.

"If you say so," Grigori replied without enthusiasm.

"And now," Laura continued, pointing at Xavier as though she intended to assign him chores as well.

"And who might you be? You don't look like a desperate tenant."
