# Maybe I've Just Lost

"With your permission, honoured sir," the ranger Boss began with a slight bow, "allow me to present three main reasons why you should not refuse my services. *Primo*, something has disturbed the Fearsome Forrestmaster. Ranger units have been tracking increased animal movement throughout the woods. Trade caravans have been attacked along the entire road."

"By the Fearsome Forrestmaster, you mean the megamammoth?" asked Medard.

"Indeed, honoured sir. I see nothing escapes your notice," Rudolf replied with another bow. "Even a hero of your stature should exercise caution if he intends to enter its domain."

*A hero of your stature?* Although Grigori understood the ranger was merely flattering Medard, he still found himself wondering what exactly the baron had done to earn such a reputation.

"It is not only your person that concerns me," Rudolf continued. "My *secundo* concerns your family. It is an open secret that you intend to escort your children to the city."

"An open secret?" the baroness frowned.

"Honoured lady, if my patron knows, others know as well. The situation in the city is tense. The War of Colours is beginning. Certain factions may consider purchasing Baron Mosshill's support with the lives of his children."

"Mosshill maintains a neutral stance in the War of Colours," the baroness objected.

"Indeed, honoured lady, however..." Rudolf tactfully left the sentence unfinished and bowed again. "Following recent events, many consider the baron an influential voice among the neutral faction. Many people seem convinced that those who are not with them must be against them."

"Indeed, ranger, you make a persuasive argument," the baroness replied acidly. "Why should we not assume your mysterious patron suffers from the same delusion?"

"That brings me to my *tertio*, honoured lady. Please examine my credentials. You will discover that my patron counts himself among your husband's friends."

The ranger raised the document once more, but Baron Mosswell remained motionless, silently studying the man. No one dared speak.

"Please convey my gratitude to your patron," Medard finally said, "but it is precisely the situation in the city that prevents me from accepting such a favour."

"If that is your decision," the ranger replied, reaching into his satchel and producing a lighter, "then I have been instructed to burn my warrant."

"I am certain you have orders you must fulfil, ranger," Medard answered. "Please proceed as you see fit."

"Very well, honoured sir."

When the flames had consumed the paper, the ranger scattered the ashes with his boot before looking up.

"My group must now return to the city. Would you permit us, honoured sir, to travel with your people? We are willing to pay for your protection."

"Ranger, I can hardly forbid it," the baron said with a faint smile. "My tax collector is over there."

Once the commotion had died down and the rangers joined the procession, Gabrielle muttered:

"Clever. Very clever."

"Clever?" Grigori asked. "Wouldn't bizarre be a better word?"

"Certainly. Bizarre," Gabrielle replied. "If events are unfolding differently than expected, should not the unusual guide our thinking?"

"What?"

"My dear brother," Gabrielle sighed, "how much of that did you actually understand?"

*Where did that condescending tone come from?* Grigori wondered. He had always felt Gabrielle maintained a polite distance to avoid provoking her step-siblings with hints of intellectual superiority, but now she made him feel like a complete simpleton.

"I gathered that there are political tensions from which our family remains largely detached," he said cautiously.

"And?"

"It seems certain interested parties have taken an interest in us. Had we accepted help from one of them, we would have sacrificed our neutrality. That's why the baron rejected Rudolf's offer."

"And?"

"There's more?"

"Naturally," Gabrielle said impatiently. "Can you not see the outcome?"

"What outcome?"

"The outcome of the little theatrical performance Father and Rudolf put on."

"The rangers joined our company. We gained their support without learning who their patron is. Am I supposed to conclude the baron accepted the help without incurring any obligations?"

"My dear brother," Gabrielle answered mockingly, "your innate cunning would surely prevent you from relying upon aid of unknown origin. How do you know Rudolf's rangers will not betray us at the first crossroads?"

"Then the baron knows them and trusts them."

Gabrielle shook her head.

"I doubt Father knows every ranger in the realm."

"Why don't you simply explain it to me?" Grigori snapped. "I admit I'm completely lost."

"But I don't know what Father is thinking either. Mother probably doesn't know, either. I can only offer my theories."

*Father? Mother?* Grigori noticed how naturally Gabrielle referred to the baron and baroness. *This Game is confusing. I never know who's answering me. This Gabrielle bears little resemblance to the Gabrielle I know outside.*

"Please, dear sister," he tried, adopting a familial tone. "Tell me what your brilliant mind has uncovered about our father's secret."

"I might. But I need something first. Would you help me?"

"With what?"

"Could you hold out your hand?"

When Grigori obeyed, Gabrielle tapped him with her whip.

"Bloody hell!"

"How did that feel?" she asked curiously.

"I nearly fell off my horse," Grigori hissed through clenched teeth as the pain subsided.

*No. This is definitely not the Gabrielle I know.*

"Excellent. Do you think you could endure twice as much?" she asked eagerly.

"Absolutely not! Have you gone mad?"

"What a pity," Gabrielle said. "Our journey is likely to prove dangerous, don't you think?"

"I agree."

"In that case, I'd very much like to acquire some live prisoners."

"Live prisoners? For experiments?"

"Yes. I see we understand one another."

Grigori was relieved he managed to maintain a stony expression. He knew Gabrielle Marrow was not prone to strong emotions, yet her utter lack of empathy still shocked him. He was equally certain it was not sadism. This was pure intellectual curiosity. She wanted to explore the capabilities of her toy.

*Experiments on people? So what? It's only a Game!* he thought, before saying aloud: "If the opportunity presents itself, I'll secure you a... live specimen."

"It will," she replied, nodding toward the woman carrying the basket. "I'd say all we'd have to do is search her. What do you suppose she's hiding? Weapons? Drugs?"

"You noticed too?"

"The traps here aren't exactly sophisticated," Gabrielle shrugged. "People of our kind may enjoy a certain advantage."

*People of our kind?* Grigori thought. *More like people of your kind!*

"So what was that business with our father?" he asked. "What did the paper-burning comedy mean?"

"I think the document was blank all along," Gabrielle explained. "Most likely it was a prearranged signal. If the ranger had not burned it, Father would have known he hadn't been sent by his friend. Simple enough, isn't it?"

"Perfectly," Grigori said through wounded pride. "How long did it take you to figure that out?"

"I became suspicious the moment Father refused the document the first time. Surely his lack of interest struck you as odd as well."

*So right from the beginning,* Grigori sighed.

"What happens next?"

"An attack or a kidnapping," Gabrielle replied calmly. "Possibly both. We also can't exclude the possibility that the megamammoth triggered a monster migration."

"How reassuring."

"Why so disappointed?" Gabrielle asked slyly, pointing at the sword hanging from his belt. "Didn't you always want to try that thing down there?"

This time Grigori knew he was hearing the real Gabrielle. She was alluding to his enthusiasm for martial arts.

His alter ego, however, had interpreted the remark rather differently.

*Shut up, you pathetic pubescent,* he thought at it. *You're making me look ridiculous.*

"It has to be firm and strong," Gabrielle teased. "Let me see it."

While his alter ego blushed furiously beneath layers of adolescent innuendo, Grigori drew the sword and offered it to her for inspection.

As she examined the weapon, he stole the chance to observe her.

Gabrielle's in-game body was taller, stronger, and healthier than her real one. Her skin, like the baroness's, carried a reddish tint, as if she were perpetually flushed or feverish.

Her large expressive eyes with vertical pupils reflected the light, and combined with her thick curly hair they gave her the appearance of a doll. Grigori was reminded of Laura Mosswell, whose admiration for such qualities had once amused both him and Svetlana.

Although Gabrielle wore riding gloves, he noticed when she removed them that her fingers were unnaturally long and slender, their nails tapering naturally into points.

"What do I look like?" he asked absent-mindedly.

"Manly," she replied proudly. "Manly like Father."

"Oh."

"What did you discover?" he asked and pointed at the sword in her hands.

"Exactly what I expected. This is a *psychoactive* weapon, one of those devices fools insist on calling magical. My riding crop belongs to the same category. These are the control and functional circuits."

She pointed at silvery metal elements that appeared decorative.

When Gabrielle gripped the hilt, the circuits lit up and the blade acquired a blue luminescent glow.

"You only need to channel your inner flame," she explained dreamily, her thoughts elsewhere. Then she caught herself and returned the weapon with a smile.

"But you undoubtedly handle it far better than I do, dear brother. You practise with it every day, don't you?"

"Of course. The sword, I mean. Not... anything else."

"I do hope that anything else is just as firm as your sword."

It was obvious Gabrielle's alter ego had no intention of revealing anything beyond embarrassing innuendo designed to fluster her brother. Conversations inside the Game were monitored by a system that enforced character roles.

When Grigori attempted to address her as *Miss Marrow* in order to bring the real Gabrielle to the surface, the world blurred and his alter ego seized control.

It seemed the alter egos served not merely as guides, but as boundaries defining what each character was permitted to say or do.

"My sword is always, er... firm," he stammered.

"Naturally, dear brother. Would you lean down a moment? I'd like to..."

With that, Gabrielle rose slightly in the saddle, pulled him closer, and planted a quick kiss on his cheek before galloping off, laughing at his reaction.

Grigori struggled to calm his second self, which seemed ready to lose its mind over the provocative experience.

*Pull yourself together!* he scolded himself, feeling alarmingly vulnerable.

Not because of the laughter his flushed face and stunned expression had provoked, but because of a danger looming over him, a threat all the more terrifying because he had no defence against it.

Someone touched him.

"Young master, are you all right?"

The village woman with the basket was looking up at him. One hand rested on his thigh, her pink tongue peeking between slightly parted lips.

*That shameless woman again!* his alter ego shrieked with delight, fantasising about her hand wandering higher.

Grigori quickly seized control and urged his horse forward, out of her reach.

*If she tries to seduce me,* he realised in horror, *then the moment I'm logged out, she'll have that idiot wrapped around her finger.*

**Maybe I've just lost!**
