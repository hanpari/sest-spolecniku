# Hands Off, You Old Hypocrite

*They live other people's lives to forget their own.*
— *Mijena Adloff*

The sound of the argument carried all the way to the mouth of the street. It was one of those happy family occasions when nobody cared what the neighbours might think, because long-suppressed grievances, whether real or imagined, had finally boiled to the surface, and everyone was eager to throw another log onto the fire.

And the fire blazed in every direction.

For some reason, the Troyak family had decided they needed fresh air, so their *affaire de famille*, as Lydia Carver would have called it, had transformed into an *affaire publique*, a public spectacle.

Naturally, the affluent neighbourhood, full of low villas hidden behind imposing walls, was not designed to let neighbours peer into each other's affairs. But the commotion had lasted long enough for the media to arrive, represented by three reporters of the particularly unsavoury breed that made its living photographing the least glorious moments of the famous.

"Paparazzi?" Xavier stopped in surprise. "What are they doing here?"

The mystery cleared up at once when the gate swung open and Svetlana Troyak stepped out onto the street, a small elegant backpack slung over her shoulders and a stormy expression on her face.

Unlike Gabrielle, who had fled her temporary refuge in distress and haste, her golden-haired counterpart retained all the majesty of an irritated goddess.

"Where do you think you're going?" barked a broad-shouldered man in his forties, striding after her. He was handsome enough, a natural blond like Svetlana herself, and might have looked charming if his face had not been red as a beet and, despite all modern expectations, twisted by pure rage.

The moment he grabbed her wrist, she shook him off as if he were an annoying dog.

"Hands off," she snapped, gesturing toward the assembled reporters. "Hands off, you old hypocrite! Think of your dazzling career."

"Hold it right there, young lady!" the man shot back. "Don't even try to suggest I've ever put my career ahead of my children."

"Nothing of the sort, dear Papa. I'm merely protecting your sterling reputation," Svetlana replied, and to Xavier's eye she sounded entirely sincere. "But touch me again and I'll have a lovely chat with those nice people about you and your recent hobbies."

"Hobbies? What hobbies? I've never done anything I'd be ashamed of."

"Oh yes, that embarrassing little matter with Gabrielle," Svetlana retorted. "Everyone has a mistress these days, so why bother with adoption, right? You threw her out because she ruined your plans."

"What nonsense are you spouting? I threw her out because she endangered my children's safety. She betrayed my trust."

"How many times do I have to tell you it was my idea to go there? She warned us you'd lose your temper."

"And how many times do I have to tell *you* that I don't care whose idea it was? If she'd shown even a shred of gratitude, she'd have come to me before the damage was done."

"Why should she have reported us to you? Believe whatever fairy tale helps you sleep at night. I'm sure you'll come up with some socially acceptable explanation. There are your reporters. Go justify yourself to them, Mister Innocent and Wronged."

Her voice turned cold.

"Thank you for everything. Goodbye."

With that, she spun on her heel and strode up the street toward Xavier and Medard.

The reporters seized the opportunity and shamelessly set their cameras clicking. Svetlana ignored them completely, but neither did she quicken her pace. She seemed entirely used to that sort of attention.

"Who the devil is that guy?" Xavier muttered.

Obviously, it was the elder Troyak, but he still could not understand what made him such a sensation.

"My Laura would be on cloud nine," Medard sighed. "She's one of his biggest fans. If she knew what was happening here, she'd drop everything and come running. Haven't you ever heard of Eugene Troyak and Camille  Bitewell? *Cats and Thorns?*"

"What? *Cats and Thorns?* Sounds like some kind of teenage fetish. Pink and fluffy."

"*Cats and Thorns* is a television series. The biggest hit of the year. It's everywhere. Laura's been obsessed with it for months. Do you think he'd sign a photograph for me?"

Medard must have loved Laura very much indeed if he believed Eugene Troyak was about to entertain fans under the circumstances.

Xavier refused to waste time on such futile speculation.

Instead, he instructed the little fairy to order another e-taxi, because he suspected his retreat might soon need to be both swift and decisive.

"Don't be ridiculous, sweetheart!" Troyak Senior shouted. His affectionate diminutive was clearly intended for the reporters' benefit and matched neither the fury in his voice nor the colour of his face. "You don't even have anywhere to go."

"That's none of your concern! Why don't you look after mother for a change? She needs you more than I do!" Svetlana yelled over her shoulder. "You spent years telling us society demands everyone take care of themselves."

"But you're my child!"

"Then cross me off the list. Write me off as another failed investment."

Xavier nudged Medard.

"There's our cue. Time to step onto the stage."

"Onto what?"

"The stage. Come on, puff out your chest and think of Laura. She'll be thrilled when you tell her you kidnapped the daughter of a famous actor."

"I'm not sure about that. Excitement like this isn't really my thing."

"Don't be absurd. We'll make the front page of every tabloid."

"An appealing prospect for some people, perhaps, but I..."

Xavier ignored him and waved his arms wildly.

"There we are! There we are!" he bellowed, startling a group of ornamental peacocks that had been pecking peacefully at grain and blocking the street from curious Plastics.

The entrance of new players surprised everyone, including Svetlana herself.

Better than anyone, Xavier understood that people under pressure would seize upon any escape route when cornered. Besides, he held an ace.

"Quickly. We've already taken care of Gabrielle," he called without moving closer.

*You have to come to us of your own accord,* he added silently.

The last thing he wanted was for Troyak Senior to legitimately accuse him of kidnapping.

The actor recovered faster than his daughter.

"And who exactly are you?" he called. "What are you doing here? My daughter doesn't know you!"

He might have accepted younger arrivals as friends of Svetlana's generation. But two unfamiliar men around forty alarmed him immediately.

"Speak for yourself," Svetlana shouted back, delighted by the opportunity to contradict him. "Is Gabrielle really all right?"

"Perfectly," Xavier assured her. "My fiancée is looking after her."

Like any skilful manipulator, he preferred truth to lies, but in this case he could not help himself. A man with a fiancée sounded far more trustworthy than a solitary stranger in whom suspicious Svetlana might suspect ulterior motives, exactly as she had during their first encounter.

"The e-taxi will be here any minute," he urged. "If you want out of here, here's your chance. Where's your brother?"

"That coward is comforting mother," Svetlana snorted as she marched toward them. "As if she weren't the one who started all this! Now she's crying, perfectly happy that the thorn in her side is gone, pretending she's heartbroken that Gabrielle finally told everyone to go to hell."

The reporters' cameras erupted once more, flashes chasing one another like heralds of a sensational headline:

*Popular Actor's Daughter Runs Into the Arms of an Older Man.*

While Medard vigorously tried to hide his face and awkwardly shepherd Svetlana and her backpack into the vehicle, Xavier turned toward the media pack and waved.

"Ladies and gentlemen. None of us are celebrities, and I expressly forbid you from filming us. If our photographs appear in public, every one of us will sue."

The reporters did not even bother to reply, too absorbed by the unfolding scene. Any damages their editors might have to pay were simply part of the business.

"They didn't even notice you," Medard complained. "Laura reads the tabloids every day. Nothing escapes her. Do they really have the right to do this?"

"They don't have the right, but threats won't stop them," Xavier explained. "So suck in your stomach and stand up straight. If Laura's going to see you in the papers, she might as well see you at your best. And don't worry. We'll squeeze a nice little fortune out of them for our business."

*Did you record everything?* he asked the little fairy mentally.

"Of course. Including footage from the street cameras," the little fairy replied with a mischievous salute. "I think they're in for a nasty surprise. The penalties for unauthorised recording of a citizen-candidate and a Plastics are among the highest. You'll get something out of it too, since they're harming your business."

During all the months of his insomnia troubles, Xavier had not accepted a single commission, so the prospect of extra income was most welcome.

Meanwhile, Eugene Troyak came jogging after them.

"Gentlemen," he said darkly. "You will immediately tell me who you are. Otherwise I won't allow you to take my daughter away."

"You don't have a single right to interrogate them," Svetlana snapped as she rolled down the car window. "Try remembering that I'm an adult. Whom I leave with and where I go is none of your business."

"Quiet, you little..." her father shot back. He evidently intended to use a much harsher word, but caught himself at the last second. "...ingrate. I'm not stopping you from doing anything. I only want to know you're safe."

But he received no answer.

"Give me your business card," Xavier said instead, unable to resist the temptation of obtaining contact details for a potential Player. "I'll call you so you won't have to worry."

"Forget him," Svetlana said coldly.

As a public figure protective of his privacy, Troyak Senior hesitated. Then, almost sheepishly, he slipped a card into Xavier's hand.

"Thank you, Mr...? Mr...?"

Xavier left the hint unanswered.

"You have my word that everything will be handled discreetly," he said instead in the tone of a merchant peddling forbidden pleasures, warmly shaking the hand the actor had forgotten to withdraw. "You can rely on there being no trouble."

This was exactly how gangsters in modern films parted ways after concluding a dirty deal.

Unfortunately, the implication sailed right over Troyak's head, which disappointed Xavier. He felt a professional actor ought to have caught the reference.

In any case, there was no reason to linger.

*Two new companions will have to do,* he reflected. *No one can have everything.*

As he climbed back into the vehicle, annoyed that they had failed to secure the last member of Svetlana's trio, a commotion erupted at the villa gate.

The gathered reporters turned in unison, hopeful that the drama had just acquired another act.
