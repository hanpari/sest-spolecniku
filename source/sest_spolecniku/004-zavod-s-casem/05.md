# Beggars Can't Be Choosers

"Do you remember those offensive slogans that keep appearing on the university walls?" Jacob asked. "The calls to execute Silvers and Golds?"

"Yes. What about them?"

"I'll sign a confession saying I am the one who wrote them," he offered nervously.

Gabrielle wasn't foolish enough to believe for even a moment that Jacob belonged to some student resistance movement. A document like that, whether true or not, would ruin him. Expulsion would follow, then an investigation, and with it any hope of a corporate career.

"Are you sure? That's a world even Silvers are afraid of."

Eugene Troyak's agitated speech still echoed in her mind, his description of a traumatic experience, of real pain. Unlike Svetlana, who saw the NT transmitter as little more than a tool of rebellion against authority, Gabrielle understood the risks connected with the Game.

*Nothing for the weak.*

"All the better," Jacob replied, almost enthusiastically. "The two of us will prove to everyone who the future belongs to."

*Or we'll fail at the first hurdle,* she thought. *If I had a choice, I wouldn't even consider Valehart's offer.*

Jacob mistook her hesitation.

"I know how to pursue what I want," he assured her. "Fine, today I'm a nobody. Tomorrow? Who knows! I don't plan on staying a nobody forever. As long as I remain at the university, you'll have me at your command. I'm useful."

*Translation: I'll become your servant if that's what you want.*

She suppressed the absurd urge to order him to dance like a puppet.

"You're offering promises," she pointed out.

"I've got nothing else," he admitted. "Please. Give me a chance."

"As you wish. But no guarantees. I'll keep your confession regardless of what happens to you."

"A drowning man will clutch at a straw," he agreed bitterly. He drafted the statement on his tablet, signed it, and forwarded it to her. Gabrielle checked it carefully for any loopholes he might later try to exploit.

"You're a fool," she concluded.

"But an ambitious fool." He grinned crookedly. "The worst kind. Now your turn."

"Very well. Listen."

Without concealing anything, she shared everything she knew, including her own observations and Eugene Troyak's warning. The longer Jacob listened, the more captivated he became.

"This is exactly what I needed," he breathed. "A ticket into the elite. Am I right that if I fail the first time, I can come back and try again?"

"Yes. The obstacle is subconscious. The less desperately you want it, the easier the NT transmitter can influence you."

"Why?"

"Counterintuitive, isn't it?" she remarked.

This was only her deduction, but she was sure she was right.

*It can't be a coincidence that entry into the Game happens during sleep.*

"Anyone who enters the **Game Center** assasment believing everything is on the line will fail."

"Then I'm doomed."

"Not necessarily," she corrected irritably. "There are drugs that suppress neural activity. I'll prepare them for you."

"Won't they make me sleepy?"

She studied him thoughtfully, intrigued by the problem.

*Of course,* she realized. *A doctor might notice the symptoms. But if I use a three-component compound with timed release, I can guide the candidate safely through every stage. One minute to suppress, fifteen to stimulate, then numbness afterward.*

"What about the torture devices?" she muttered. "The anesthetic can't impair your cognitive functions."

"Torture devices?" he repeated nervously.

"A neural pain-induction apparatus. Quite ingenious."

He stared at her as though she were a monster.

"Ingenious?" he shuddered. "I gathered the process was unpleasant."

"Oh, very much so," she assured him, then asked in surprise, "Does pain bother you?"

"Who isn't bothered by pain?"

"Has it never occurred to you that controlled suffering might reveal the nature of a test subject?"

"Are you insane? Who would willingly suffer?"

"Yes, that's the problem," she sighed. "Fortunately for us, you don't have a choice. Personal preferences needn't concern us."

*What's wrong with him?* she wondered when she noticed him edging away in horror.

"I find our discussion inspiring," she reassured him. "Interesting horizons are opening before us, Jacob. If we develop a compound that helps aspiring players enter the Game, we'll both profit."

"*We* will? You and me? People say you're a genius. I'm afraid to ask what exactly I'll be doing."

"You?" She smiled faintly. "I greatly appreciate your social skills. I'm sure you'll handle the business side. And then there's the development process itself, where you'll be indispensable."

Jacob translated *indispensable* as *guinea pig*.

"Wishful thinking! You're planning to experiment on me!"

"For our mutual benefit," she replied gravely. "I should thank you. I've had a dreadful day, full of unnecessary emotions. But your little problem stimulates me. You may leave now. I need to think through our next steps. Don't people usually say we'll keep in touch on occasions like this?"

Jacob looked distinctly reluctant to board the lifeboat she'd offered.

"What if I refuse?" he asked.

"If you refuse to be useful," Gabrielle explained, "I won't need the confession you signed. I'll simply hand it over to the university administration."

The moment his distant promises became immediate and deeply unpleasant obligations, Jacob visibly recoiled.

"I don't want..."

"Why would you want to refuse?" Gabrielle continued, unable to understand his concerns. "Are you afraid I'll leave you out or cheat you? I'd have thought you'd be delighted."

"Overjoyed," he assured her weakly.

"In that case, goodbye."

Mildly annoyed, Gabrielle turned on her heel and headed for the institute's glass doors.

*Strange attitude,* she thought, disappointed. *Jacob isn't nearly as ambitious as he claims. I'd expected more enthusiasm.*

The restlessness she'd carried since leaving the Troyaks gradually gave way to focused fascination.

*How can I stimulate the subject enough to replace induced pain?* she wondered. *I need a long-term effect on the nervous system. Preferably without permanent consequences.*

She climbed the stairs to the first floor and stopped before a door bearing the inscription:

**Lydia Carver, Ph.D.**  
**Director**

That sight plunged Gabrielle straight back into the icy waters where she herself was fighting to survive.

"Come in," a voice called almost immediately after she knocked.

She obeyed and entered, remaining modestly beside the padded door.

A red-haired woman sat behind a desk, dressed in a black suit, a glass of water beside her. Her fox-green eyes never looked up, remaining fixed on a red-bound book titled *Toxicology, Volume III*. A notebook filled with handwritten remarks lay beneath it.

*Toxicology?* Gabrielle wondered. *Doesn't this institute specialize in materials engineering?*

The book was open to a section describing snake venoms.

**Toxic Disorders of Neuromuscular Transmission**  
*The effects of snake neurotoxins*  
*Presynaptic β-neurotoxins*  
*(β-bungarotoxin, notexin, taipoxin) inhibit ACh release*

"I'll be with you in a moment," Lydia remarked while copying down several paragraphs. "Make yourself comfortable. You're Miss Marrow, aren't you?"

"Yes, Director."

"Please, sit down," the woman repeated impatiently. "And call me Lydia. As I've been informed, we're about to become business partners. Partners," she added with the faintest smile, "are on a first-name basis."

"Yes, Director."

"This is going to be difficult," Lydia sighed.

She rose and approached the rigidly standing Gabrielle, studying her for several seconds.

"I think I taught you once. Gifted," she recited to herself. "Pretty face, orphan, no support network, socially inactive. Have I missed anything?"

"Homeless," Gabrielle added, somewhat offended by the dry description.

"Ah yes, homeless." Lydia nodded and asked bluntly, "Why did you refuse to become a concubine? And the truth, please. You may skip the adoption story."

"I didn't want to."

"You simply didn't want to? That's all? No moral, physiological, or other reasons? You know, my father never married. My mother is a concubine as well."

"I'm not your mother," Gabrielle shot back. "I have different goals. My apologies," she added quickly, realizing she'd spoken too sharply.

"Not at all. If you'd answered differently, I couldn't have liked you."

*Liked me?*

Hearing something like that from a Silver was surprising, to say the least. Conventional wisdom held that Silvers and Golds were people whose emotions had been surgically removed.

As though reading her thoughts, Lydia remarked with amusement, "I've always suspected the philosophy of society wasn't for me. Recent events have convinced me to abandon the doctrine of enlightened selfishness. I'll take care of you and hope to gain a loyal friend in return."

"I understand."

"I doubt it."

She gently but firmly pushed Gabrielle into a leather armchair and offered her tea, which the girl didn't dare decline.

A kettle hissed in the small kitchenette while dishes clinked.

Several minutes after Lydia called out, "Green, black, or herbal?" a loud crash rang through the room, accompanied by shattering glass and a cry of pain.

Gabrielle leapt to her feet.

She found Lydia leaning against the wall, visibly shaken. Tea was pooled around her feet, and the skin across one foot had reddened from scalding. Strangely, however, Lydia seemed utterly unconcerned with her injury, staring blankly toward the refrigerator.

Only when Gabrielle rushed over and began treating the burn did Lydia finally regain her composure.

"Thank you."

"Did something happen, Director?"

"Congratulate me. I just got engaged," Lydia whispered, sounding terrified.

"Engaged?"

*I was under the impression that engagements required two people,* Gabrielle thought as she looked around the empty kitchenette.

*Don't tell me she's implying that the two of us...*

"The message was delivered by Shadowstep," Lydia continued, gesturing to her right as though introducing someone. "He took it rather hard. Who would've guessed he'd get jealous?"

*Has she lost her mind?* Gabrielle wondered. *Engaged? She's acting as though someone died. Who is Shadowstep? A dog? A stuffed toy? Has she become detached from reality?*

"I'm sorry," she said aloud. "For your loss."

"It's more than a loss," Lydia replied distantly. "All things considered, I've gained far more than I ever hoped."

"An inheritance, then?" Gabrielle suggested.

*That would explain everything. Loss and gain often come together when someone dies.*

"Yes. An inheritance."

Lydia nodded.

"You figured that out remarkably quickly. No emotional involvement," she added bitterly. "Nothing of the sort. Tell me, what's your opinion of Xavier Valehart?"

"I'd rather avoid him," Gabrielle answered honestly.

*Why are we talking about Valehart?*

"Avoid him? Then we're alike. But beggars can't be choosers."

"You got engaged to Valehart?" Gabrielle blurted out. "Why?"

"Because I didn't have a choice. He finds me repulsive. I know that. That's why his proposal took me by surprise. You must understand, I had to answer quickly before he changed his mind."

*Shouldn't it be the other way around?* Gabrielle thought.

Comparing the beautiful, confident woman before her with the image of the shabby, weathered Xavier Valehart, the only role she'd ever imagined for him was that of a rejected suitor.

"Weren't you a bit hasty?"

"Shadowstep seems to think so too," Lydia admitted. "Even Xavier's little fairy disagrees."

"Little fairy? Shadowstep?"

*Is she still sane? If someone forced me to become engaged to Valehart, would I go mad too? The man terrifies people!*

"Yes, our virtual assistants," Lydia explained when she noticed Gabrielle's bewildered glances. "You can't see them because you aren't wearing a smart headband. Shadowstep is sulking over there by the kettle."

"He sent an avatar to propose?"

Gabrielle had never attached much importance to romance, but the idea of such a moment unfolding so impersonally and coldly made her head spin.

"That bastard," she said.

For a moment she forgot the gulf between them and awkwardly wrapped Lydia in a hug. The redhead pulled away as if she disliked being touched.

"Alas, the warmth of a human body I cannot accept," Lydia sighed. "Don't stress it, dear."

Then Lydia suddenly stiffened.

"No!" she cried. "He can't possibly mean this seriously! The madman!"

When Gabrielle looked up, she saw tears running from Lydia's fox-green eyes.

"My dear fiancé is moving rather quickly," Lydia explained.

*Moving quickly? He proposed barely fifteen minutes ago!*

"Oh no." Gabrielle stared in disbelief. "He can't already want a wedding!?"

Lydia burst into laughter tinged with hysteria.

"Marriage wouldn't be so bad," she muttered. "Weddings don't hurt anyone. It's worse than you're imagining."

"How?"

"My dear fiancé wants me to authorize an attack on my stepsister. He wants her eliminated so my family understands they have to reckon with me."

"You can't!"

Gabrielle had no idea what such an attack meant in the corporate world, whether it involved a hired killer or merely a brutal beating, but she couldn't believe Lydia would agree.

"On the contrary," Lydia replied wearily. "The only thing I can't do is refuse. I've resisted for years... Shadowstep," she said firmly, "you have my authorization. Proceed according to their instructions. I, Lydia Carver, approve all actions necessary to remove my sister, Darina Carver, from the line of succession."
