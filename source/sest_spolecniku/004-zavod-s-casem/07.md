# I Was Clumsy

Low autumn sunlight shone through the windows as Gabrielle Marrow set down her phone and stared at the bare wall of the room. She was alone in the bleak minimalist apartment while her companions had gone out to gather the necessities Lydia Carver needed to stay alive.

The phonecall with **Svetlana Troyak** had been long, full of subtle hints and giggling. It seemed the Troyak siblings had found a safe harbor where, as Svetlana put it, they could survive.

*Survive? But nothing more,* Gabrielle thought gloomily.

She herself was trapped in unfamiliar surroundings among people who were almost frightening and certainly offered no sympathetic understanding.

If she had ever considered living with Svetlana, that walking cauldron of emotional outbursts, difficult, then the opposite extreme suited her no better.

When she heard the two Silvers return, she headed for the kitchen, where dinner had already been prepared. It was some exotic delicacy: finely chopped meat marinated in a sweet-and-sour sauce with a creamy aroma, served alongside sharp yellow rice. Finally, dessert appeared, and Xavier Valehart eyed it so suspiciously that Lydia almost took offense.

"Don't you like sweets?" she asked.

"Why did you order a cake with nuts and whipped cream?"

"At the request of our young partner here." Lydia pointed at Gabrielle.

"Ah."

Xavier grunted, clenched his teeth, and drove a spoon into the chocolate.

"So many pleasant memories," he hissed hostilely, "packed into a single sweet delight."

"I'll never buy it again," Lydia remarked.

"Nonsense. It's excellent."

As if being force-fed, Xavier shoveled down bite after bite, swallowing without chewing.

"You really must like it," Lydia observed sourly.

"Don't mind my sudden fit of nostalgia," he replied dryly. "Let's change the subject! What about our indecisive messenger?" Xavier turned to Gabrielle. "Did you get anything out of him or her?"

"A little."

Gabrielle had no trouble reciting the note from memory.

"Data disks?" Lydia asked. "Do we need to charge them? What exactly are we talking about?"

"This."

Xavier opened a package bearing the HC logo and pulled out six transparent plastic prisms.

"What are they?"

"Another mystery," Xavier said wearily. "They really are trying their hardest not to give us the slightest clue."

He then delivered a lecture on the conspiracy theories he'd developed, consuming half an hour with phrases like *perhaps*, *probably*, and *if we accept that...*

"In any case," he concluded, "here you are. Whoever figures out how to charge a data disk wins."

"But how?"

"No idea. And neither does that little nitwit."

At Gabrielle's questioning and slightly offended look, Lydia replied: "He doesn't mean you, darling. He and his little fairy have a lovely, balanced relationship. He thinks she's a fool, and she thinks likewise."

Both women laughed, but Gabrielle was already analyzing the word Lydia had used.

*Darling? Coming from her, it sounds inappropriate, without a trace of genuine warmth. Is she forcing herself to be kind? Why?*

"I'd suggest," she said timidly, "placing the data disk near an NT transmitter. I don't see any connectors, so it must use wireless transfer."

"As far as I'm concerned, I'll stick the thing under my pillow, think about it very hard, and hope for a miracle," Xavier smirked. He made no visible effort at kindness. "Anyway, ladies. Time for evening preparations and sleep. I'll take the couch, leaving the bed to you. Any objections?"

Gabrielle shrugged.

*Better than sleeping on the floor,* she thought, recalling the orphanages and boarding houses she'd passed through before the Troyaks had taken her in.

"Why not?" Lydia said uncertainly. "I've never shared a bed before."

"I have," Xavier replied. "And I have no intention of repeating the experience... It's no big deal," he added reassuringly. "Once we're in the Game, we're gone for twelve hours. No discomfort."

"I'm not that sensitive," Lydia shot back.

The bed was wide and comfortable, but she lay down on the far right edge, as far from Gabrielle as possible. Embarrassed, Gabrielle adopted a similar position.

She found herself thinking about Svetlana again and her excited voice.

*For her, this is a little adventure, a rebellion against her parents. For me, it's an existential crisis. Unlike her, I have nowhere to return to once I get tired of pretending to be independent.*

She began sobbing quietly, overwhelmed by the world's unfairness. Before long, her pillow was wet.

*I'm alone,* she thought. *I've always been alone.*

She sank into silent emptiness.

Then a gentle hand touched her.

Lydia pulled her toward the middle of the bed, wrapped her arms around her, and whispered:

"No one ever comforted me, so I don't know what to do. But that doesn't mean I can't try. I'll hold you until you fall asleep, if you'd like."

"Yes, please."

Gratefully, Gabrielle curled into the offered embrace, though she could feel Lydia's muscles tensing as she fought the revulsion physical contact seemed to provoke in her.

*It's like pressing against a rock,* Gabrielle thought as she drifted off. *A solid, unwilling rock. A stone pretending to care.*

---

"Young lady! Young lady!"

*Where am I?*

She awoke beneath painted heavens filled with frolicking animals, both familiar and unfamiliar. Sabertooth tigers lurked in thickets opposite fanged deer. Nearby stood extinct mammoths, unknown green-brown reptiles, birds ranging from hummingbirds to condors, and mythical creatures such as phoenixes, sea monsters, and both European and Asian dragons.

The fabric blazed with bright cheerful colors yet possessed a striking realism, as if reminding the observer that fairy tales could be real.

*Very nice,* thought Gabrielle, who had never cared much about art. *Am I in the Game? Am I in danger?*

As she rose, she bumped into a chair, sending it crashing to the floor.

*Careful!*

Right on cue, the door to the spacious bedroom opened.

A young maid entered, dressed in black-and-white clothes and a fluffy bonnet. She could not have been more than seventeen, with a timid expression and doe-like brown eyes.

"Has young miss awakened?" she asked gently. "Would young miss like to be dressed?"

*Is she retarded?* Gabrielle thought.

From a habit of speaking in the third person to a shy, fearful manner, which even Plastics did not display, the maid irritated her.

*No, that's not me,* she suddenly realized. *That's my second personality. My local alter ego.*

Fascinated, she examined her two selves, one her own and one foreign. The first retreated into the background while the latter impatiently drummed her fingers against the bedpost.

"Dressed?" she asked, masking her irritation. "Why?"

"Madam ordered both young ladies and the young master to prepare for the journey. I've brought traveling clothes."

"If Mother wishes it so," she said indifferently. "Dress me."

Exactly as Lydia had warned her, the other personality controlled her body, but only in brief intervals. Once she assumed the desired pose, the alter ego withdrew and left Gabrielle to cope on her own.

It felt strange to be taken care of by someone else.

*I'd be finished three times faster if I did it myself,* she thought.

Still, she didn't protest. The process gave her time to think and observe.

Like Medard Mosswell, she appeared to have landed in a wealthy family where she faced no immediate danger. Though the room looked medieval, it was no less modern than what she was used to. She spotted a decorative washbasin with running water, a writing desk supplied with carefully sharpened pencils, books with colorful covers, and above all warm walls that bore little resemblance to the cold stone walls of real castles.

*It's only an extravagantly decorated replica,* she thought thankfully.

Gabrielle liked her comforts and had never understood people's fascination with antiques.

"What is this?" she asked after the maid finished dressing her and handed her a leather object reinforced with silvery metal.

"Your riding crop," the maid replied cautiously, with fear in her eyes.

"I see. Thank you."

Gabrielle had no particular interest in horses or equestrian equipment.

But the moment she grasped it, she felt a fire ignite beneath her ribs, an additional organ that Xavier had emphasized repeatedly during his training sessions.

The flame stretched down her arm and connected with the crop, producing a faint tingling in her fingers.

Before she realized what she was doing, she flicked the crop across the maid's hands.

The girl jerked back but did not dare cry out.

*Stop that!*

Gabrielle directed the thought at her other self.

Past the shock, however, scientific curiosity took over. Though the strike had been light, almost a caress, the crop acted as a conductor for the inner flame. The energy reached the girl's nerves and irritated them, creating a burst of brief but intense pain.

"I'm sorry," Gabrielle said when she saw tears in the maid's eyes.

The maid clearly hadn't expected an apology.

Yet it pleased her.

"It's quite all right, young miss. I was clumsy."

*She must be thoroughly trained,* Gabrielle thought. *She gets flogged and then thanks me for it. My other self is quite a monster!*

To avoid further experiments on the maid, she pressed the crop against her own palm and tested the discharge on herself. Before long she forgot the world around her and immersed herself in systematic experimentation. The inner flame and everything connected to it fascinated her.

"Ehm, young miss should go," the maid coughed, astonished that Gabrielle was willingly torturing herself. "Her mother is already waiting."

"Of course. Thank you."

As she soon discovered, her room occupied a castle tower. The girls had to descend a spiral staircase of stone steps.

Portraits of men and women lined the walls. Beneath each hung a brief biography accompanied by dates.

**Baroness Isabella Sorrowful**, widow, appointed steward of the castle at the age of twenty-six. Killed by bandits three years later. Children: Jonah and Joachim.

**Sir John Quickhand**, appointed steward of the castle at the age of thirty-two. Missing after a hunting visit to the Forest of Cute Owlets. No children.

**Baron Medard Mosswell** and his wife **Sylvia**, current stewards of the castle. Children: **Grigori**, **Svetlana**, and **Gabrielle**.

Unlike the other plaques embedded permanently into the wall, the description beneath the current rulers looked temporary, as though whoever had written it saw little point in creating anything lasting.

Considering how rapidly the castle's rulers seemed to disappear and be replaced, perhaps the inscriptions would need changing again in a few months.

The record-holder remained **Count Ernest Stern**, who lasted thirty years before resigning in favor of his daughter **Agnes**. She managed ten years before being killed by an anonymous lover for reasons the plaque did not specify.

As time passed, the stewards' terms grew shorter while their noble titles became steadily less impressive.

From that, Gabrielle concluded that the local lords and ladies were dying before they could climb any higher on the social ladder.
